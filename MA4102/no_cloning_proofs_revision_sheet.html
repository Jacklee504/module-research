How to answer

  1. Assume one fixed unitary clones every state.
  2. Apply it to two nonorthogonal states with the same blank register.
  3. Take the inner product on both sides and invoke unitarity.
  4. Name a concrete pair, e.g. |0⟩ and |+⟩, for the contradiction.

Practice question -- no universal copier (12 marks)

Inspired by: 2023/24 Question 3(ii) and 2025/26 Question 4(b).

(a) Prove that no unitary U can clone every qubit. [9 marks]

Demand / quickest route: inner-product proof. Answer: suppose U(|ψ⟩|0⟩)=|ψ⟩|ψ⟩ and similarly for |φ⟩. Unitarity preserves the input inner product, so ⟨φ|ψ⟩=⟨φ|ψ⟩2. Choose |φ⟩=|0⟩ and |ψ⟩=|+⟩, whose overlap 1/√2 is neither zero nor one: contradiction. Trap: compare output vectors without using unitarity.

(b) Why does this not prohibit copying |0⟩ or |1⟩ when promised? [3 marks]

Answer: an operation may copy a fixed known orthonormal set; the proof excludes one U that clones every unknown state, including nonorthogonal pairs. Trap: claim all copying is impossible.
