MA4102 Topic Guide

Gates and Circuits

Read a circuit in time order and preserve qubit ordering in every tensor product.

Purpose

Controlled gates, Hadamards and circuit outputs are stable calculation targets.Exam use: Basis/qubit order, control-target roles, intermediate states, normalisation and final measurement interpretation are labelled.

Question plan

  1. Declare the basis order, normally |00⟩,|01⟩,|10⟩,|11⟩.
  2. Apply gates from right to left in an algebraic product, or left to right in a drawn circuit.
  3. Use tensor products for single-qubit gates on a register.
  4. Measure only after the final state is obtained.
  5. Question interpretation: Read circuit time left-to-right but multiply matrices in operator order; declare whether |q0 q1> or another qubit ordering indexes the basis.

Notation and derivation

H|0⟩=(|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2, H|1⟩=(|0⟩−|1⟩)/√2. CNOT maps |c,t⟩ to |c,t⊕c⟩; it is linear, so apply it term-by-term to a superposition.Rapid recall: H|0>=|+>, H|1>=|->; CNOT maps |a,b> to |a,a xor b>; tensor products act on ordered subsystems and unitary gates preserve norm.Exam workflow: Expand the state in the computational basis, apply each gate linearly, collect amplitudes, and use norm or reversibility as a check.

Worked calculation

Start |00⟩. Apply H to qubit one: (|00⟩+|10⟩)/√2. CNOT (first control) gives (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, a Bell state.

Practice 1 — track a Bell-generating circuit and a phase gate [9 marks]

Prompt. Starting at |00>, apply H on qubit one, controlled-NOT, then Z on qubit two; state the final state and Z-basis probabilities. Question-demand diagnosis. Credit is for intermediate states in the declared qubit order. Fastest route. Expand after H, apply CNOT term-by-term, then apply Z only to terms with target bit 1. Topic-specific trap. The relative minus is invisible in this measurement but cannot be discarded before later gates.

Start |00>. Apply H on the first qubit: (|00>+|10>)/sqrt2. Apply CNOT with first control: (|00>+|11>)/sqrt2. Apply Z on the second: (|00>-|11>)/sqrt2. Z-basis outcomes 00 and 11 each have probability 1/2; the relative minus is invisible in this measurement but changes later interference.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Find XH|0⟩. Check: X|+⟩=|+⟩.

2. Compute the output of CNOT(H⊗I)|01⟩ in the stated ordering. Check: distribute CNOT across both terms.

Practice 2 — undo an entangling circuit [8 marks]

Prompt. Apply CNOT followed by H on the first qubit to (|00>+|11>)/√2 and identify the recovered basis state. Question-demand diagnosis. This is an inverse-circuit calculation, so operation order is decisive. Fastest route. Write the state after CNOT before applying H linearly. Topic-specific trap. A drawn circuit runs left-to-right while the corresponding matrix product is read right-to-left.

Paper-style synthesis: apply the inverse circuit CNOT then H on qubit 1 to (|00>+|11>)/sqrt2. Check CNOT gives (|00>+|10>)/sqrt2=|+0>, then H gives |00>.

Mistakes

  • Reversing matrix/circuit order.
  • Changing control and target.
  • Using a different basis order halfway through.
  • Recovery rule: Gate diagrams and matrix products use opposite-looking orders; write the state after each gate to avoid reversing time.

Checklist

  • I can write H, X, Z and CNOT actions.
  • I annotate control/target and qubit order.
  • I can convert the output to probabilities.
  • Final 60-second audit: Basis/qubit order, control-target roles, intermediate states, normalisation and final measurement interpretation are labelled.
