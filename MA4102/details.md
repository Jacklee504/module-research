# MA4102 - Algebraic Foundations of Quantum Computing

## High-Level View

- Predictability: High
- Priority: Good option if comfortable with linear algebra, complex numbers, proof-style answers, and abstract notation.
- Assessment: 70% written assessment, 30% continuous assessment.
- Semester: Semester 1.
- Current module page validity: Valid from 2025 onwards.
- Current teachers/administrators: Mary Kelly, Michael Mc Gettrick.
- Lecturer/staff-change risk: Medium-high. Michael Mc Gettrick is the starred lecturer on several recent papers and is listed on the current page, but Mark Howard is the starred lecturer on 2022-2023 and 2025-2026 and is not listed on the current page. The exam style still remains very repetitive despite this.

## Recommendation

This is a predictable module, but it is not an easy marks module unless the underlying linear algebra is comfortable. The exam repeatedly tests the same core operations with changed states, matrices, channels, and algorithms. It is a strong choice for someone who likes mathematical calculation and can drill templates, but weaker if the goal is to maximise CA because the CA is only 30%.

Compared with CS402 and CS4423, this is similarly mathematical but more quantum-linear-algebra focused. Compared with CT421, it has some algorithm overlap through Grover/search, but the style is much more formal and calculation-heavy.

## Repeated Question Patterns

- The structure is highly repetitive: exact numbers/states change, but task types recur.
- State calculations are one of the safest areas: measurement probabilities, density matrices, applying unitaries, Bloch sphere angles and `|+>`, `|->` measurements.
- Entanglement and mixed-state calculations recur: reduced density matrices, purity, Von Neumann entropy, entanglement checks and unitary preservation of purity.
- Channels/measurements are active but less predictable in wording: Kraus operators, trace preservation, POVM probabilities and CPTP definitions.
- Circuits and gates recur: CNOT, controlled-Hadamard, Hadamard, SWAP, Pauli gates, controlled rotations, truth tables and output states.
- Grover and QFT are strong targets: Grover iterations/success probabilities, `|a>`, `|phi>` basis work and QFT of small qudit states.
- Recurring proofs: no-cloning, no-deleting/no-information deletion, Bell-state entanglement, projective measurement validity and unitaries preserving purity.

## Predictability Judgment

High predictability.

Reasons:

- The exam format is stable across all five papers.
- The official learning outcomes map very directly onto the repeated exam blocks.
- Qutrit/qubit calculations, density matrices, entanglement, Grover, QFT, circuits, no-cloning, and Kraus/POVM material recur heavily.
- Even when internal examiners change, the paper remains close to the established template.

Risks:

- The module page and latest paper do not fully align on the starred lecturer: the latest paper starred Mark Howard, while the current page lists Michael Mc Gettrick.
- The material is notation-heavy; small algebra mistakes can cost marks.
- CA is only 30%, so it does not strongly support the goal of maximising marks through coursework.

## Study Strategy

Highest-return preparation:

1. Master density matrices, measurement probabilities, and unitary updates.
2. Drill reduced density matrices, purity, and Von Neumann entropy.
3. Memorise and practise the no-cloning proof.
4. Practise Grover iterations/probabilities and QFT on small vectors.
5. Practise Kraus/POVM calculation templates.
6. Practise drawing circuits from gate expressions and computing simple circuit outputs.

If taking this module, past-paper drilling should be very effective. The best approach is to build worked templates for each recurring question type and then practise with changed numbers.

## Short Learning Material

Use Degreed/Percipio-style catalogues for short tutorials, guided projects, or video chapters. Prefer material under 3 hours per topic.

- Quantum computing basics: qubits, measurement, superposition and simple circuits.
- Linear algebra refresher: complex vectors, inner products, tensor products and unitary matrices.
- Quantum gates and circuits: Hadamard, Pauli gates, CNOT, SWAP and controlled operations.
- Density matrices and mixed states: trace, purity, reduced density matrices and entropy.
- Quantum algorithms overview: Deutsch-Jozsa, Grover and QFT at small scale.
- Quantum channels and measurements: Kraus operators, POVMs and CPTP-map intuition.
- Qiskit intro/tutorial: simulate small circuits and inspect statevectors.
