Entanglement and Reduced States

Test factorisation cleanly, then confirm with a reduced state when needed.

What repeatedly appears in the papers

Proving whether two-qubit vectors are entangled appears in 2022/23 Question 4(a), 2025/26 Question 2(d), and entropy-based detection in 2021/22 and 2024/25 Question 2. The papers favour a two-case, 8-mark proof.

How to answer

  1. Write the four coefficients in the stated computational order.
  2. Use ad=bc for the fastest two-qubit pure-state decision.
  3. If asked to prove, show the failed equality or the mixed reduced state.
  4. Do not confuse superposition with entanglement.

Practice question -- certify two states (16 marks)

Inspired by: 2025/26 Question 2(d) and 2022/23 Question 4(a).

(a) Decide whether (|00⟩-|01⟩-|10⟩+|11⟩)/2 is entangled. [8 marks]

Demand / quickest route: apply ad=bc. Answer: coefficients are (1,-1,-1,1)/2, so ad=1/4=bc. It factors as (|0⟩-|1⟩)⊗(|0⟩-|1⟩)/2 and is separable. Trap: call it entangled merely because all four basis states occur.

(b) Decide whether (|00⟩+|01⟩+|10⟩)/√3 is entangled. [8 marks]

Demand / quickest route: compare ad and bc. Answer: ad=0 while bc=1/3, so no product factorisation exists; the state is entangled. This direct contradiction is a complete proof. Trap: attempt an unmotivated partial trace and lose the basis factors.
