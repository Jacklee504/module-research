MA4102 Topic Guide

Projective Measurements and POVMs

Validate effects first, then use trace formulas for probabilities and updates.

Purpose

Projective-measurement validity, changed bases and POVM probabilities repeatedly appear.Exam use: Validity, completeness, trace probabilities, sum-to-one audit and the justified update rule are all shown.

Question plan

  1. For projectors, check Pj 2=Pj, Pj †=Pj, and ∑Pj=I.
  2. For POVM effects, check Ej≥0 and ∑Ej=I.
  3. Use p(j)=Tr(Pjρ) or Tr(Ejρ).
  4. For a projective outcome, update to PjρPj/p(j).
  5. Question interpretation: For valid measurement, check positivity, orthogonality/idempotence where projective, and completeness; for outcomes, calculate traces and distinguish probability from post-state.

Notation and derivation

The trace rule is the density-matrix form of Born's rule. Completeness ensures ∑jp(j)=Tr((∑Pj)ρ)=1.Rapid recall: Projectors obey P_j^2=P_j=P_j^dagger and P_jP_k=0; POVM effects obey E_j>=0 and sum E_j=I; an instrument/Kraus choice, not effects alone, determines post-measurement state.Exam workflow: Validate operators before using Born's rule, calculate every outcome probability, check they sum to one, and normalise the conditional state.

Worked calculation

Measure |+⟩ in the Z basis: P0=|0⟩⟨0| and P1=|1⟩⟨1|. Both probabilities are Tr(Pj|+⟩⟨+|)=½; the outcome state is |j⟩.

Practice 1 — projective X-basis measurement of a mixed qubit [9 marks]

Prompt. For the supplied ρ and X-basis projectors, verify the probabilities, their sum, and the conditional states. Question-demand diagnosis. Separate the Born probabilities from the state-update conclusion. Fastest route. Evaluate Tr(P±ρ) before using the projective update rule. Topic-specific trap. In a non-computational basis, off-diagonal entries can contribute to an outcome probability.

Let rho=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,1/4]] and measure in the X basis with P_+=1/2[[1,1],[1,1]], P_-=1/2[[1,-1],[-1,1]]. Then p(+)=Tr(P_+rho)=3/4 and p(-)=1/4. Conditional projective states are |+><+| and |-><-|. The probabilities sum to 1 and differ from the Z-basis diagonal probabilities 3/4,1/4 for a different reason: coherence contributes in the X basis.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Check whether {½I,½I} is a POVM. Check: both effects are positive and sum to I.

2. For a supplied ρ and POVM, calculate each outcome probability and verify they total one. Check: preserve complex conjugation in matrix products.

Practice 2 — validate and use a two-effect POVM [8 marks]

Prompt. Verify that E0=diag(1,1/2), E1=diag(0,1/2) is a POVM and find both probabilities on |+>. Question-demand diagnosis. Validity requires positivity and completeness before the trace calculation. Fastest route. Check diagonal eigenvalues and E0+E1, then evaluate traces. Topic-specific trap. Effects specify probabilities but not, by themselves, a post-measurement instrument.

Paper-style synthesis: E0=diag(1,1/2), E1=diag(0,1/2). Verify a POVM and find probabilities on |+>. Check both are positive, E0+E1=I, and probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4. Effects alone do not specify the conditional states.

Mistakes

  • Assuming every POVM effect is a projector.
  • Using amplitudes directly with a mixed state.
  • Skipping the completeness check.
  • Recovery rule: Do not use rho_j=E_j rho E_j/p_j for a general POVM; a compatible measurement operator M_j with E_j=M_j^dagger M_j is needed.

Checklist

  • I distinguish projector, effect and Kraus operator.
  • I can use the trace probability rule.
  • I state post-measurement state where required.
  • Final 60-second audit: Validity, completeness, trace probabilities, sum-to-one audit and the justified update rule are all shown.
