MA4102 Topic Guide

Qubits, Qudits and State Vectors

Normalise a complex state, change basis carefully, then square amplitudes for probabilities.

Purpose

These are the foundation calculations behind repeated qutrit and qubit questions.Exam use: Dimension, norm, conjugated inner products, modulus-squared probabilities and global/relative phase distinction are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Write the state in the stated orthonormal basis.
  2. Check ⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1; normalise if needed.
  3. For outcome j use p(j)=|⟨j|ψ⟩|2.
  4. State the post-measurement basis vector when asked.
  5. Question interpretation: For state validity, normalise complex amplitudes; for basis change, compute inner products with basis vectors; for measurement, square moduli and sum degenerate outcomes.

Notation and derivation

|ψ⟩=∑j=0 d−1αj|j⟩, with ∑|αj|2=1. The Born rule gives p(j)=|αj|2; the probabilities sum to one exactly because the vector is normalised.Rapid recall: A d-level pure state is a unit vector up to global phase; probabilities are |amplitude|^2; relative phase affects interference, and tensor-product dimension multiplies.Exam workflow: Calculate the norm first, factor out any global phase only after identifying it, and preserve complex conjugation in inner products.

Worked calculation

For |ψ⟩=(|0⟩+i|1⟩)/√2, p(0)=1/2 and p(1)=|i/√2|2=1/2. In the computational basis the column is (1,i)T/√2.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Normalise |psi>=(1+i)|0>+2|1>. Norm squared is |1+i|^2+4=2+4=6, so |psi>=((1+i)|0>+2|1>)/sqrt6. In the X basis, <+|psi>=(3+i)/sqrt12 with probability 10/12=5/6; <-|psi>=(-1+i)/sqrt12 with probability 2/12=1/6. The probabilities sum to 1.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Normalise |v⟩=2|0⟩−i|1⟩. Check: divide by √5.

2. A qutrit has coefficients (1,1,i)/√3. Give all computational-basis probabilities and the state after outcome 2. Check: each probability is 1/3; outcome 2 leaves |2⟩.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: for qutrit (|0>+omega|1>+|2>)/sqrt3 with |omega|=1, give computational probabilities and discuss omega. Check each outcome is 1/3; omega is a relative phase that can affect another basis measurement.

Mistakes

  • Squaring a complex amplitude instead of its modulus.
  • Forgetting that a qutrit has three basis outcomes.
  • Mixing a global phase with a relative phase.
  • Recovery rule: Square the modulus of a complex amplitude, not the complex number itself.

Checklist

  • I can normalise complex vectors.
  • I can apply the Born rule and state collapse.
  • I label the basis used.
  • Final 60-second audit: Dimension, norm, conjugated inner products, modulus-squared probabilities and global/relative phase distinction are explicit.
