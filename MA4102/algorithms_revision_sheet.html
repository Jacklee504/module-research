MA4102 Topic Guide

Algorithms: Grover, QFT and Deutsch–Jozsa

For exam calculations, show the small-state transformation at every named algorithm step.

Purpose

Grover and QFT are strong recurring targets; Deutsch–Jozsa also appears in the archive.Exam use: Basis order, normalisation, every stage vector, oracle convention and final probabilities are explicit.

Question plan

  1. State the input basis and dimension N.
  2. For Grover: prepare uniform state, apply oracle, then diffusion.
  3. For QFT: use FN|x⟩=N−½∑yωxy|y⟩, ω=e2πi/N.
  4. Write exact amplitudes first; only then give probabilities.
  5. Question interpretation: Name the algorithmic stage, write the complete small state before and after it, and turn amplitudes into probabilities only at the measurement step.

Notation and derivation

Grover's diffusion D=2|s⟩⟨s|−I reflects amplitudes about their mean. QFT is unitary because its phase columns are orthogonal roots-of-unity sums.Rapid recall: Grover uses phase-oracle plus diffusion and about pi sqrt(N)/4 iterations; QFT maps |x> to N^{-1/2} sum_y exp(2 pi i xy/N)|y>; Deutsch-Jozsa distinguishes constant from balanced with one oracle query.Exam workflow: Fix basis order, apply one gate/operator at a time, retain signs and phases, then state the measurement distribution and success claim.

Worked calculation

For N=4 with marked |00⟩, |s⟩ has amplitudes ½. Oracle changes them to (−½,½,½,½). Their mean is ¼; diffusion maps each a to 2(¼)−a, giving (1,0,0,0): success probability 1.

Practice 1 — one Grover iterate on a marked two-qubit state [9 marks]

Prompt. With marked state |11> and the supplied uniform state, carry out the oracle and one diffusion iterate, then give the measured success probability.

Question-demand diagnosis. This is a state-by-state calculation: a named oracle phase flip alone is not an answer. Fastest route. Record the four amplitudes, calculate their mean after the oracle, then reflect each amplitude. Topic-specific trap. Diffusion reflects amplitudes, not probabilities; retain the negative marked amplitude until the reflection.

For Grover search on two qubits with marked |11>, start |s>=(1/2)(1,1,1,1). The oracle gives (1/2)(1,1,1,-1), whose mean amplitude is 1/4. Diffusion reflects each amplitude a about the mean: a'=2(1/4)-a, producing (0,0,0,1). One iteration therefore yields |11> with probability 1.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Apply the N=2 QFT to |1⟩. Check: (|0⟩−|1⟩)/√2.

2. Carry out one Grover iteration for N=4 with a different marked basis state. Check: oracle flips amplitude, not probability.

Practice 2 — exact four-dimensional QFT [8 marks]

Prompt. Compute F4|2> exactly and state the four computational-basis probabilities. Question-demand diagnosis. The required conclusion is an amplitude vector and a probability distribution. Fastest route. Substitute x=2 into the finite-QFT phase formula for y=0,1,2,3 before squaring moduli. Topic-specific trap. A relative minus affects later interference even when all four final probabilities are equal.

Paper-style synthesis: compute QFT_4|2>. Check amplitudes are (1/2)exp(2 pi i*2y/4), y=0,...,3, giving (1/2)(1,-1,1,-1); all measurement probabilities are 1/4.

Mistakes

  • Using the wrong sign in diffusion.
  • Forgetting QFT normalisation.
  • Overclaiming Shor detail: it is official scope, but is not comparably evidenced in 2021–26 papers.
  • Recovery rule: Global phase is unobservable, but relative signs control interference; do not discard them during intermediate steps.

Checklist

  • I can perform a one-iteration Grover table.
  • I know the finite-QFT definition.
  • I distinguish official scope from recurring evidence.
  • Final 60-second audit: Basis order, normalisation, every stage vector, oracle convention and final probabilities are explicit.
