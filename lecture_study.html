Week 2 · high priority

Git workflow and merge conflicts

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(d) [5] asks how conflicts arise and how to prevent and resolve them; 2024/25 Q1(b) asks for Git commands.

A feature branch conflicts with main. Explain the cause, a safe resolution sequence, and two habits that reduce conflicts.

Reveal answer plan
  • Concurrent edits to the same lines or related code create conflicts when histories are integrated.
  • Fetch current main, merge or rebase into the feature branch, resolve each conflict deliberately, then test and complete the integration.
  • Keep branches small, sync often and open pull requests early. Review the final diff before merge.

Vector space model and term independence

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(a) and Q1(b), 5 marks each; 2024/25 Q1(a)–(b).

Describe how a query and document are compared as vectors, then explain a consequence of assuming terms are independent and one remedy.

Reveal answer plan
  • Represent both as weighted term vectors in the same vocabulary; compare direction with cosine similarity.
  • Normalisation reduces length bias. Independent term dimensions miss related words and meaning.
  • Give one concrete synonym example, then a remedy such as expansion or a semantic representation.

Weighting · high priority

Normalisation, tf-idf and BM25

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(d) and Q1(e), 5 marks each; weighting appears across all four local papers.

Explain why duplicating a document can inflate an unnormalised score. Name BM25’s components and the jobs of its two tuning parameters.

Reveal answer plan
  • Raw term frequency grows when text is duplicated, so an unnormalised score can reward length without new relevance.
  • BM25 combines term rarity, saturating term frequency and length normalisation.
  • k1 controls term-frequency saturation; b controls document-length correction.

Evaluation · high priority

Precision, recall, MAP and user needs

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(c) and Q1(h), 5 marks each; 2021/22 and 2024/25 ask for precision-recall graphs.

Define MAP, give one benefit and two limitations, then propose a way to assess diversity among the first k results.

Reveal answer plan
  • AP averages precision at ranks where relevant documents occur; MAP averages AP over queries.
  • It rewards early relevant results but commonly uses binary judgements and misses diversity or user satisfaction.
  • For top-k diversity, measure distinct intents covered or pairwise dissimilarity; state what your measure captures.

Indexing · supporting

Boolean queries and inverted indexes

Paper route: This current lecture supports the module outcome on data structures; it is lower priority than the repeated question families in Most Asked.

Trace how an inverted index answers a two-term Boolean query. What extra positional information is needed for a phrase query?

Reveal answer plan
  • Map each term to its posting list of document IDs, then intersect for AND or union for OR.
  • Keep postings ordered so merges are efficient.
  • Store term positions within each document to test adjacency or proximity rather than mere co-occurrence.

Week 1 · high priority

Choose the learning task

Paper route: 2023/24 Q1(a) [1, 2, 1], Q1(b) [9], and Q1(c) [2] within the 25-mark question.

Given a labelled dataset, identify inputs and target, decide classification or regression, then explain how the same data would be used for clustering.

Reveal answer plan
  • Inputs are independent features; the dependent variable is the target.
  • A discrete target calls for classification; a continuous target calls for regression. Both require labelled examples.
  • Clustering groups unlabelled examples, so remove the target column; provide a different suitable algorithm and application for each task.

Week 3 · high priority

Prepare data without leakage

Paper route: 2023/24 Q1(c) [4] asks for two feature rescaling approaches; 2021/22 Q3(d) asks why kNN needs normalisation.

Explain why distance-based learning needs scaling. Give two methods, then show where imputation and scaling belong in a train/test workflow.

Reveal answer plan
  • Large-unit features can dominate distance even when less informative.
  • Min-max scaling maps to a chosen range; z-normalisation centres by the training mean and divides by training standard deviation.
  • Fit imputation and scaling on training folds only, then apply the learned transforms to validation/test data; a pipeline prevents leakage.

Week 4 · high priority

Cross-validation and metrics

Paper route: 2023/24 Q2(a) [2+2], Q2(b) [6], Q2(c) [5], Q2(d) [10].

Explain 5 × 5-fold stratified cross-validation on 500 examples, then choose the correct confusion-matrix measures and sketch ROC axes.

Reveal answer plan
  • Each of five repeats has five folds of 100; train on 400 and validate on 100 per run, giving 25 fitted classifiers. Stratification preserves class balance.
  • Fix the positive class before counting TP, FP, TN and FN. Calculate only the measures requested in the part.
  • ROC plots true-positive rate against false-positive rate across thresholds; ideal performance is top left. Tune hyperparameters within validation, not on the held-out test set.

Week 5 · high priority

Regression, classification and error

Paper route: 2023/24 Q3(c) [4] asks for MSE, RMSE and MAE; 2021/22 Q1(d) also compares error measures.

For numeric predictions, calculate MSE, RMSE and MAE from residuals. Explain why logistic regression is for a different target type.

Reveal answer plan
  • For residuals ei, MAE = Σ|ei|/n; MSE = Σei2/n; RMSE = √MSE.
  • MAE and RMSE have target units; MSE has squared units and weights large errors more heavily.
  • Linear regression predicts a continuous value. Logistic regression models class probability through a sigmoid of a linear score.

Week 6 · high priority

kNN and decision-tree reasoning

Paper route: 2023/24 Q1(c) [2+6+4] tests kNN and scaling; Q4(a) [4] and Q4(b) [4+6] test entropy and information gain.

Compare kNN classification with regression, then show how a tree chooses a split and why both methods need generalisation checks.

Reveal answer plan
  • kNN votes among nearby labels for classification, or averages nearby numeric targets for regression; justify distance, scaling and k.
  • For a tree, compute parent entropy and the weighted child entropy. Information gain is their difference; choose the largest useful reduction.
  • Check validation performance: small k or deep trees can overfit; large k or shallow trees can underfit.

Lectures 1–2 · high priority

Stocks, net flow and graphical integration

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(a) [12], 2024/25 Q4(a) [12], 2023/24 Q4(a) [12].

From inflow and outflow graphs, draw the stock-flow structure, calculate net flow and explain each interval of the stock trajectory.

Reveal answer plan
  • Write net flow = total inflows − total outflows, with units of stock per time.
  • The stock changes by the signed area under net flow. Positive, zero and negative net flow make it rise, stay level or fall.
  • For each graph segment, state slope and curvature, then carry the previous stock value forward; respect the paper’s stated initial value and any permission for negative stock.

Lecture 3 · high priority

Euler calculation and error direction

Paper route: 2025/26 Q1(b) [5] and Q1(c) [8]; comparable parts appear in 2023/24 and 2024/25.

State Euler’s within-step assumption, calculate a stock update from a supplied net-flow graph, and justify whether the estimate is high or low.

Reveal answer plan
  • Use S(t + Δt) = S(t) + Δt × net flow at the step start.
  • Euler treats that sampled rate as constant across the step. Compare each rectangle with the actual area under the net-flow curve to decide error direction.
  • Show each step, its rate, area and running stock; use exactly the Δt requested in that paper part.

Fractional flows and goal seeking

Model a stock with a fractional decrease rate that itself approaches a maximum goal, then explain the stock behaviour and units.

Reveal answer plan

Signed feedback in a backlog

Paper route: 2025/26 Q3(a) [6], Q3(b) [12], Q3(c) [7]; 2024/25 Q1 is a related assignment-backlog variant.

Construct the backlog stock with inflows and completed-work outflow, sign the information links, and trace policy and side-effect loops.

Reveal answer plan
  • Start with the stock equation: backlog accumulates new work plus rework minus completed work.

Lecture 6 · high priority

Smoothing and stock management

Paper route: 2025/26 Q4(b) [5], Q4(c) [10], Q4(d) [5]; 2024/25 Q2(b)–(c) tests staff recruitment.

Distinguish an expected-loss anchor from gap correction. Explain how a delayed expectation changes hiring after the desired stock moves.

Reveal answer plan
  • Required inflow = expected normal losses + (desired stock − actual stock)/adjustment time.
  • Expected losses replace routine outflow; the gap term moves the stock toward its goal. A smoothing delay makes expectations respond gradually.
  • For an ageing-chain question, draw every staff stock and progression/attrition flow before writing equations. Explain any lower bound on hiring when the target falls.

Topic 6 · implementation

Choose an AI-assisted workflow

Practice basis: Topic 6 lecture compares review, support, junior-developer, test-driven and high-autonomy workflows. No local CT4111 paper establishes marks or wording.

For a change with moderate risk and clear tests, choose an AI-assisted implementation workflow. Explain its benefits, limitations and human checks.

Reveal answer plan
  • State the task, acceptance criteria and available tests before choosing a workflow.
  • Compare how much work the tool does and what the human can verify. An LLM reviewer gives quick feedback; a junior-developer or test-driven workflow needs stronger specification and review.
