CT4100 Topic Guide

Recommender systems

Rank items for a person using their behaviour, preferences and similar people or items.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions: CF predicts from similar users/items; content-based matches item and user profiles. Missing interaction is not a dislike. Compare: user-CF needs shared ratings; item-CF is often stable; hybrid metadata helps cold start. Question cue: give data representation, score, filtering, evaluation and deployment weakness.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) CF

Question demand: Similarities .8/.2, ratings 5/1: predict. Plan/choice: use normalised weighted average. Worked answer: (.8x5+.2x1)/1=4.2, a strong candidate after consumed-item filtering. Mark-winning points: weights/denominator. Common trap: missing=zero.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Recommend films for a new user and identify difficult users. Plan/choice: explicit onboarding/content fallback, then monitor sparsity. Worked answer: collect genre/item seeds, use metadata and diverse popularity initially, transition to CF; flag low-neighbour similarity or uncertain predictions. Evaluate precision plus novelty/diversity/satisfaction. Mark-winning points: cold-start mechanism and uncertainty. Common trap: CF without comparable evidence.

Purpose and module relevance

Recommenders are a stated CT4100 outcome. They turn partial preference data into useful suggestions, but bring cold-start, sparsity and evaluation challenges.

What a question is really asking

Identify the recommendation signal, explain how a score is predicted, and discuss a real deployment weakness. Plan: choose content-based or collaborative filtering; show neighbours/features; predict and rank; evaluate beyond accuracy.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and notation

  • Content-based: recommend items like those the user liked.
  • Collaborative filtering: use similar users or similar items.
  • Cold start: too little data for a new user/item.
  • Sparsity: most user-item ratings/interactions are absent.

Method: make a neighbour recommendation

  1. Represent users by ratings or implicit interactions.
  2. Compute similarity only on shared evidence, then select trusted neighbours.
  3. Aggregate neighbours’ ratings for an unseen item, often weighted by similarity.
  4. Rank candidate items and filter already-consumed or unsuitable items.
  5. Evaluate offline and with users, checking novelty, diversity, fairness and satisfaction.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question

Alice is similar to Ben by 0.8 and Cara by 0.2. Ben rates film X as 5; Cara rates it as 1. Give a weighted prediction.

Model answer

(0.8×5+0.2×1)/(0.8+0.2)=4.2. X is a strong candidate. The calculation relies on similarity being meaningful and cannot solve a brand-new Alice problem.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Build confidence

How can a new-item cold start be reduced? Check: use metadata/content features, promotion/exploration, or early onboarding interactions.

Exam bridge

Design recommendations for an online course platform. Outline: signals; algorithm; cold-start path; accuracy plus completion/diversity measures; privacy and feedback-loop risk.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Calling missing data a negative rating.
  • Using accuracy alone when repeated popular items are unhelpful.
  • Ignoring cold start after naming it.

Final answer checklist

  • Is the data representation clear?
  • Did I show how the score is formed?
  • Did I include a practical limitation and metric?
