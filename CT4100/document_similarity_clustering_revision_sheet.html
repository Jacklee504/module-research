CT4100 Topic Guide

Document similarity and clustering

Group related documents and measure how close two documents are in a shared representation.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions: document vectors share vocabulary; cosine compares direction; k-means alternates nearest-centroid assignment and centroid mean update. Assumption: proximity reflects topic; features, k and seeds matter. Compare: cosine resists length bias, Euclidean is magnitude-sensitive. Question cue: specify fields, metric, algorithm, quality check and search purpose.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) article similarity

Question demand: Cluster articles with title, abstract, keywords, body, authors and bibliography. Plan/choice: weighted textual vectors plus optional normalised metadata. Worked answer: emphasise title/abstract/keywords in tf-idf, optionally add citation features, and use cosine; inspect representative terms to check coherence. Mark-winning points: field weights and normalisation. Common trap: metric proves semantics.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Use k-means to diversify results and assess it. Plan/choice: assignment-update loop, then representatives and tests. Worked answer: choose k, initialise, assign nearest, recompute means to stability; choose across coherent clusters and assess cohesion/separation plus labels over multiple seeds/k. Mark-winning points: assignment before update. Common trap: clusters meaningful without inspection.

Purpose and module relevance

Similarity supports near-duplicate detection, clustering, result organisation and recommenders. It is a secondary but active CT4100 topic.

What a question is really asking

Show the representation, compute or describe similarity, then explain what a clustering choice does. Plan: vectorise; choose metric; assign/merge; inspect cluster quality; discuss sensitivity.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and notation

  • Cosine similarity: angle-based similarity for weighted term vectors.
  • Centroid: average vector of a cluster.
  • k-means: repeatedly assign to closest centroid then update centroids.
  • Intra/inter-cluster: members should be close within and separated across clusters.

Method: run k-means conceptually

  1. Represent each document with comparable weights.
  2. Choose k initial centroids (and note that initial choice matters).
  3. Assign every document to its most similar/nearest centroid.
  4. Recalculate each centroid from its assigned documents.
  5. Repeat until assignments stabilise, then inspect coherence and outliers.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question

With centroids c1=(1,0), c2=(0,1), assign d=(2,1) using cosine.

Model answer

cos(d,c1)=2/√5≈0.89 and cos(d,c2)=1/√5≈0.45. Assign d to cluster 1. Next, update c1 using d and its other members; this is why k-means is iterative.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Build confidence

Why can raw Euclidean distance mislead on text vectors? Check: longer documents can have larger magnitude; cosine focuses on direction.

Exam bridge

Explain how clustering could diversify search results. Outline: form topical clusters; select representatives; benefit for ambiguous queries; choose k/quality measure; failure mode.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Updating centroids before all assignments are considered.
  • Calling a cluster meaningful without inspecting its terms/documents.
  • Forgetting the choice of k and representation change the answer.

Final answer checklist

  • Have I specified the metric?
  • Are assignment and update separate?
  • Did I name a quality or sensitivity issue?
