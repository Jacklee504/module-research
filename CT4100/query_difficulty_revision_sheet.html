CT4100 Topic Guide

Query difficulty prediction

Estimate whether a query is likely to produce a useful ranking before, or after, search.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions: pre-retrieval features use query/collection evidence; post-retrieval features use returned documents/scores. Signals include ambiguity, idf, clarity and score spread. Assumption: these estimate risk, not relevance. Compare: df is known before ranking; top-result agreement is not. Question cue: label evidence timing, direction and intervention.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) sources

Question demand: Give query, collection and returned-list predictors for “mercury”. Plan/choice: classify before explaining. Worked answer: short ambiguous query; common/high-df term; heterogeneous top results or flat scores. Offer planet/element facets. Mark-winning points: source labels and causal direction. Common trap: short always means hard.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Design and validate a digital-library predictor. Plan/choice: construct features, calibrate on held-out effectiveness, define safe actions. Worked answer: combine query length/idf, collection coherence, top-k score gap and topical agreement; learn against held-out AP/NDCG and trigger clarification only above a risk threshold. Evaluate false interventions. Mark-winning points: separate features, labels and action. Common trap: tuning/testing on same queries.

Purpose and module relevance

Difficulty prediction lets an IR system warn, reformulate or spend more computation on likely failures. CT4100 distinguishes pre-retrieval, collection-wide and post-retrieval evidence.

What a question is really asking

Classify the evidence by when it is available and explain why it signals risk. Plan: name the stage; define a feature; connect it to ambiguity, rarity or score separation; propose a system response.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and notation

  • Pre-retrieval: available from query/collection before results.
  • Post-retrieval: uses the returned list or score distribution.
  • Clarity: whether top-result language differs from the collection.
  • Score spread: a clear gap may indicate a more decisive ranking.

Method: reason from feature to action

  1. Inspect query length, term rarity and ambiguity.
  2. Use collection statistics such as df to see whether terms are discriminative.
  3. After retrieval, inspect score concentration, result agreement or topic mix.
  4. Predict easy/hard cautiously: a signal is evidence, not certainty.
  5. Choose an intervention: ask for clarification, expand a query, diversify, or show a fallback.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question

Compare “jaguar” with “jaguar habitat rainforest” as a query-difficulty case.

Model answer

“Jaguar” is short and ambiguous: car, animal and sports-team intents can mix, so it is likely hard. The longer query has rare contextual terms that constrain intent, so pre-retrieval evidence predicts a clearer ranking. The system could offer intent facets for the first query.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Build confidence

Name one post-retrieval feature. Check: the gap between top scores, or the similarity of top documents, is only known after ranking.

Exam bridge

Design a difficulty predictor for a digital library. Outline: list features by stage; say what each signals; describe validation against effectiveness; propose an intervention and a false-positive risk.

High-value traps and corrections

Integrated paper-style practice - predict a difficult query [8 marks]

Scenario. A medical service receives “mercury”. Top-ten scores have little separation, results cover planet, element and newspaper, and users often reformulate to “mercury poisoning symptoms”. Design a difficulty response.

Question-demand diagnosis: identify evidence, choose an intervention, and justify uncertainty handling.

Fastest route: connect score ambiguity and reformulation to ambiguous intent, then choose clarification or diversification.

Worked answer: weak score separation, heterogeneous results and reformulation are independent difficulty signals. Present intent choices such as planet, element, newspaper or health exposure, or diversify early results. Do not simply promote the most clicked sense because popularity can suppress urgent medical intent. Monitor selection, successful clicks and further reformulation against a conventional ranked baseline, revising intents if users remain unsatisfied.

Topic-specific trap: a low score can be a clear niche query; difficulty needs more than one signal.

  • Calling returned-list evidence pre-retrieval.
  • Equating a short query with a bad query in every case.
  • Giving features without explaining their expected direction.

Final answer checklist

  • Did I label when the evidence exists?
  • Did I link feature, prediction and action?
  • Did I acknowledge uncertainty?
