1. Vector Space Model And Normalisation

FrontBackPapers
How does the VSM represent and rank documents?Queries and documents are weighted term vectors, usually TF-IDF; rank by cosine similarity.2023/24-2025/26
What is the cosine-similarity formula?cos(q,d) = q.d / (|q||d|).2023/24-2025/26
What is the purpose of normalisation in the VSM?It reduces document-length bias, so a long or duplicated document does not score highly just from extra term occurrences.2024/25-2025/26
What is the term-independence limitation, and how can it be reduced?VSM misses meaning/synonymy; use expansion, a thesaurus, embeddings, or neural representations.2024/25-2025/26

2. Term Weighting, BM25, And Learned Weights

FrontBackPapers
What are TF and IDF?TF is within-document evidence; IDF rewards rare terms: log(N / df).2021/22-2025/26
What makes a good weighting scheme?Reward query and rare terms, use diminishing returns for repeated terms, and correct length bias.2021/22, 2024/25, 2025/26
What is TF saturation, and why use it?Extra occurrences still help, but with diminishing returns, so repetition does not dominate ranking.2021/22, 2024/25, 2025/26
What is BM25?A ranking function combining IDF, saturating TF, and length normalisation.2025/26
What do BM25's k1 and b control?k1 controls TF saturation; b controls length normalisation.2025/26
What is an axiomatic weighting approach?Designing a weighting function to satisfy desired ranking properties.2021/22, 2024/25
What are the main risks of learning term weights, and how are they controlled?Overfitting learns quirks of limited labels; click bias favours seen high-ranked results. Validate on held-out queries and use debiased clicks or editorial labels.2021/22, 2024/25

3. Retrieval Evaluation, MAP, And Diversity

FrontBackPapers
What is precision?The fraction retrieved that is relevant: TP / (TP + FP).2021/22, 2024/25
What is recall?The fraction of all relevant documents retrieved: TP / (TP + FN).2021/22, 2024/25
How do you make a precision-recall graph?Plot precision against recall at each relevant rank; higher and further right is better, though precision often falls as recall rises.2021/22, 2024/25
What are AP and MAP?AP averages precision values at ranks containing relevant documents; MAP averages AP across queries.2025/26
What is MAP's key limitation?It usually assumes binary relevance and does not capture diversity, novelty, or user satisfaction.2025/26
Why are precision and recall not enough to evaluate search?They measure relevance coverage, but not satisfaction, result diversity, novelty, effort, or the quality of the user experience.2023/24, 2025/26
What is top-k diversity?How varied and non-redundant the first k results are.2025/26
Why measure top-k diversity?It reduces near-duplicates and increases the chance that an early result covers each possible user intent.2021/22, 2023/24, 2025/26
How can diversity in the top k results be measured?Measure distinct intents/subtopics covered, or average pairwise dissimilarity between the top k results.2025/26

4. Relevance Feedback And Query Expansion

FrontBackPapers
What is explicit relevance feedback?User labels identify returned documents as relevant or non-relevant.2023/24
What is Rocchio, including its update rule?It moves a query toward relevant and away from non-relevant documents: q' = alpha q + beta mean(Drel) - gamma mean(Dnonrel).2023/24-2025/26
What is pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF), including its weakness?Expand from assumed-useful top results; incorrect assumptions can cause drift.2023/24
What is local analysis?Selecting candidate expansion terms from the current query's returned documents.2021/22-2025/26
Local analysis versus a fixed thesaurus?Local analysis is contextual; a thesaurus is controlled and less dependent on noisy results.2025/26
What is query drift, and how can it be limited?Expansion shifts intent; retain original terms, limit weights, or ask approval.2021/22-2025/26
How are local-analysis or PRF expansion terms selected?Prefer terms frequent in relevant or pseudo-relevant top documents but rare in the collection; remove stopwords, irrelevant terms, and near-duplicates.2021/22, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
How should suggested expansion terms be made diverse?Choose useful terms covering different aspects, not near-duplicates.2023/24, 2024/25
How can feedback use non-binary labels, and when can it fail?Weight documents/labels by confidence or graded relevance; it can fail when relevant documents do not form one coherent cluster.2025/26
What do alpha, beta, and gamma do in Rocchio?alpha retains the original query; beta weights relevant-document evidence; gamma weights non-relevant-document evidence that is subtracted.2023/24-2025/26
What are Rocchio's main limitations?It needs accurate feedback, which can be costly; noisy or misjudged documents cause query drift, and one relevant-document centroid can blur multiple intents.2024/25, 2025/26

5. Query Difficulty Prediction

FrontBackPapers
What is the difference between pre- and post-retrieval features?Pre-retrieval features use only the query and collection statistics, before any search. Post-retrieval features use the initial ranked results and their scores.2021/22-2025/26
What are the three sources of query-difficulty evidence?The query itself, collection-wide statistics, and the initial returned ranked list.2021/22-2025/26
What can ambiguity or weak score separation suggest?A difficult query: ambiguous terms indicate several possible intents, while similar top scores indicate an uncertain ranking.2021/22-2025/26
What can an IR system do for a difficult query?Clarify intent, suggest reformulation, expand the query, or diversify early results.2021/22-2025/26
What are common pre-retrieval query-difficulty predictors?Query length; average IDF, where low values suggest broad/common terms; term ambiguity; and collection statistics such as term frequency and co-occurrence, where weak coherence can suggest difficulty.2023/24

6. Collaborative Filtering And Recommenders

FrontBackPapers
What are user-based and item-based collaborative filtering?User-based recommends items liked by similar users; item-based recommends items similar to those the user has already liked or consumed.2021/22-2025/26
What is the basic CF workflow?Build a user-item rating/interaction matrix, find similar users or items, predict scores for unseen items, then rank them.2021/22-2025/26
What is cold start?A new user/item has too little interaction data for reliable CF predictions.2021/22-2025/26
How can cold start be handled?Use onboarding ratings, metadata/content, a popularity fallback, or a hybrid recommender.2025/26
How can difficult recommendation users be identified?Look for sparse histories, weak similarity to other users, inconsistent preferences, or low-confidence predictions.2025/26
How should recommenders be evaluated, and what is a key limitation?Test against held-out interactions using ranking metrics; offline data may not reflect satisfaction, novelty, or diversity in real use.2021/22-2025/26
What are CF's main advantages and limitations?It personalises without needing item content and can surface unexpected items; sparsity, cold start, and popularity bias can reduce quality and diversity.2021/22, 2024/25
How can a recommender diversify its top results?Re-rank high-scoring candidates to penalise near-duplicates and cover different genres, topics, or user intents.2021/22

7. Document Similarity And Clustering

FrontBackPapers
How should scientific articles be represented for similarity?Build a weighted feature vector: give title, abstract, and keywords high weight; optionally add full text, authors, venue, and citation/bibliography evidence.2021/22, 2024/25
How can article similarity be scored?Use cosine similarity between normalised weighted term vectors; optionally combine it with author or citation overlap using a weighted score.2021/22, 2024/25
What are the repeated k-means steps?Choose k initial centroids, assign each document to its nearest centroid, recompute each centroid as its cluster mean, then repeat until assignments stop changing.2021/22, 2024/25
How can clustering help IR?Group results or collections by topic, helping users browse subtopics, navigate results, and find related documents.2021/22, 2024/25
How can clustering quality be assessed?Good clusters have high within-cluster coherence and high between-cluster separation; also test stability or compare against known labels.2023/24
What are clustering's main limitations?Results depend on the representation, chosen number of clusters, and initial seeds; clusters may not reflect relevance or handle overlapping topics well.2021/22, 2023/24, 2024/25

8. Extended Boolean Retrieval

FrontBackPapers
What is extended Boolean retrieval, and why use it?A soft Boolean model that gives AND/OR queries a graded match score, rather than a strict match/no-match outcome, so partial matches can be ranked.2021/22, 2024/25
What are the steps in an extended-Boolean calculation?Take each term's relevance score, use the stated extended-AND or extended-OR formula, substitute the values, calculate the final score, then rank higher-scoring documents first.2021/22, 2024/25

9. Web Search: PageRank And HITS

FrontBackPapers
What is PageRank, and how does a link contribute?An iterative link-based authority score. A page distributes its current score across its outgoing links, so a link from a high-authority page contributes more authority.2023/24
What do damping and link spam mean for PageRank?Damping gives a user a small probability of teleporting instead of following a link, preventing sinks and traps. Link farms can artificially inflate authority, while popularity can outweigh topical relevance.2023/24
What are HITS hubs and authorities?Authorities are pages linked to by good hubs; hubs are pages that link to good authorities. The two scores reinforce each other.2025/26
How does HITS compute scores?Repeatedly set each authority score from the hub scores of pages linking to it, and each hub score from the authority scores of pages it links to; normalise after each iteration.2025/26
How does HITS differ from PageRank?HITS computes separate hub and authority scores on a query-related graph at search time; PageRank computes one global, query-independent authority score in advance.2025/26

10. Learning To Rank

FrontBackPapers
What is LTR, and what does a training example contain?LTR learns a ranking function from relevance data. A training example contains a query, a candidate document, its feature values, and a relevance grade or preference label.2025/26
What are pointwise, pairwise, and listwise LTR?Pointwise predicts each document's relevance grade; pairwise learns which of two documents should rank higher; listwise directly optimises the ordering of a result list.2025/26
How should LTR be evaluated, and what is a click-data risk?Evaluate on held-out queries with ranking metrics such as MAP or nDCG. Clicks are position-biased because highly ranked results are seen more often.2025/26
How does neural LTR differ from traditional LTR?Neural LTR can learn semantic representations directly from text; traditional LTR relies more on engineered features. Neural models need more data and compute and are less interpretable.2025/26
Why use LTR rather than manually tuned ranking weights?It learns how features should influence ranking from relevance data, rather than relying on hand-set weights; its quality depends on representative training labels.2025/26
