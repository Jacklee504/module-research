Exam study route

Attempt each paper family before revealing its plan. Current Canvas lectures support the early topics; the remaining families come from repeated local papers.

  • Retrieval models

    2025/26 Q1(a)–(b), 5 marks each; 2024/25 Q1(a)–(b): compare query and document vectors, then explain term independence and a remedy.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Represent both as weighted term vectors in the same vocabulary; compare direction with cosine similarity.
    • Normalisation reduces length bias. Independent term dimensions miss related words and meaning.
    • Give one concrete synonym example, then a remedy such as query expansion or semantic representation.
    Canvas IR models2025/26 paperTopic guide
  • Weighting and BM25

    2025/26 Q1(d)–(e), 5 marks each: explain score inflation without normalisation and the jobs of BM25’s two tuning parameters.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Raw term frequency grows when text is duplicated, so an unnormalised score can reward length without new relevance.
    • BM25 combines term rarity, saturating term frequency and length normalisation.
    • k1 controls term-frequency saturation; b controls document-length correction.
    Canvas weighting schemes2025/26 paperTopic guide
  • Evaluation

    2025/26 Q1(c) and Q1(h), 5 marks each: define MAP, give a benefit and two limitations, then assess top-k diversity.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Average precision averages precision at ranks containing relevant documents; MAP averages that result over queries.
    • MAP rewards early relevant results, but commonly uses binary judgements and misses diversity or user satisfaction.
    • For top-k diversity, measure distinct intents covered or pairwise dissimilarity; state what that measure captures.
    Canvas evaluation2025/26 paperTopic guide
  • Indexing support

    Inverted indexes and Boolean matching support a module outcome; recent repeated families have higher priority. Trace a two-term Boolean query.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Map each term to its posting list of document IDs, then intersect for AND or union for OR.
    • Keep postings ordered so merges are efficient.
    • Store term positions within each document to test adjacency or proximity for a phrase query.
    Canvas indexingExam question families
  • Relevance feedback

    2025/26 Q1(f)–(g) [5+5] and Q3(a) [10]: extend Rocchio to weighted feedback, test its clustering assumption and compare local query expansion with a thesaurus.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Move the query toward relevant-document vectors and away from non-relevant ones; use graded weights when feedback is not binary.
    • Explain how multiple distinct intents or misleading top results cause query drift.
    • Local analysis uses this query's returned set; a fixed thesaurus is stable but less contextual. Compare coverage, noise and control.
    Canvas feedback lecture2025/26 Q1 and Q3Question familyTopic guide
  • Query difficulty

    2025/26 Q2(a) [10]: predict hard queries from query wording, collection statistics and the retrieved result set.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Separate pre-retrieval signals such as ambiguity, length and term rarity from post-retrieval signals such as score spread or result diversity.
    • State what each signal suggests about likely effectiveness, without claiming it proves relevance.
    • Give a response to a difficult query, such as clarification, expansion or diverse suggestions.
    2025/26 Q2(a)Question family
  • Recommenders

    2025/26 Q2(b) [10]: design movie collaborative filtering, handle a new user's cold start and identify users for whom recommendations may be unreliable.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Start with user-item interactions, find similar users or items, predict unseen preferences and rank suggestions.
    • For cold start use onboarding ratings, item features or a hybrid fallback.
    • Flag sparse histories, low neighbour similarity and unstable preferences; distinguish offline accuracy from novelty and user satisfaction.
    2025/26 Q2(b)Question familyTopic guide
  • Similarity and clustering

    2024/25 Q4(b)–(c) [9+8]: combine article fields into a similarity measure, then explain how clustering supports two IR tasks.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Weight title, abstract, keywords and body text; add authors or citations as separate evidence and normalise before combining scores.
    • Choose a document representation and similarity, describe clustering steps, then give two uses such as browsing and result grouping.
    • Evaluate coherence and separation, and note sensitivity to feature weights, cluster count and vocabulary mismatch.

  • Extended Boolean retrieval

    2021/22 Q2(a)–(b) and 2024/25 Q3(a)–(b) [10+5]: calculate graded AND/OR relevance, then construct a precision-recall graph from the ranked results.

    Reveal answer plan
    • Apply the paper's stated extended-Boolean formula and term weights, showing each intermediate score.
    • Walk the ranked list, count relevant results encountered and calculate precision and recall at each relevant rank.
    • Plot precision against recall and explain the effect of ranking changes.
    2024/25 Q3Question family
  • Web search and ranking

    2025/26 Q3(b) [10] asks for HITS versus PageRank; Q4(a)–(b) [10+10] asks for learning-to-rank examples, metrics and neural trade-offs.

    Reveal answer plan
    • HITS assigns mutually reinforcing hub and authority scores on a query-related graph; PageRank is a damped global link score.
    • For learning to rank, name query-document features, relevance labels or preferences, and evaluation on held-out queries with MAP or nDCG.
    • Compare engineered features with learned neural representations on data needs, compute and interpretability.
