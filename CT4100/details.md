## Predictability Rating

High. The same core topics recur heavily across the papers. The main warning is that the 2025/26 paper changed the exam structure: Question 1 became compulsory and worth 40 marks, with two further 20-mark questions chosen from the rest.

## Evidence From Past Papers

- Core retrieval model questions recur: vector space model, term independence, normalisation, tf-idf, BM25 and weighting-scheme design.
- Query expansion and feedback are high-value: Rocchio, pseudo-relevance feedback, local analysis and diverse suggested query terms.
- Evaluation is regular: precision/recall, MAP, user-focused measures and diversity of top-k results.
- Query difficulty appears repeatedly, especially pre-retrieval, collection-wide and returned-list/post-retrieval evidence.
- Recommender systems are a major repeat area: collaborative filtering, evaluation limits, cold-start and difficult-user problems.
- Secondary but active areas: clustering/document similarity, PageRank/HITS, learned term weights, Learning to Rank and neural LTR.

## Format Pattern

- 2021/22, 2023/24, 2024/25:
  - Answer any three questions.
  - Four questions, all equally weighted.
- 2025/26:
  - Answer Question 1 and any two other questions.
  - Question 1 is worth 40 marks.
  - Questions 2-4 are worth 20 marks each.

This matters: if the newer format continues, broad coverage of the fundamentals is more important because Question 1 is compulsory.

## Assessment Strategy

- This is 70% exam and 30% CA, so it is less CA-friendly than CT421 but better than CS4423.
- Because the exam is repetitive, it may still be a good target if the lecturer/style suits you.
- Highest-value preparation areas:
  - Vector space model, cosine similarity, term independence, normalisation.
  - tf-idf, BM25, axiomatic term weighting.
  - Precision, recall, precision-recall graph, MAP, diversity metrics.
  - Query difficulty prediction: query features, collection evidence, returned-list evidence.
  - Rocchio, relevance feedback, pseudo-relevance feedback, local analysis, query expansion.
  - Collaborative filtering, recommender-system evaluation, diversity, cold start.
  - Document similarity and clustering.
  - PageRank, HITS, web search/link analysis.
  - Learning to Rank, neural LTR, feature representation, interpretability, evaluation.
- If the 2025/26 structure continues, prepare short, high-confidence answers for many subtopics because the compulsory Question 1 can sample widely.

## Overall Judgement

CT4100 looks like a good target despite being 70% exam. The past papers are strongly repetitive, the lecturer overlap with CT421 is useful, and the repeated topics are clear. The main downside is only 30% CA and the newer compulsory-question format, which means weak spots are harder to avoid.

## Short Learning Material

Use Degreed/Percipio-style catalogues for short tutorials, guided projects, or video chapters. Prefer material under 3 hours per topic.

- Information retrieval fundamentals: indexing, inverted files, ranking and query processing.
- Vector space model and cosine similarity: worked examples with term weights.
- tf-idf and BM25: intuition, formula use and ranking examples.
- Search evaluation: precision, recall, MAP, precision-recall curves and top-k diversity.
- Query expansion and relevance feedback: Rocchio, pseudo-relevance feedback and local analysis.
- Recommender systems basics: collaborative filtering, cold start, difficult users and evaluation.
- Web search/link analysis: PageRank, HITS and document similarity.
