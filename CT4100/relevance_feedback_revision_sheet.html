CT4100 Topic Guide

Relevance feedback and query expansion

Improve a query by learning from documents or explicit user judgements.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions/equation: explicit feedback uses labels; PRF assumes top-k useful. Rocchio q'=alpha q+beta centroid(relevant)-gamma centroid(non-relevant). Assumption: useful documents cluster; noisy/diverse needs violate it. Compare: PRF is label-free but drifts; thesauri are controlled but less contextual. Question cue: state evidence, update rule, safeguard and evaluation.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) Rocchio

Question demand: q=(1,0), relevant=(1,1), non-relevant=(0,1), all weights 1. Plan/choice: add and subtract componentwise. Worked answer: q'=(2,0); term one strengthens and term two cancels. Mark-winning points: negative centroid. Common trap: subtracting relevant evidence.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Suggest diverse expansion terms without labels. Plan/choice: PRF candidate scoring plus diversification. Worked answer: retrieve top documents, select informative candidates, cluster/enforce marginal relevance, preserve original terms and present a small varied set. Evaluate effectiveness and diversity against baseline. Mark-winning points: drift control. Common trap: add every frequent word.

Purpose and module relevance

Feedback is a major CT4100 tool for vocabulary mismatch: users and relevant documents may use different words for the same need.

What a question is really asking

Explain where added terms come from, how the query changes, and how drift is controlled. Plan: identify explicit versus pseudo feedback; give Rocchio or a term-selection method; state benefit and risk.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and notation

  • Explicit feedback: user labels documents relevant/non-relevant.
  • Pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF): assume top k initial results are relevant.
  • Rocchio: q′=αq+β(mean relevant)−γ(mean non-relevant).
  • Query drift: expansion moves away from the original intent.

Method: apply feedback safely

  1. Run the original query and collect labels or top k documents.
  2. Extract candidate terms, usually weighted by usefulness and not just frequency.
  3. Move the query toward relevant vectors and away from non-relevant vectors.
  4. Keep strong original terms and cap/inspect additions to prevent drift.
  5. Re-rank and compare effectiveness against the baseline.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question

q=(1,0), relevant centroid=(1,1), non-relevant centroid=(0,1), α=β=γ=1. Find q′.

Model answer

q′=(1,0)+(1,1)−(0,1)=(2,0). The evidence strengthens term one and cancels term two because it appears in both relevant and non-relevant evidence.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Build confidence

Why can PRF fail? Check: if early results are off topic, their terms reinforce the wrong intent.

Exam bridge

Recommend an expansion approach for a medical search interface. Outline: explicit feedback or trusted vocabulary; candidate terms; drift safeguards; evaluation; user-safety caveat.

Integrated paper-style practice - Rocchio feedback [8 marks]

Scenario. For “coastal flood adaptation”, two reports about managed retreat are relevant and a tourism report is non-relevant. Explain the Rocchio update and a drift safeguard.

Question-demand diagnosis: show how positive and negative evidence move the vector, then protect the original intent.

Fastest route: start at q, move toward the relevant centroid, away from the non-relevant centroid, retain anchor terms.

Worked answer: q'=αq+βmean(Drel)−γmean(Dnonrel). Managed-retreat terms gain weight; tourism terms lose it, so later results favour the judged need. Two judgments can be unrepresentative, however. Retain strong coastal/flood terms, cap expansion weights and offer additions for user approval. Compare later precision and recall before accepting the expansion.

Topic-specific trap: subtracting a centroid does not mean deleting every shared term.

  • Claiming PRF has real relevance labels.
  • Forgetting the negative centroid in Rocchio.
  • Adding every frequent term instead of selecting informative, diverse terms.

Final answer checklist

  • Is the evidence source clear?
  • Have I explained each Rocchio component?
  • Did I mention drift and evaluation?
