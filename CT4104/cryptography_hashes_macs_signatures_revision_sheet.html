CT4104 overview

CT4104 Topic Guide

Cryptography, hashes, MACs and signatures

Choose a primitive from the security claim and who can hold or verify a key.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Encryption gives confidentiality; AEAD also detects modification. Never reuse an AEAD nonce with one key.
  • Hash: unkeyed fingerprint. MAC: shared-key integrity/origin. Signature: private-key signing, public verification/non-repudiation-style evidence.
  • A MAC verifier can forge; a signature verifier need not hold signing key.
  • Use a salted memory-hard KDF for passwords; a fast hash is not password storage.

Medium practice — 15 marks

Payroll sends records to three auditors. (a) [5] choose hash/MAC/signature. (b) [5] protect transit. (c) [5] state key/nonce handling.

Answer for every part

(a)

Route: ask who verifies and who may forge. Answer: use a signature: auditors verify public key but cannot sign. Trap: choosing MAC for public verification.

(b)

Route: choose transport property. Answer: TLS/AEAD gives confidentiality and tamper detection in transit; a bare hash does neither origin authentication nor secrecy. Trap: calling encryption automatically integrity.

(c)

Route: identify secret and uniqueness. Answer: protect signing private key; generate unique AEAD nonce per key and rotate/version keys. Trap: reuse nonce.

Hard practice — 15 marks

A developer stores SHA-256(password) and uses HMAC as a public file checksum. (a) [5] diagnose. (b) [5] correct password storage. (c) [5] state appropriate HMAC use.

Answer for every part

(a)

Answer: fast unsalted password hashes enable offline guessing; clients cannot verify HMAC without shared secret. Trap: calling any hash encryption.

(b)

Answer: use a unique salt and memory-hard password KDF, then verify with constant-time comparison. Trap: storing an encryption key beside password hash.

(c)

Answer: HMAC suits two parties sharing a secret who need message integrity/authentication; public files need signature or trusted channel. Trap: claiming HMAC is non-repudiation.
