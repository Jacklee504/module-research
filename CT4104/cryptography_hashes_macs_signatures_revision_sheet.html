Purpose, scope and examiner demand

Question-demand diagnosis and answer plan

State the goal, parties and keys; choose the primitive; show verification; state an unmet property.

Question-type cues

  • Application cue: Authenticate software updates for many clients.
  • Analysis cue: Partner disputes HMAC request after timeout.

Definitions, notation and discriminating comparisons

  • Hash: digest; no origin guarantee alone.
  • MAC: shared-secret integrity/origin check.
  • Signature: private-key signing, public verification.
  • Symmetric / public-key encryption: shared-key / recipient-key confidentiality.

Step-by-step methods and solution routes

  1. Ask whether confidentiality, integrity, origin or public verification is needed.
  2. Identify shared or public/private key relationships.
  3. Select encryption, hash, MAC or signature.
  4. Explain what the construction cannot provide.

Worked examples and method extensions

Services sharing K send m with MAC(K,m). The receiver recomputes it, detecting alteration or a sender without K. It gives no public proof: either holder of K could create it.

Exam-paper-led practice

Practice question 1 [medium/hard]

Scenario and demand: ReleaseHub publishes update U and its SHA-256 digest. 50,000 clients must verify U without holding a shared secret; the vendor may later need to prove which release it signed. Explain and justify a suitable construction. [9 marks]

Question-demand diagnosis and fastest route: This asks for property and key-holder reasoning, not “use encryption.” Fastest route: hash the exact bytes, sign the digest with the protected vendor private key, then verify with an authenticated public key. A plain digest is alterable with the file; an HMAC would force every client to hold a forgery-capable secret. The signature gives integrity, origin authentication and public verification, but not confidentiality. A verifier recomputes H(U), checks the signature and rejects any mismatch. Trap: a signature over a hash does not hide U.

Practice question 2 [medium/hard]

Scenario and demand: A partner sends amount=50&to=A&nonce=71 with HMAC(K,m), then times out and disputes the transfer. Give a replay-safe verification design and state what evidence HMAC cannot supply. [8 marks]

Question-demand diagnosis and fastest route: The supplied object requires canonical bytes, freshness and attribution limits. Fastest route: authenticate a canonical serialization containing a unique nonce, expiry and request ID; reject a nonce already recorded, then atomically persist the outcome under that ID. Recompute HMAC(K,m) before acting. This detects alteration and proves that some K-holder made the request, but either partner could have done so; it is not non-repudiation. A signed, retained receipt is needed for third-party attribution. Trap: timestamps alone do not stop replay inside their acceptance window.
