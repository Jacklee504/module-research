MA4344 Topic Guide

Sylow Theorems

Count Sylow subgroups until normality or a contradiction appears.

Purpose

Apply the three Sylow theorems and turn congruence/divisibility into structural conclusions.Exam use: Full p-power, divisibility, congruence, complete candidate list, any intersection count and justified normality are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Write |G|=pam with p∤m.
  2. State a Sylow p-subgroup has order pa.
  3. Let np be their number: np|m and np≡1 mod p.
  4. If np=1, conclude normality; use it in a quotient or simplicity argument.
  5. Question interpretation: Factor |G|, write both constraints on n_p, list all candidates, and turn uniqueness into normality only after the arithmetic forces n_p=1.

Key terms

Sylow p-subgroup, np, conjugacy of Sylow subgroups, normality. Theorems: existence, conjugacy, and np≡1 mod p with np dividing m.Rapid recall: Sylow p-subgroups have order p^a where p^a is the full p-part of |G|; they exist, are conjugate, and n_p divides m while n_p=1 mod p for |G|=p^a m.

Proof method

For an application, list the divisors of m that are 1 modulo p. This often leaves only 1. Do not infer uniqueness just because the subgroup order is large.Exam workflow: Use the largest prime first, intersect/count distinct Sylow subgroups when uniqueness is not forced, and state why normality follows from conjugacy.

Worked simple proof

Claim: every group of order 35 has a normal Sylow 5-subgroup. Here n5 divides 7 and n5≡1 mod 5; the only possibility is 1.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Prove a group G of order 132=2^2*3*11 is not simple. Here n11 divides 12 and n11=1 mod 11, so n11=1 or 12; n3 divides 44 and n3=1 mod 3, so n3=1,4 or 22. If neither Sylow subgroup were unique, the 12 Sylow 11-subgroups would contribute 12*(11-1)=120 nonidentity elements and the 22 Sylow 3-subgroups would contribute 22*(3-1)=44. Subgroups of different prime orders intersect only in the identity, so this would require 164 distinct nonidentity elements in a group with only 131. Hence n11=1 or n3=1, giving a proper nontrivial normal subgroup.

Graduated exam bridge

1. For |G|=56, list possible n7. Check: it divides 8 and is 1 mod 7, so 1 or 8.

2. Prove every group of order 15 is not simple. Check: n5 divides 3 and is 1 mod 5, hence is 1.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: prove every group of order 45 is not simple. Check n5 divides 9 and n5=1 mod 5, so n5=1 or 6; n3 divides 5 and n3=1 mod 3, so n3=1 only. The unique Sylow 3-subgroup has order 9 and is normal.

Mistakes

  • Using np≡0 mod p instead of 1.
  • Forgetting to divide |G| by the full p-power first.
  • Assuming all p-subgroups are Sylow p-subgroups.
  • Recovery rule: A candidate list with more than one value is not a conclusion; continue with counting, actions or another prime instead of selecting 1 by preference.
