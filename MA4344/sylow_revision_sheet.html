Sylow Theorems

Factor the group order, constrain np, then turn uniqueness into normality.

What repeatedly appears in the papers

The three Sylow statements are explicitly requested in 2022/23 Question 4(b) and 2023/24--2024/25 Question 4(b), then used to rule out simple orders. This is a recurrent 6 mark theorem + application.

Practice question -- order 30 (15 marks)

Inspired by: 2024/25 Question 4(b,c).

(a) State the three Sylow theorems. [6 marks]

Answer: existence, conjugacy, and np|m with np≡1 (mod p), as in the table. Trap: omit the pa maximality condition.

(b) Prove no group of order 30 is simple. [9 marks]

Demand / quickest route: use p=5 then p=3 if necessary. Answer: n5 divides 6 and is 1 mod 5, so n5=1 or 6. If 1, its Sylow-5 subgroup is normal. If 6, count 24 nonidentity elements of order 5; then n3 divides 10 and is 1 mod 3, hence 1 or 10. n3=10 would require 20 more nonidentity elements, impossible, so n3=1 and a normal Sylow-3 subgroup exists. Trap: conclude n5=1 from congruence alone.
