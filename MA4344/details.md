# MA4344 - Advanced Group Theory

- Predictability: High, especially if the Dane Flannery paper style continues.

## Recommendation

MA4344 looks like a strong maths option if group theory suits you. It has the same favourable 60% exam / 40% CA split as MA3491 and CT421, and the two most recent papers are extremely close in structure.

The main caveat is topic style: this is proof-heavy algebra, not a calculation-light module. If you are comfortable preparing standard theorem statements and proof templates, the exam looks very targetable. If abstract algebra proofs are slow for you, MA3491 may still be the safer maths option.

## Predictability Judgment

Predictability is high for the recent format.

The 2023/24 and 2024/25 papers both use the same overall structure:

- Q1: matrix subgroup of `SL(2,Z)` isomorphic to `Z`, then Lagrange's theorem / isomorphism theorem / homomorphism consequences.
- Q2: cosets, left/right index equality, index 2 normal subgroup, then simple groups or centre/quotient arguments.
- Q3: group actions embedded into a symmetric group, then class equation, nontrivial centre of p-groups, and groups of order `p^2` being abelian.
- Q4: Cauchy's theorem for finite abelian groups, Sylow's theorem, then proving specific group orders are not simple.

The 2021/22 and 2022/23 papers differ more, but still share important recurring material: direct products, isomorphism theorems, finite abelian groups, Cauchy, Sylow, class equation, conjugacy/orbits, and non-simple group arguments.

## Past Paper Pattern

- Recent papers are very similar, especially 2023/24 and 2024/25.
- Core repeat areas: Lagrange's theorem, isomorphism theorems, homomorphism image/order arguments, cosets and normal subgroups.
- Group actions recur strongly: kernels, stabilisers, embeddings into symmetric groups, orbits and conjugacy-style arguments.
- Class equation and p-group results are high-value: nontrivial centre and groups of order `p^2` being abelian.
- Cauchy's theorem, finite abelian groups, Sylow theorems and proving groups are not simple are central preparation targets.
- Older papers add direct products, alternating groups, automorphisms, Euclidean/wallpaper groups and Burnside counting; useful background, but the newest pattern is more focused on cosets, actions, class equation and Sylow.

## Study Strategy

- Build polished proof templates for Lagrange, first/fundamental isomorphism theorem use, cosets, index 2 normality, class equation, p-group centre, Cauchy for finite abelian groups, Sylow theorem statements, and non-simple group order arguments.
- Practise the exact repeated matrix subgroup proof from 2023/24 and 2024/25, but expect the matrix entries to change.
- Prepare group action examples carefully: action on a set, stabilisers, kernels, conjugation action, and embeddings into symmetric groups.
- Keep older topics on a secondary list: Burnside counting, finite abelian classification, direct products, alternating groups and automorphisms.

## Official Module Information

Source: https://www.universityofgalway.ie/course-information/module/MA4344

- Title: Advanced Group Theory
- Description: monoids and groups and their actions, finite state machines, orbits and stabilizers, combinatorics applications such as vertex colorings, Sylow theory, finite simple groups.
- Learning outcomes include:
  - Define monoid actions and prove fundamental theorems.
  - Determine regular languages for finite automata.
  - Use orbit-stabilizer theory to compute automorphism groups of graphs.
  - State and prove Sylow's theorems.
  - Prove there is no finite non-abelian simple group of order less than 60.
