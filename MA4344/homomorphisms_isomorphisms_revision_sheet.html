Homomorphisms and Isomorphisms

Use image/kernel structure and order divisibility to constrain maps.

Practice question -- coprime orders (18 marks)

Inspired by: 2023/24 and 2024/25 Question 1(b)(ii).

(a) If |G| and |H| are coprime finite orders, prove every φ:G→H is trivial. [9 marks]

Demand / quickest route: apply Lagrange to im φ. Answer: imφ≤H, so |imφ| divides |H|. Also G/kerφ≅imφ, so |imφ| divides |G|. Coprimality forces |imφ|=1; therefore φ(g)=eH for every g. Trap: claim G and H have no common elements.

(b) State the First Isomorphism Theorem. [3 marks]

Answer: for φ:G→H, kerφ&lhd;G and G/kerφ≅imφ. Trap: replace image by all H without surjectivity.

(c) Explain why Z21→Z18 need not be trivial. [6 marks]

Answer: gcd(21,18)=3, so the previous obstruction does not apply; e.g. [1]↦[6] has image of order 3. Trap: use coprime reasoning when the orders are not coprime.
