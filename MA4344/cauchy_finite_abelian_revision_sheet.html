MA4344 Topic Guide

Cauchy's Theorem and Finite Abelian Groups

Force prime-order elements, then use abelian structure safely.

Purpose

State and prove the finite-abelian version of Cauchy's theorem, and use it in order arguments.Exam use: Prime factorisation, theorem hypotheses, complete partition list and an isomorphism invariant are visible.

Question plan

  1. Identify a prime p dividing |G|.
  2. State the exact Cauchy conclusion: an element of order p exists.
  3. For a proof, choose an appropriate product/tuple set and group its elements by cyclic shift.
  4. For an application, pass to the cyclic subgroup generated by that element.
  5. Question interpretation: For existence, apply Cauchy prime by prime; for classification, state invariant factors or elementary divisors and rule out isomorphism with an order/exponent invariant.

Key terms

Cauchy's theorem, exponent, direct product, finite abelian group. The finite-abelian theorem: if p divides |G|, G contains an element of order p.Rapid recall: If prime p divides |G|, Cauchy gives an element of order p. A finite abelian group decomposes as a direct product of cyclic prime-power groups, uniquely up to order.

Proof method

Induct on |G|. Choose an element a. If p divides |⟨a⟩|, take a suitable power of a. Otherwise p divides |G/⟨a⟩| (abelian makes ⟨a⟩ normal), use induction in the quotient, then lift and take a power if necessary.Exam workflow: Factor the group order, list partitions of each prime exponent, combine factors, then compare element orders or exponents.

Worked simple proof

Claim: an abelian group of order 21 has an element of order 3. Since 3 divides 21, Cauchy's theorem supplies g with |g|=3.

Practice 1 — classify abelian groups of order 72 [10 marks]

Prompt. Classify all finite abelian groups of order 72 up to isomorphism, then distinguish a cyclic and a non-cyclic example. Question-demand diagnosis. This is a prime-power decomposition and counting problem, not a request to list divisors. Fastest route. Split 72=2³3², list partitions for each primary part, then combine them. Topic-specific trap. Coprime cyclic factors combine to a cyclic product, but an elementary abelian p-part does not become cyclic.

Classify abelian groups of order 72=2^3*3^2. The 2-part is Z8, Z4xZ2, or Z2^3; the 3-part is Z9 or Z3^2. Combining independently gives 3*2=6 isomorphism types. For example Z8xZ9 is cyclic Z72, whereas Z2^3xZ3^2 has exponent lcm(2,3)=6, so they are not isomorphic.

Graduated exam bridge

1. What does Cauchy's theorem give for an abelian group of order 40? Check: elements of orders 2 and 5 exist.

2. Explain why the quotient step in the induction proof is legal. Check: every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.

Practice 2 — square-free finite abelian groups [8 marks]

Prompt. Prove that every finite abelian group of square-free order n is cyclic. Question-demand diagnosis. The proof must construct an element of order n. Fastest route. Use Cauchy for each prime factor and combine commuting elements of coprime orders. Topic-specific trap. The product-order argument needs both commutativity and coprime component orders.

Paper-style synthesis: prove an abelian group of square-free order n is cyclic. Check Cauchy gives elements of each prime order; their product has order the product because the group is abelian and orders are coprime, hence order n.

Mistakes

  • Claiming Cauchy gives an element of every divisor order.
  • Forgetting the finite hypothesis.
  • Confusing an element of order p with a subgroup of order p without linking them.
  • Recovery rule: Cauchy guarantees an element of order p, not automatically one of order p^a.
