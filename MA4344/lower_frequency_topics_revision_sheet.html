MA4344 Topic Guide

Lower-Frequency and Current-Scope Topics

Cover the syllabus beyond the recent-paper core without overpredicting it.

Purpose

Maintain a compact safety net for direct products, automorphisms, automata/regular languages and combinatorial actions.Exam use: Named theorem, normality, intersection, product size, action/factors and isomorphism conclusion are justified.

Question plan

  1. Read the definition requested before selecting a theorem.
  2. For a direct product, work coordinatewise.
  3. For automata, state states, alphabet, transitions, start/accept states, then test strings.
  4. For counting under symmetry, identify the action and fixed objects before averaging.
  5. Question interpretation: Identify the exact theorem requested - direct product, semidirect product, composition series or presentation - and do not import stronger classification claims.

Key terms

Direct product, automorphism, alternating group, finite automaton, regular language, Burnside's lemma, wallpaper/Euclidean group. These are current- or older-paper material, but not a strong recent-repeat signal.Rapid recall: Internal direct products need normal subgroups with trivial intersection and product G; semidirect products relax one normality condition; composition factors are simple and Jordan-Holder makes their multiset invariant.

Proof method

For direct products prove statements componentwise. For Burnside, prove that each orbit contributes one to the average number of fixed points. For automata, prove language recognition in both directions: accepted implies the stated pattern, and every stated-pattern word is accepted.Exam workflow: State all structural hypotheses, construct the canonical multiplication map, prove homomorphism/bijection, then interpret the resulting factors.

Worked simple proof

Claim: the map f:G×H→G×H, f(g,h)=(g,h) is an automorphism. It is the identity map, hence preserves multiplication and is bijective.

Practice 1 — semidirect but not direct product in S3 [9 marks]

Prompt. Show S3=A3H for H=⟨(12)⟩ and decide whether this is direct or semidirect. Question-demand diagnosis. Verify orders, intersection and normality separately. Fastest route. Use |A3H|=|A3||H|/|A3∩H|, then test H. Topic-specific trap. One normal factor supports a semidirect product; direct product needs the appropriate commuting/normal structure.

In S3, A3 is normal of order 3 and H=< (12) > has order 2, A3 intersect H={e}, and |A3 H|=3*2=6, so S3=A3 H. Since H is not normal, this is not a direct product; it is A3 semidirect H, isomorphic to Z3 semidirect Z2 with inversion action.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Describe the language accepted by a two-state automaton that toggles state on each a and accepts the start state. Check: words with an even number of a's (assuming no other transitions change state).

2. State Burnside's lemma and list the data needed to count vertex colourings up to a symmetry group. Check: sum fixed colourings over group elements, divide by |G|.

Practice 2 — composition series of S3 [8 marks]

Prompt. Give a composition series for S3 and explain why its factors do not make S3≅Z6. Question-demand diagnosis. Name the normal inclusions and prove both factor groups simple. Fastest route. Use {e}<A3<S3 with factors Z3, Z2. Topic-specific trap. Composition factors determine a multiset, not necessarily the group’s direct-product structure.

Paper-style synthesis: give a composition series for S3. Check {e}<A3<S3 has factors A3/{e}=Z3 and S3/A3=Z2, both simple; explain why the nonabelian group is not their direct product Z6.

Mistakes

  • Treating older-paper occurrence as a forecast.
  • Using Burnside without specifying the action and fixed set.
  • Giving an automaton answer without defining acceptance.
  • Recovery rule: Coprime subgroup orders and full product size do not by themselves give a direct product; both factors must be normal.
