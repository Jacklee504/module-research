Subgroups and Lagrange's Theorem

What repeatedly appears in the papers

Practice question -- matrix subgroup (19 marks)

Inspired by: 2023/24 and 2024/25 Question 1(a,b).

Let xm=[[1+2m,4m],[-m,1-2m]] and S={xm:m∈Z}.

(a) Prove S≤SL(2,Z). [10 marks]

Demand / quickest route: compute determinant and parameter product. Answer: det xm=(1+2m)(1-2m)+4m2=1. Direct multiplication gives xmxn=xm+n; x0=I and xm-1=x-m. Hence S is a subgroup. Trap: prove closure but not membership of SL(2,Z).

(b) Prove S≅Z. [6 marks]

Demand / quickest route: m↦xm. Answer: it is onto by definition, a homomorphism by xm+n=xmxn, and injective since the lower-left entry is -m. Trap: assert different parameters give different matrices without evidence.

(c) State Lagrange's theorem. [3 marks]

Answer: if G is finite and H≤G, then |H| divides |G| and |G|=[G:H]|H|. Trap: omit finite.
