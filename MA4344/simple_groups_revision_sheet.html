MA4344 Topic Guide

Simple Groups and Non-Simplicity

Find a proper nontrivial normal subgroup using Sylow, actions, or the centre.

Purpose

Build short, complete proofs that a finite group cannot be simple.Exam use: Candidate normal subgroup, all Sylow possibilities, intersection/count or action kernel, and proper/nontrivial conclusion are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Choose a normal-subgroup source: unique Sylow subgroup, nontrivial centre, or action kernel.
  2. Prove the subgroup is normal.
  3. Check it is neither {e} nor G.
  4. Finish with the definition of simple.
  5. Question interpretation: To prove non-simple, produce a proper nontrivial normal subgroup via a unique Sylow subgroup, centre, kernel or action; to prove simple, eliminate every possible normal subgroup order.

Key terms

Simple group, proper nontrivial normal subgroup, centre, kernel, conjugation action. A simple group has only {e} and G as normal subgroups.Rapid recall: Simple means only {e} and G are normal (and usually nontrivial). A homomorphism from a simple group has trivial or whole kernel; a nontrivial action can embed it when the kernel is trivial.

Proof method

Start with Sylow counts. If a count is forced to 1, its Sylow subgroup is normal. If all counts evade uniqueness, count elements in distinct Sylow subgroups or use a permutation action to construct a proper kernel.Exam workflow: Assume a normal subgroup, constrain its order by Lagrange/Sylow, and turn its coset or conjugation action into a kernel/order contradiction.

Worked simple proof

Claim: no group of order 21 is simple. n7 divides 3 and is 1 mod 7, so n7=1. The unique order-7 Sylow subgroup is proper, nontrivial and normal.

Practice 1 — Sylow counting excludes simplicity at order 30 [10 marks]

Prompt. Prove that no group of order 30 is simple. Question-demand diagnosis. The non-uniqueness cases must be eliminated by a disjoint element count. Fastest route. List possible n5, n3, count nonidentity elements in distinct Sylow subgroups, and compare with 30. Topic-specific trap. Distinct prime-order Sylow subgroups intersect only in identity; do not double-count it.

No group G of order 30 is simple. Sylow gives n5 divides 6 and n5=1 mod 5, so n5=1 or 6; n3 divides 10 and n3=1 mod 3, so n3=1 or 10. If neither is 1, distinct Sylow subgroups contribute 6*4=24 elements of order 5 and 10*2=20 of order 3, disjoint except identity, exceeding 30. Hence one Sylow subgroup is normal.

Graduated exam bridge

1. Prove a group with nontrivial proper centre is not simple. Check: Z(G) is normal.

2. Show no group of order 45 is simple. Check: use n5 divides 9 and is 1 mod 5.

Practice 2 — order 56 and the Sylow-7 action [9 marks]

Prompt. Investigate simplicity of a group of order 56 by its action on Sylow 7-subgroups. Question-demand diagnosis. If n7=8, the induced action needs a kernel argument; an embedding claim alone is insufficient. Fastest route. First use n7∣8 and n7≡1 mod 7, then analyse the conjugation action. Topic-specific trap. A homomorphism G→S8 does not automatically contradict |G|=56.

Worked answer. Let G have order 56=23·7. Sylow gives n7∣8 and n7≡1 (mod 7), hence n7=1 or 8. If n7=1, the unique Sylow 7-subgroup is a proper nontrivial normal subgroup and G is not simple. Suppose n7=8. Conjugation on the eight Sylow 7-subgroups gives a nontrivial homomorphism φ:G→S8; its kernel is normal. If ker φ is nontrivial, it is proper because the action is nontrivial, so again G is not simple. If ker φ=1, the action is faithful, but that fact alone is not a contradiction. Instead count the Sylow subgroups: distinct subgroups of order 7 intersect only in the identity, so the eight of them contribute 8(7−1)=48 distinct nonidentity elements. There are therefore exactly 56−1−48=7 nonidentity elements left. Let Q be a Sylow 2-subgroup. It has order 8, hence seven nonidentity elements; none can lie in a subgroup of order 7. Thus Q consists of the identity together with exactly those seven remaining elements. Every Sylow 2-subgroup has the same seven nonidentity elements, so Q is unique. Therefore Q is a proper nontrivial normal subgroup. In all cases G is not simple.

Mistakes

  • Finding a subgroup but never proving normality.
  • Calling G itself a witness to non-simplicity.
  • Stopping at a possible Sylow count rather than resolving it.
  • Recovery rule: Counting nonidentity elements from different Sylow p-subgroups is safe because their intersection is trivial; state that reason.

Checklist

  • I can state the definition of simple.
  • I can turn a unique Sylow subgroup into a complete proof.
  • I can verify properness and nontriviality.
