CT420 Revision

Soft Real-time and Multimedia

Judge perceived quality by delay variation, loss and adaptation rather than a single bandwidth number.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Budget capture, encode, queue/network, buffer, decode, render; latency/jitter/loss jointly affect QoS.
  • RTP supplies timestamps/sequence; RTCP supplies reports; buffer smooths jitter at latency cost.
  • Intrinsic QoS is measured; perceived QoS is user experience.

Paper evidence and scope

RTP/RTCP 2017/18; latency/QUIC performance 2023/24–2024/25; adapted practice.

Medium practice — 15 marks

40 encode+80 network+10–90 jitter+30 decode. (a) [5] budget. (b) [5] buffer. (c) [5] RTP/RTCP.

Answers

(a): 150ms+buffer. (b): 90ms buffer≈240ms. (c): RTP/RTCP guide adaptation. Trap: larger buffer always better.

Hard practice — 15 marks

A call has capture 20ms, encode 30ms, network one-way 60ms±25ms, decode/render 25ms. (a) [5] select buffer and calculate playout delay. (b) [5] diagnose 4% late packets with RTP/RTCP. (c) [5] adapt when RTCP shows rising loss.

Answer for every part

(a) Demand / route / worked answer / trap

Route: buffer for worst expected jitter: 25ms. Answer: playout=20+30+60+25+25=160ms; choosing 10ms gives 145ms but late packets when variation exceeds 10. Credit: components 2, buffer choice 2, trade-off 1. Trap: adding ±25 twice.

(b)

Route: compare RTP sequence/timestamp arrival with playout deadline and RTCP jitter/loss reports. Answer: classify missing sequence as loss versus received-after-deadline as late; correlate reported jitter with buffer occupancy. Trap: treating all gaps as network loss.

(c)

Route: reduce offered load before queue grows. Answer: lower bitrate/resolution, choose more robust codec/FEC where justified and increase buffer only if latency budget permits; verify next RTCP interval. Trap: increase bitrate to recover quality during congestion.
