Concurrency and Priority Protocols

Explain blocking precisely, then choose the protocol that bounds or prevents the relevant failure.

Rapid exam refresher

Purpose, scope and examiner demand

Shared resources make a high-priority real-time task wait. Priority protocols make that wait bounded and reduce the risk that unrelated medium-priority work extends it.

Exam focus by part

  • Priority inversion H waits on L while M preempts L.
  • inheritance boosts L temporarily.
  • ceiling prevents unsafe acquisition.
  • cue H/L/M timeline.

Question-demand diagnosis and answer plan

  1. Draw a timeline with high (H), medium (M), and low (L) priority tasks.
  2. Identify the resource owner and the blocked task.
  3. Name priority inversion, deadlock, or unbounded blocking accurately.
  4. Explain inheritance or ceiling step by step, then state the remaining trade-off.

Question-type cues

  • Application cue: Explain inheritance for H waiting on L-held mutex while M ready.
  • Analysis cue: Two locks deadlock under priority system.

Definitions, notation and discriminating comparisons

  • Mutex: exclusive lock; semaphore: synchronisation/counting primitive; critical section: protected code.
  • Priority inversion: H waits for L's lock while M runs ahead of L.
  • Priority inheritance: L temporarily inherits H's priority while blocking H.
  • Priority ceiling: a resource has a ceiling based on potential users; a protocol restricts locking to prevent deadlock and bound blocking.

Step-by-step methods and solution routes

L locks R. H arrives and blocks on R. Without a protocol, M can pre-empt L repeatedly, so H's delay depends on M's work: priority inversion. With inheritance, L inherits H's priority, runs its critical section, releases R, then returns to low priority. H's blocking is now tied to the critical section rather than arbitrary M activity.

Worked examples and method extensions

Question

Explain why priority inheritance helps but does not eliminate all blocking.

Model answer

Inheritance prevents medium-priority tasks from extending the delay while a low-priority lock holder blocks a high-priority task. It boosts the holder until it releases the resource. H can still wait for the holder's critical section, and careful lock design is still needed; nested locks and deadlock require further protocol rules or ordering.

Medium practice — question demand

Explain inheritance for H waiting on L-held mutex while M ready.

Plan / choice of method

Select the governing concepts before calculating: Priority inversion H waits on L while M preempts L.; inheritance boosts L temporarily.; ceiling prevents unsafe acquisition. Apply them only under the assumptions stated in the question, and compare the result with the required condition.

Worked answer

Without protocol M delays L and H; with inheritance L runs at H priority until unlock then drops; account bounded blocking.

Mark-winning points

Credit requires the named mechanism, all intermediate values or protocol states, the final conclusion, and these topic checks: Priority inversion H waits on L while M preempts L.; inheritance boosts L temporarily.; ceiling prevents unsafe acquisition.; cue H/L/M timeline.

Common trap

Ignoring critical-section length in a timing claim.

Graded practice and hard extensions

Prompt 1 - 7 marks

Use H, M and L to illustrate priority inversion and priority inheritance. Exam bridge: timeline (3), definition (1), inherited boost/release (2), bounded-delay conclusion (1). Check: M does not own the resource.

Prompt 2 - 8 marks

Compare priority inheritance and a priority-ceiling protocol. Exam bridge: explain each (4), discuss blocking/deadlock (3), choose for stated system (1). Check: do not claim a ceiling is merely a higher normal task priority.

Hard practice — question demand

Two locks deadlock under priority system.

Plan / choice of method

Model the failure or boundary first, then apply these page-specific concepts in order: Priority inversion H waits on L while M preempts L.; inheritance boosts L temporarily.; ceiling prevents unsafe acquisition. Finish by checking the final state against the deadline, security property, consistency contract, or performance requirement.

Worked answer

Assign ceilings/highest user and enforce system-ceiling acquisition rule to prevent circular wait; inheritance alone is not deadlock prevention.

Mark-winning points

Credit requires the complete method sequence, explicit verification of the conclusion, and the limiting assumption/trade-off—not merely the recommended technology.

Common trap

Ignoring critical-section length in a timing claim.

Topic-specific traps and diagnosis

  • Calling any waiting deadlock; deadlock needs a circular wait/no progress argument.
  • Saying inheritance makes the low task permanently high priority.
  • Ignoring critical-section length in a timing claim.

Exam marking checklist

  • Did I identify owner, waiter, and interfering task?
  • Is the timeline in causal order?
  • Did I state what the protocol bounds/prevents?
