Real-time Foundations

Use this sheet to distinguish a system that is merely fast from one whose correctness includes timing.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Hard deadline: late is failure; firm: late value is zero; soft: late quality degrades.
  • WCET is a safe bound, not mean; include maximum path/loop, cache, I/O, interrupt and pre-emption effects.
  • Latency, jitter and deadline differ; safety-critical describes consequence, not speed.

Medium practice — 15 marks

Braking controller and video call share CPU. (a) [5] classify. (b) [5] metrics. (c) [5] why mean fails.

Answer for every part

(a) Route/answer/trap: classify consequence: braking is hard; video is soft. Trap: “fast” means real-time.

(b): braking needs deadline/WCET/jitter; video end-to-end latency/jitter/loss. Trap: bandwidth alone.

(c): mean hides tail execution and misses. Trap: average proves deadline.

Hard practice — 15 marks

A 10-ms sensor task has a loop of at most 6 iterations. Each iteration has 0.7-ms compute and may make one 0.15-ms cache-miss path; interrupt interference is 0.4 ms. (a) [5] derive a WCET bound. (b) [5] decide schedulability for this task alone. (c) [5] propose a change if a second 4-ms interference source is added.

Answer for every part

(a) Demand / quickest route / worked answer / trap

Route: take maximum iterations and add all bounded overhead: 6(.7+.15)+.4=5.5 ms. Answer: quote 5.5 ms as conditional WCET under stated cache/interrupt bounds (2 calculation, 2 assumptions, 1 conclusion). Trap: using expected iteration count or subtracting cache cost.

(b)

Route: compare WCET to D=10. Answer: 5.5≤10 leaves 4.5 ms slack, so this isolated task meets its deadline; this is not a multi-task guarantee. Trap: calling slack a proof against unmodelled blocking.

(c)

Route: add worst interference then choose bounded mitigation. Answer: 5.5+4=9.5 ms, leaving .5 ms; measure/limit source, split/defer non-critical work, raise scheduling priority only after checking other tasks, or redesign to reduce loop/cache bound. Credit: arithmetic 2, risk statement 1, concrete bounded mitigation 2. Trap: “optimise code” without a new bound.
