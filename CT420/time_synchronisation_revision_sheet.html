CT420 Revision

Time Synchronisation

Calculate offset and delay, then explain the protocol mechanism that makes the result believable.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Offset, skew/rate, accuracy and stability differ; quartz drift accumulates.
  • NTP δ=(t4−t1)−(t3−t2), θ=((t2−t1)+(t3−t4))/2; asymmetric paths bias θ.
  • PTP uses hardware timestamps, BMCA and boundary/transparent clocks; Lamport orders causal events, vectors show concurrency.

Paper evidence and scope

NTP/PTP/ntpq/logical clocks recur in 2017/18, 2018/19, 2021/22, 2022/23 and 2024/25; adapted practice.

Medium practice — 15 marks

t1=10,t2=14,t3=15,t4=21ms. (a) [5] calculate. (b) [5] limitation. (c) [5] ntpq.

Answers

(a): δ=10ms; θ=−1ms. (b): path asymmetry biases θ. (c): reach,stratum,delay,offset,jitter. Trap: low stratum guarantees accuracy.

Hard practice — 15 marks

PTP master sends Sync at T1=100.000000 ms; slave receives at T2=100.120000. Slave sends DelayReq at T3=101.000000; master receives at T4=101.080000. Assume symmetric path. (a) [5] calculate mean delay/offset. (b) [5] show transparent-clock use if switch residence is 20 µs. (c) [5] specify failover.

Answer for every part

(a) Demand / route / worked answer / trap

Route: forward=120µs, reverse=80µs; mean=(120+80)/2=100µs; offset=forward−mean=+20µs, so slave is 20µs behind master. Trap: adding timestamps with inconsistent units.

(b)

Route: correct residence before path calculation. Answer: transparent clock places 20µs correction field in Sync; subtract it from measured forward residence so queueing does not look like propagation delay. Credit: correction 2, recalculation principle 2, precision reason 1. Trap: calling transparent clock a new grandmaster.

(c)

Route: name election and holdover policy. Answer: configure redundant grandmasters/BMCA; on Announce timeout BMCA selects best clock, slave enters bounded holdover then slews to new master. Trap: stepping time abruptly in a deadline-critical system.
