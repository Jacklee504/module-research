MA490 Topic Guide

Sigma-algebras and Set Operations

Turn closure axioms into reliable set calculations and proof steps.

Purpose

Recognise sigma-algebras, generated sigma-algebras, and the liminf/limsup of a sequence of sets.Exam use: Underlying space, complements, countable operations, atoms and smallest-family argument are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Write the universe and the proposed family clearly.
  2. Check empty set, complements, then countable unions.
  3. For generated families, list the smallest forced closures.
  4. For liminf/limsup, translate “eventually” and “infinitely often” into unions and intersections.
  5. Question interpretation: For verify a sigma-algebra, test complement and countable union closure; for generated sigma-algebra, construct the smallest closed family rather than merely listing the generators.

Definitions

A sigma-algebra F on Ω contains Ω, is closed under complements, and under countable unions. liminf A_n = union_n intersection_{k≥n} A_k; limsup A_n = intersection_n union_{k≥n} A_k.Rapid recall: A sigma-algebra contains empty/Omega and is closed under complements and countable unions, hence countable intersections and differences; sigma(G) is the intersection of all sigma-algebras containing G.

Proof method

When proving closure under a derived operation, reduce it to the axioms: intersections use De Morgan, differences use intersection with a complement. For equality of set expressions, prove each inclusion using membership language.Exam workflow: Exploit atoms of a finite generator: every measurable set is a union of atoms, which gives both completeness and minimality.

Worked simple example

If A_n=(0,1/n), then no x>0 belongs eventually, while 0 is excluded throughout. Hence both liminf A_n and limsup A_n are empty.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Let Omega={1,2,3,4} and G={{1,2},{2,3}}. Intersections with complements yield atoms {2}, {1}, {3}, {4}. Therefore sigma(G) is every union of these four singleton atoms, namely the full power set with 16 sets. Showing the atoms are singletons proves both inclusion of all subsets and minimality.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Basic: Is {∅, Ω, A, Ac} a sigma-algebra? Check: yes, it is closed under complements and all unions stay in the family.

2. Exam: Prove liminf A_n is contained in limsup A_n. Check: “eventually in” implies “in infinitely many”.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: if G={A} with empty !=A!=Omega, find sigma(G). Check it is {empty,A,A^c,Omega}; verify complement and arbitrary/countable unions stay in the four-set family.

Mistakes

  • Checking only finite unions.
  • Reversing liminf and limsup.
  • Calling an arbitrary collection generated without closing under complements.
  • Recovery rule: Closure under finite unions alone defines an algebra, not necessarily a sigma-algebra on an infinite space.

Checklist

  • I can state all three sigma-algebra axioms.
  • I can use De Morgan in a closure proof.
  • I can read liminf and limsup in words.
  • Final 60-second audit: Underlying space, complements, countable operations, atoms and smallest-family argument are explicit.
