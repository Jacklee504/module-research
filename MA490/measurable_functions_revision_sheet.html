MA490 Topic Guide

Measurable Functions

Prove measurability through preimages and closure, not intuition.

Purpose

Test measurability and prove closure under algebraic operations, compositions and pointwise limits.Exam use: Chosen ray convention, all threshold cases/preimage identity, countable closure and extended-value caveat are explicit.

Question plan

  1. State the measurable-space domains and codomain.
  2. Use inverse images of Borel sets, or the threshold test {f>a}.
  3. Reduce a new function to known measurable pieces.
  4. For limits, express the relevant threshold set using countable unions/intersections.
  5. Question interpretation: For measurability, use preimages of generating Borel rays or closure under limits/algebra; for a constructed function, identify each measurable level set.

Definitions

f:(X,F)→(R,B(R)) is measurable when f−1(B)∈F for every Borel B. Equivalent threshold tests use {f>a}∈F for every real a.Rapid recall: f is measurable iff {f>a} is measurable for every real a (equivalently other ray conventions); sums, products and pointwise limits of measurable real functions are measurable under the usual extended-value qualifications.

Proof method

For products, first establish f+g and f−g are measurable, then use fg=((f+g)^2−(f−g)^2)/4. For pointwise limits, use rational thresholds to express {lim fn>a} measurably.Exam workflow: Choose the simplest generating family, calculate the preimage with all threshold cases, and invoke sigma-algebra closure explicitly.

Worked simple example

If f=1A with A∈F, then f is measurable because inverse images of Borel sets are one of ∅, A, Ac, X.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Let A be measurable and f=2 1_A-1. For a<-1, {f>a}=X; for -1<=a<1, it is A; for a>=1, it is empty. Every case is measurable, so f is measurable. This proof also shows why measurability of A is exactly what the step function needs.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Basic: Why is f² measurable when f is? Check: square is continuous, so compose it with f.

2. Exam: Prove max(f,g) is measurable. Check: max(f,g)=(f+g+|f−g|)/2.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: if f_n are measurable, prove g=sup_n f_n is measurable. Check {g>a}=union_n {f_n>a}, a countable union of measurable sets. State why countability matters.

Mistakes

  • Proving only that preimages of intervals are measurable without saying why that suffices.
  • Assuming every pointwise limit is continuous.
  • Using a non-measurable set in an indicator function.
  • Recovery rule: Preimages commute with unions and complements, but images generally do not; measurability proofs should work backwards through the function.

Checklist

  • I can state the inverse-image definition.
  • I know two closure routes for products/maxima.
  • I can distinguish measurability from continuity.
  • Final 60-second audit: Chosen ray convention, all threshold cases/preimage identity, countable closure and extended-value caveat are explicit.
