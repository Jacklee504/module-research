# MA490 - Measure Theory

## High-Level View

- Status: Optional.
- Assessment: 70% written assessment, 30% continuous assessment.
- Credits: 5
- Semester: Semester 1
- Current module page validity: Valid from 2025 onwards.
- Current teachers/administrators: James Cruickshank, Collette McLoughlin.
- Past papers reviewed: 2018/19, 2019/20, 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24.
- Predictability: High for structure and recurring topic types, but the module is proof-heavy and conceptually demanding.

## Recommendation

MA490 gives Semester 1 another researched option, but it is not a CA-maximising module. It is 70% exam / 30% CA and the subject matter is abstract real analysis: sigma-algebras, measures, measurable functions, integrals, convergence theorems, Borel-Cantelli and Cantor-set style questions.

The exam is very predictable in structure. The risk is not surprise; the risk is whether measure theory proofs are comfortable enough under exam pressure. It is probably a better pick than an unresearched or disliked module if you like analysis, but it is not as strategically attractive as the 40% CA Semester 2 options.

## Predictability Judgment

Predictability is high.

The most stable structure is:

- Q1: answer five short questions, usually 40%, from a menu of ten. These repeat the same broad skills: generated sigma-algebras, liminf/limsup of sets, Lebesgue measure computations, Bernoulli/share-price space events, Cantor set membership, measurability checks, pointwise/uniform/almost-uniform convergence, and term-by-term integration.
- Q2: definitions and measure-space theorem proof, often sigma-algebra/measure definitions, subadditivity, continuity from below/above, Borel-Cantelli, or related event/probability arguments.
- Q3: measurable functions, closure under operations, pointwise limits, square/product measurability, or counterexamples.
- Q4: integration definitions, zero integral iff zero almost everywhere, monotone convergence / convergence theorem applications, and simple/characteristic function arguments.

The 2022/23 and 2023/24 papers are especially close. The older Graham Ellis/Martin Kerin papers also use the same skeleton, though with more share-price/Bernoulli-space examples.

## Past Paper Pattern

- The paper is very stable: Q1 is usually a broad short-question menu, followed by longer questions on definitions/proofs, measurable functions and integration/convergence.
- Q1 high-return topics: generated sigma-algebras, liminf/limsup, Bernoulli events, Cantor-set/ternary examples, Borel measurability, pointwise vs uniform convergence and term-by-term integration.
- Q2 usually targets measure foundations: sigma-algebras, measures/probability, Borel sets, subadditivity, continuity from above/below and Borel-Cantelli.
- Q3 usually targets measurable functions: closure properties, products/squares/compositions, limits and counterexamples involving non-measurable functions.
- Q4 usually targets integration: simple/non-negative integral definitions, zero integral iff zero a.e., characteristic functions, additivity and convergence theorems.
- Best revision strategy: drill definitions and standard proof templates first, then practise the recurring short-question calculations.

## Study Strategy

- Prepare exact definitions: algebra, sigma-algebra, Borel sigma-algebra, measure, probability space, measurable function, simple function, non-negative integral and integrability.
- Build proof templates for continuity from below/above, subadditivity, closure of measurable functions under operations, pointwise limits of measurable functions, zero integral iff zero a.e., and Monotone Convergence.
- Practise Q1-style short questions heavily. The format gives choice, so this is where predictability can translate into marks.
- Keep Bernoulli/share-price probability space and Cantor-set ternary expansion questions on the revision list, even though they are not the whole module.

## Official Module Information

Source: https://www.universityofgalway.ie/course-information/module/MA490

- Title: Measure Theory
- Description: measures as a way to assign size to suitable subsets, generalising length, area and volume; Lebesgue measure; integration; probability-theory foundations.
- Learning outcomes include:
  - Carry out basic operations on sequences of sets.
  - Decide whether a set function is a measure.
  - Apply integration theory in real-number and probability-space settings.
  - Decide when term-by-term integration of sequences or series is permissible.
  - Write proofs about measures and integrals.

## Short Learning Material

Use Degreed/Percipio-style catalogues for short tutorials, guided projects, or video chapters. Prefer material under 3 hours per topic.

- Measure theory basics: sigma-algebras, generated sigma-algebras, measures and measurable spaces.
- Lebesgue measure and Borel sets: intervals, Cantor-set intuition and common examples.
- Measurable functions: preimage definitions, closure properties and pointwise limits.
- Integration theory intro: simple functions, non-negative integrals and convergence theorems.
- Probability foundations: events, limsup/liminf, Borel-Cantelli and Bernoulli spaces.
- Real analysis proof refresher: epsilon-style proofs, sequences of functions and counterexamples.
