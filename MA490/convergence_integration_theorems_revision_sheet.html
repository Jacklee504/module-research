MA490 Topic Guide

Convergence and Integration Theorems

Match the theorem to its hypotheses before exchanging limit and integral.

Purpose

Use Monotone Convergence and related term-by-term integration arguments without skipping hypotheses.Exam use: Limit, exceptional set, theorem name, every hypothesis, conclusion direction and direct integral check appear.

Question plan

  1. Write the proposed limiting function f.
  2. Check measurability and non-negativity.
  3. Decide whether fn increases pointwise, or whether another theorem is required.
  4. State the theorem, then substitute the data into its conclusion.
  5. Question interpretation: Before interchanging limit and integral, name MCT, Fatou or DCT and verify each hypothesis against the actual sequence.

Definitions

Monotone Convergence Theorem: if 0≤fn↑f pointwise and each fn is measurable, then ∫fndμ↑∫f dμ. “↑” means nondecreasing with pointwise limit f.Rapid recall: MCT needs 0<=f_n increasing; Fatou gives integral liminf <= liminf integrals for nonnegative functions; DCT needs a.e. convergence and an integrable dominating g.

Proof method

For an application, do not re-prove the theorem: verify each hypothesis in a separate sentence. For a series, use partial sums as the increasing sequence when all summands are non-negative.Exam workflow: Find the pointwise/a.e. limit, test measurability and sign/monotonicity/domination, state the theorem in the needed direction, then compute both sides.

Worked simple example

On [0,1], fn(x)=min(nx,1) increases to 1 except at 0, and ∫fn→∫1=1. The theorem legitimises the limit calculation.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

On (0,1), let f_n(x)=n 1_(0,1/n)(x). Then f_n->0 a.e. but integrals equal 1. DCT cannot use a common integrable dominator: any g dominating all f_n satisfies g(x)>=n when x<1/n, forcing severe growth near 0; indeed the unchanged integrals contradict the DCT conclusion. Fatou only yields 0<=1, which is valid.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Basic: Can MCT apply to fn(x)=1/n? Check: not as an increasing sequence; it decreases.

2. Exam: For non-negative measurable gk, justify ∫Σgk=Σ∫gk. Check: apply MCT to partial sums.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: let f_n(x)=x^n on [0,1]. Check f_n->0 a.e. (the point x=1 is exceptional), |f_n|<=1 with 1 integrable, so DCT gives integral f_n ->0; directly integral=1/(n+1).

Mistakes

  • Applying MCT to a decreasing or signed sequence.
  • Writing “by convergence theorem” without naming assumptions.
  • Interchanging sum and integral before establishing non-negativity/dominance.
  • Recovery rule: A bound depending on n is not a DCT dominator; one integrable g must control every f_n almost everywhere.

Checklist

  • I can state MCT exactly.
  • I can convert a series to partial sums.
  • I check monotonicity, measurability and non-negativity.
  • Final 60-second audit: Limit, exceptional set, theorem name, every hypothesis, conclusion direction and direct integral check appear.
