# MA3491 - Fields and Applications

## High-Level View

- Predictability: High
- Priority: Strong option if comfortable with finite fields, polynomial arithmetic, matrices, and coding theory.
- Assessment: 60% written assessment, 40% continuous assessment.
- Semester: Semester 2.
- Current module page validity: Valid from 2025 onwards.
- Current teachers/administrators: Mary Kelly, Collette McLoughlin, Rachel Quinlan, Emil Skoldberg.
- Lecturer/staff-change risk: Low. Emil Skoldberg is the starred lecturer on the recent papers and is also listed on the current module page. Niall Madden appears on recent papers as an unstarred internal examiner, so he should not be treated as the main lecturer-change risk.

## Recommendation

This is one of the better maths-heavy options from a preparation strategy point of view. It has a 40% CA component, which is better than the 30% CA split in MA4102, CS402, CT561, CT420 and CT4100. The exam is also very template-driven: bounds, finite field arithmetic, linear codes, Hamming codes, syndrome decoding and cyclic codes recur heavily.

The module is not quantum computing, but it is strategically linked to MA4102 and CS402 through finite fields, error-correcting codes, and algebraic coding theory. It is probably the strongest maths-side overlap with quantum/security topics among the Year 4 maths options checked so far.

## Exam Format

Stable in content, with a format shift after the older papers:

- Semester 2 exam.
- 2 hours.
- Students answer all questions.
- 2018-2019 and 2021-2022: 4 questions.
- 2022-2023 to 2025-2026: 5 questions.
- Questions are broken into many small calculation/proof parts.

The change from 4 to 5 questions does not reduce predictability because the topic blocks remain very stable.

## Repeated Question Patterns

- The paper is highly repeatable: most years cover bounds, finite fields, linear codes, decoding, Hamming codes and cyclic codes.
- Start with code bounds and block-code basics: sphere packing, Singleton, minimum distance, perfect/MDS codes and structured codeword counts.
- Finite fields are central: irreducibility, factorisation, constructing fields such as `F8`/`F16`/`F25`/`F27`, field multiplication, inverses and primitive elements.
- Linear-code calculations are high-return: generator matrices, standard form, parity-check matrices, minimum distance and codeword membership.
- Decoding is very drillable: syndrome tables, standard arrays and nearest-neighbour decoding.
- Hamming and cyclic codes are among the safest topics: parity-check construction, single-error correction, generator/check polynomials and cyclic-shift arguments.
- Weight enumerators appeared in 2022/23, 2023/24 and 2025/26. In 2025/26 they were paired with a cyclic-code question and MacWilliams' identity, so they are now a recurring secondary topic.

## Study Strategy

Highest-return preparation:

1. Memorise sphere packing and Singleton bound templates.
2. Practise irreducibility, polynomial reduction, multiplication and inverses in finite fields.
3. Drill generator matrices, standard form, parity-check matrices and minimum distance.
4. Practise syndrome tables and decoding.
5. Learn Hamming code construction and single-error correction cold.
6. Practise cyclic-code generator/check polynomial questions, including the link between a cyclic code and its dual where asked.
7. Prepare perfect/MDS/self-dual definitions and short proofs; add the weight-enumerator definition and MacWilliams' identity after the core skills.

This module should reward past-paper drilling heavily. It is a strong candidate if the CA is manageable and the algebra style suits you.
