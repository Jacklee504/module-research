Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Products, inverses and orders in constructed fields are Q2 staples in 2021/22-2024/25.

(a) In F2[alpha]/(alpha^3+alpha+1), find alpha^-1. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: From alpha3=alpha+1, divide the relation by alpha: alpha2+1+alpha^-1=0.

Mark-aware answer: Over F2, alpha^-1=alpha2+1. Check alpha(alpha2+1)=alpha3+alpha=(alpha+1)+alpha=1.

Trap: Do not reduce using an unproved or wrong sign convention.

(b) Find an inverse using the extended Euclidean algorithm in Fp[x]. [6 marks]

Question demand: Compute gcd(a,f)=1 and back-substitute ua+vf=1. Quickest route: Compute gcd(a,f)=1 and back-substitute ua+vf=1.

Mark-aware answer: If gcd(a(x),f(x))=1, Euclid gives u(x)a(x)+v(x)f(x)=1. Reducing modulo f leaves u(x)a(x)=1, so the residue of u is a-1. Reduce u to degree less than deg f.

Trap: Fermat-style exponentiation works only for a nonzero element of the actual field.

(c) Find the order of a nonzero element in F16. [4 marks]

Question demand: Use that the order divides 15 and test divisors 1,3,5,15. Quickest route: Use that the order divides 15 and test divisors 1,3,5,15.

Mark-aware answer: The multiplicative group F16* has order 15, so ord(a) divides 15. Compute a, a3 and a5; the least positive exponent giving 1 is the order.

Trap: Do not claim every nonzero element is primitive.
