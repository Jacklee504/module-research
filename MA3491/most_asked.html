1. Bounds And Parameters Of A Described Block Code

Sphere-packing bound

Recurring wording and marks:

  • 2022/23 Q1(a), 6 marks: “State the sphere packing bound, and determine the upper bound it gives” for a 4-ary code of length 8 and minimum distance 3.
  • 2023/24 Q1(a), 6 marks: the same task for a ternary code of length 8 and minimum distance 3.
  • 2024/25 Q1(a)(i)–(ii), 2 + 1 marks: state it, then apply it to binary length 10, minimum distance 5.
  • 2025/26 Q1(a)(i)–(ii), 2 + 4 marks: state it, then apply it to binary length 12, minimum distance 5.

Complete answer must contain: for a q-ary code C of length n and minimum distance d, set t = ⌊(d − 1)/2⌋ and state

|C| Σi=0t C(n, i)(q − 1)i ≤ qn.

Then substitute q, n, and t, divide by the sphere size, and take the integer upper bound. “Corrects all single errors” means t = 1, equivalently d ≥ 3.

Singleton bound, including its proof

  • 2022/23 Q1(b), 6 marks: state the bound and apply it to a ternary length-8 code of minimum distance 3.
  • 2023/24 Q1(b), 6 marks: the same task for a 4-ary length-8 code of minimum distance 3.
  • 2024/25 Q1(b)(i)–(iii), 2 + 4 + 1 marks: state, prove, then apply to ternary length 9 and distance 3.
  • 2025/26 Q1(b)(i)–(iii), 2 + 4 + 2 marks: state, prove, then apply to binary length 12 and distance 5.

Complete answer must contain: |C| ≤ qn−d+1. For the proof, delete any d − 1 coordinate positions. If two codewords acquired the same remaining word, they could differ in at most d − 1 positions, contradicting minimum distance d. Deletion is injective into qn−d+1 words, giving the bound.

Structured code: count, distance, parameters, and classification

  • 2022/23 Q1(c)(i)–(ii), 4 + 4 marks: binary length-8 words with equally many 0s and 1s, find |C| and dmin.
  • 2023/24 Q1(c)(i)–(ii), 4 + 4 marks: palindromes P2k,q and P2k+1,q, find number of words and minimum distance separately for even and odd length.
  • 2024/25 Q1(c)(i)–(iii), 2 + 2 + 2 marks: ternary 7-ply repetition code, find parameters, prove it is not perfect, and prove it is MDS.
  • 2025/26 Q1(c), 6 marks: words over {X, Y} of length 8 where X-count in positions 1–4 equals Y-count in positions 5–8, determine the parameters.

Standard calculation patterns: equal 0/1 counts give |C| = C(8,4) and dmin = 2. For P2k,q, choose the first k symbols, so |P| = qk and dmin = 2; for P2k+1,q, choose k mirrored pairs and the middle symbol, so |P| = qk+1 and dmin = 1. For the 2025/26 condition, choose the X-count i and positions on both halves: Σi=04C(4,i)² = C(8,4) = 70; a paired swap gives distance 2. A q-ary repetition code has [n, 1, n], and is MDS because |C| = qn−d+1.

2. Finite Fields And Polynomial Arithmetic

Irreducibility and factorisation

  • 2022/23 Q2(a)(i)–(ii), 3 + 3 marks: decide whether two cubics in F5[x] are irreducible and factor reducible ones.
  • 2023/24 Q2(a), 6 marks: factor x6 − 1 in F3[x] into irreducibles.
  • 2024/25 Q2(a), 4 marks: show x2 + x + 1 ∈ F5[x] is irreducible.
  • 2025/26 Q2(a)–(b), 3 + 5 marks: find all irreducible degree-2 polynomials in F2[x], then show x5 + x2 + 1 is irreducible.

Standard proof strategy: a degree-2 or degree-3 polynomial over a field is irreducible exactly when it has no root in that field, so test every field element. For a degree-5 polynomial over F2, rule out a linear factor and the irreducible quadratic x2 + x + 1; otherwise any proper factorisation would have a factor of degree 1 or 2. For factorisation, first find roots, divide, and continue until every factor is irreducible.

Quotient-field arithmetic and multiplicative inverses

  • 2022/23 Q2(b)(i)–(iii), 3 + 5 + 6 marks: establish F27 = F3[x]/(f), multiply two residue classes, and invert x2.
  • 2023/24 Q2(b)(i)–(iii), 3 + 5 + 6 marks: establish F16, find the order of x2 + x, then invert x2.
  • 2024/25 Q2(b), 6 marks: invert x + 3 in F25 = F5[x]/(p), with representative degree ≤ 1.
  • 2025/26 Q2(c), 6 marks: invert x2 + 1 in F32 = F2[x]/(p), with representative degree ≤ 4.

Complete method: write elements as residue classes of polynomials of degree below deg p. Multiply normally and replace powers using p(x) = 0. For an inverse of a, use the extended Euclidean algorithm to find u, v with ua + vp = 1; then [u] is [a]−1. A coefficient-comparison solve is also valid if the final product is checked modulo p.

Field-theory proof variants

  • 2023/24 Q2(b)(ii), 5 marks: find the multiplicative order of a specified nonzero field element.
  • 2024/25 Q2(c)(i)–(iii), 2 + 4 + 4 marks: define a primitive element; prove (a−1)n = (an)−1; prove the inverse of a primitive element is primitive.
  • 2024/25 Q1(d)(i)–(ii), 4 marks: prove {1, a} is an F2-basis of F4, then restrict scalars of an F4-basis.
  • 2025/26 Q2(d), 6 marks: for a field of characteristic p, prove (a + b)p = ap + bp.

Proof ingredients: F× is a finite group of order |F| − 1; an element is primitive when its order is |F| − 1, and a and a−1 have the same order. To restrict scalars from F4 to F2, expand each coefficient uniquely in the F2-basis {1, a} and prove spanning and independence. In characteristic p, expand by the binomial theorem; every C(p,j) with 0 < j < p is divisible by p, hence is zero in the field.

3. Linear Codes, Minimum Distance, And Decoding

Generator matrix → standard form → check matrix → distance / self-duality

  • 2022/23 Q3(a)(i)–(iii), 4 + 4 + 4 marks: list ternary codewords, find minimum distance, then nearest-neighbour decode.
  • 2022/23 Q3(b)(i)–(ii), 4 + 4 marks: construct a check matrix and a syndrome table, then decode 1111.
  • 2023/24 Q3(a)(i)–(iii), 4 + 2 + 6 marks: list codewords, minimum distance, standard array, and decode two words; Q3(b), 2 + 6 marks, repeats check matrix and syndrome decoding.
  • 2024/25 Q3(a)–(e)(ii), 4 + 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 + 4 marks: standard form, check matrix, distance, self-duality, syndrome table, and decode 1111 and 1122.
  • 2025/26 Q3(a)–(e)(ii), the same 4 + 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 + 4 structure, with decoded words 1111 and 1212.

Construction and checks: row-reduce G over the stated field to G = [Ik | A]. Then a parity-check matrix is H = [−AT | In−k], with GHT = 0. A row vector v is a codeword exactly when vHT = 0. For a linear code, dmin is the least nonzero codeword weight; enumerate the small code or use the smallest number of linearly dependent columns of H. To prove C = C⊥, show self-orthogonality (for example GGT = 0) and use dim C = dim C⊥ = n/2.

Syndrome, nearest-neighbour, and standard-array decoding

  • 2022/23 Q3(a)(iii), 4 marks: “What is the result of nearest-neighbour decoding” of a received word?
  • 2023/24 Q3(a)(iii), 6 marks: construct a standard array and decode two received words.
  • 2022/23–2025/26 Q3(b) or Q3(e), 4–8 marks: construct a syndrome table and use it to decode named received words.

Complete decoding answer: compute the syndrome s = rHT. The table maps each syndrome to a selected minimum-weight coset leader e; return r − e. In a nonbinary code, the syndrome identifies both a column of H and its nonzero scalar multiple, so record the error magnitude as well as its position. In a standard array, locate r in the row headed by its coset leader and decode to the codeword at the top of that column. Nearest-neighbour wording asks for the codeword(s) at minimum Hamming distance; explain a tie if one exists.

4. Hamming Codes

This is a true single sequence, not three unrelated topics. Every supplied paper asks definition, parity-check matrix, and single-error decoding together. The 2024/25 and 2025/26 papers append parameters and a perfection proof.

  • 2022/23 Q4(b)(i)–(iii), 4 + 4 + 6 marks: define Ham(r,q), construct H for Ham(2,7), correct one error, and decode a word.
  • 2023/24 Q4(b)(i)–(iii), 4 + 4 + 6 marks: the same sequence for Ham(2,4), decoding two words.
  • 2024/25 Q4(a)–(e), 2 + 4 + 6 + 2 + 6 marks: definition, Ham(3,2) matrix, correction/decode, parameters, and perfection.
  • 2025/26 Q4(a)–(e), 2 + 4 + 6 + 2 + 6 marks: the same full sequence for Ham(2,4).

Complete answer must contain: Ham(r,q) = {c ∈ Fqn : cHT = 0}, where H is an r × n parity-check matrix whose columns are one representative from each one-dimensional subspace of Fqr, and n = (qr − 1)/(q − 1). Its parameters are [n, n − r, 3]. For a single error aej, the syndrome is ahj, so identify the column and scalar, subtract the error, and give the corrected word. For perfection, radius-1 balls are disjoint because d = 3 and have size 1 + n(q − 1) = qr; multiplying by |C| = qn−r gives qn, all ambient words.

5. Cyclic Codes, Weight Enumerators, And MacWilliams’ Identity

Cyclic generator/check workflow

  • 2022/23 Q5(b)(i)–(ii), 4 + 8 marks: over F5, construct a generator matrix, then find the check polynomial and construct a check matrix.
  • 2023/24 Q5(b)(i)–(ii), 4 + 8 marks: the same workflow over F3.
  • 2024/25 Q5(a)(i)–(iii), 4 + 8 + 4 marks: the workflow over F4, then show the code is MDS.
  • 2025/26 Q5(a)–(b), 4 + 4 marks: the binary workflow for length 6 and g(x) = x2 + x + 1.

Complete construction: view a length-n cyclic code as an ideal in Fq[x]/(xn − 1), generated by a monic divisor g(x) of xn − 1. If deg g = n − k, rows from the coefficient vectors of g, xg, …, xk−1g give G. Compute h(x) = (xn − 1)/g(x). A standard check matrix is a generator matrix for C⊥: use the reciprocal h*(x) = h(0)−1xdeg hh(x−1) as its generator polynomial; rows are its n − k shifts h*, xh*, …, xn−k−1h*. Verify GHT = 0.

Distinct 2024/25 extension, Q5(b), 4 marks: for a binary cyclic code of odd length containing an odd-weight word u, prove it contains the all-ones word. Sum all cyclic shifts of u: each coordinate is the sum of wt(u) ones, hence is 1 in F2.

Weight enumerators and the 2025/26 dual-code extension

  • 2022/23 Q5(a)(i)–(ii), 2 + 6 marks: define the weight enumerator of a linear [n,k]-code and compute it from a supplied binary generator matrix.
  • 2023/24 Q5(a)(i)–(ii), 2 + 6 marks: the same definition and computation for a ternary code.
  • 2025/26 Q5(c)–(f), 2 + 4 + 4 + 2 marks: define it; compute WC⊥; state MacWilliams’ identity and obtain the two lowest-degree terms of WC; then determine dmin.

Complete answer: the one-variable weight enumerator is WC(z) = Σc∈Czwt(c); count all codewords, including the constant term 1. In binary form, MacWilliams’ identity is WC⊥(z) = |C|−1(1 + z)nWC((1 − z)/(1 + z)). Rearrange or apply the identity in the requested direction, expand only as far as required, and read dmin as the smallest positive exponent with nonzero coefficient.
