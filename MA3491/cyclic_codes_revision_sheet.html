MA3491 Topic Guide

Cyclic Codes and Generator Polynomials

Represent length-n words modulo xn−1: multiplying by x is a cyclic shift.

Purpose

Cyclic codes convert shift closure into polynomial divisibility and give efficient encoders.Exam use: Field, modulus x^n-1, divisor test, dimension, coefficient order and final remainder/codeword are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Factor xn−1 over Fq.
  2. Choose a monic divisor g(x); calculate h(x)=(xn−1)/g(x).
  3. Use k=n−deg g and encode c(x)=m(x)g(x).
  4. Check divisibility or use h for parity/check reasoning.
  5. Question interpretation: For cyclic, move between word and polynomial representations; for generate, verify g divides x^n-1 and reduce multiples modulo the stated field.

Formulas

C is cyclic iff its word polynomials form an ideal ⟨g(x)⟩ in Fq[x]/(xn−1), where g|xn−1. Parameters: [n,k] with k=n−deg g; g(x)h(x)=xn−1.Rapid recall: A length-n cyclic code is an ideal <g(x)> in F_q[x]/(x^n-1), where monic g divides x^n-1; dimension k=n-deg g and h=(x^n-1)/g is the check polynomial.

Derivation

A cyclic shift is x c(x) reduced modulo xn−1. Every ideal in Fq[x] is principal, so the code has a unique monic generator divisor. Messages of degree below k give distinct multiples m g.Exam workflow: Fix coefficient order, factor x^n-1 over the field, multiply message polynomials of degree below k by g, and check a cyclic shift remains divisible by g.

Worked example

Over F2, x3−1=x3+1=(x+1)(x2+x+1). Choose g=x+1. Then k=2 and c=(1+x)(1+x)=1+x2, the word 101; h=x2+x+1.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Over F_2, n=7 and g(x)=x^3+x+1 gives k=4. For m(x)=x^3+x, c=m g=(x^3+x)(x^3+x+1)=x^6+x^3+x^2+x (middle x^4 terms cancel). Coefficient vector from x^0 to x^6 is (0,1,1,1,0,0,1). Multiplying by x modulo x^7-1 gives 1+x^2+x^3+x^4, another multiple modulo the quotient, so the shift stays in C.

Graduated exam bridge

1. For the example, find k for g=x2+x+1. Check: k=1.

2. Factor x7−1 over F2, choose a divisor g, state [n,k], and construct two codewords. Check: show g divides the modulus and keep polynomial coefficients in F2.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: decide whether r(x)=x^6+x^4+x^3+x is in this code. Check division by g over F_2: subtract x^3g=x^6+x^4+x^3, leaving remainder x. The remainder is nonzero, so r is not a codeword; the subtraction is coefficient-wise XOR.

Mistakes

  • Using any polynomial rather than a divisor of xn−1.
  • Reversing deg g and k.
  • Forgetting reduction modulo xn−1 for shifts.
  • Recovery rule: Coefficient order is a convention, but changing it midway changes the reported word even if the polynomial calculation is right.

Checklist

  • I can factor the modulus.
  • I can calculate h and k.
  • I can encode by multiplying m and g.
  • Final 60-second audit: Field, modulus x^n-1, divisor test, dimension, coefficient order and final remainder/codeword are explicit.
