CT4108 Revision

Concurrency and Mutual Exclusion

Protect shared resources while explaining correctness and progress, not just naming a lock.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Critical section needs mutual exclusion/progress/bounded waiting.
  • race is interleaving.
  • deadlock conditions are fourfold.
  • lock discipline is global.

Purpose, scope and examiner demand

Concurrency allows multiple activities to overlap. Mutual exclusion makes a critical section accessible to at most one participant at a time, avoiding unsafe interleavings.

Exam focus by part

  • Critical section needs mutual exclusion/progress/bounded waiting.
  • race is interleaving.
  • deadlock conditions are fourfold.
  • lock discipline is global.

Question-demand diagnosis and answer plan

  1. Identify the shared state and critical section.
  2. Show the race interleaving before protection.
  3. Choose a lock, message-based permission or atomic primitive.
  4. Evaluate mutual exclusion, progress, fairness and failure handling.

Question-type cues

  • Application cue: Two threads increment shared x.
  • Analysis cue: Two resources deadlock under opposite ordering.

Definitions, notation and discriminating comparisons

  • Critical section: code that reads/writes shared resource.
  • Race condition: result depends on timing of unsynchronised actions.
  • Mutual exclusion: never two holders at once.
  • Deadlock: participants wait indefinitely in a cycle.
  • Starvation: a participant is repeatedly denied progress.

Step-by-step methods and solution routes

Write the unsafe read-modify-write as separate steps. Exhibit two actors both reading the old value. Then place acquire before and release after the whole operation. In a distributed design, add a permission owner or timestamped request and state what happens if the owner crashes.

Reasoning checkpoints

  1. Use and justify: Critical section needs mutual exclusion/progress/bounded waiting.
  2. Use and justify: race is interleaving.
  3. Use and justify: deadlock conditions are fourfold.
  4. Use and justify: lock discipline is global.

Worked examples and method extensions

Balance=10. Two withdrawals each read 10 and write 7; the final balance is 7 though £6 was withdrawn. A mutex makes one withdrawal complete before the other reads. In a distributed service, the lock manager must not grant two leases and needs expiry/recovery rules.

Exam-paper-led practice

Practice question 1 [medium/hard]

Scenario and demand: Two threads execute x=x+1 when x=7. Show a losing interleaving and repair it while stating the required critical-section properties. [8 marks]

Question-demand diagnosis and fastest route: Each increment reads, computes and writes. T1 reads 7; T2 reads 7; both compute 8; T1 writes 8; T2 writes 8: one increment is lost. Put the read-modify-write inside one mutex (or use an atomic fetch-add), so mutual exclusion holds. The mechanism must also allow progress when no thread is inside and bounded waiting so one contender is not postponed indefinitely. Trap: declaring x volatile does not make the increment atomic.

Practice question 2 [medium/hard]

Scenario and demand: T1 locks A then waits for B; T2 locks B then waits for A. Diagnose the deadlock and give a design-level cure. [8 marks]

Question-demand diagnosis and fastest route: All four Coffman conditions hold: mutual exclusion, hold-and-wait, no pre-emption and circular wait. Establish one global order, e.g. always acquire A before B; then T2 cannot take B first, breaking circular wait. Release in reverse order and keep critical sections small. A timeout may recover operationally but is not proof that the design has eliminated deadlock. Trap: a lock per resource does not by itself give a safe lock discipline.

Topic-specific traps and diagnosis

  • Treating a lock as automatically fair or failure-safe.
  • Holding a lock across slow network work without discussing cost.
  • Confusing deadlock with starvation.

Exam marking checklist

  • Did I show the bad interleaving?
  • Did I state safety and a progress condition?
  • Did I include release/failure behaviour?
