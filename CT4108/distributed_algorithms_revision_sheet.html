CT4108 Revision

Distributed Algorithms

Describe an algorithm through its local actions, messages, assumptions and system-wide property.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Specify node state, messages, transitions and termination, then prove safety/liveness under failure assumptions.
  • Election needs symmetry breaking; asynchronous failure detection is suspicion/eventual, not perfect.
  • Consensus needs agreement, validity and termination; crash and Byzantine models differ.
  • Epochs/terms, sequence numbers and idempotent handlers tolerate reordering/duplicates.

Paper evidence and scope

No local CT4108 papers: syllabus-based practice.

Medium practice — 15 marks

Three replicas elect coordinator after timeout. (a) [5] properties. (b) [5] epoch rule. (c) [5] partition hazard.

Answer for every part

(a) Answer: safety: at most one leader per epoch; liveness: eventually a leader if majority/communication assumptions hold. Trap: omitting assumptions.

(b) Answer: persist highest epoch, vote once per epoch, include epoch on messages. Trap: volatile vote after restart.

(c) Answer: require majority quorum so two sides cannot both elect; minority loses availability. Trap: saying timeout proves peer dead.

Hard practice — 15 marks

“Commit after all nodes reply.” (a) [5] flaw. (b) [5] quorum. (c) [5] Byzantine contrast.

Answer for every part

(a) Answer: one crashed/slow node blocks progress. Trap: confusing safety with availability.

(b) Answer: majority plus durable votes supports crash-fault progress. Trap: no recovery state.

(c) Answer: Byzantine nodes equivocate, requiring authentication/stronger fault-tolerant quorum. Trap: applying crash proof unchanged.
