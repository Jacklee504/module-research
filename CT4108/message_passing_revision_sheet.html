CT4108 Revision

Message Passing

Reason about communication when senders and receivers do not share memory or timing.

Rapid exam refresher

  • Distinguish synchronous/asynchronous and blocking/non-blocking communication.
  • At-most-once can lose; at-least-once duplicates; exactly-once needs application idempotency/state.
  • Ordering is usually per producer/partition, not global; use schema, correlation ID and deadline.
  • Bound queues, backpressure and dead-letter handling prevent slow-consumer collapse.

Medium practice — 15 marks

Orders publish payment requests. (a) [5] delivery choice. (b) [5] duplicate safety. (c) [5] failing worker.

Answer for every part

(a) Answer: at-least-once durable queue prevents loss; acknowledge only after durable handling. Route/trap: match loss/duplicate consequence; do not promise magic exactly-once.

(b) Answer: store idempotency key/result transactionally before ack; repeat returns recorded outcome. Trap: charge before recording.

(c) Answer: visibility timeout, bounded retry/backoff and dead-letter queue with alert. Trap: infinite poison-message loop.

Hard practice — 15 marks

Balances need per-account ordering. (a) [5] constraint. (b) [5] design. (c) [5] recovery.

Answer for every part

(a) Answer: concurrent producers need serial account order, not global order. Trap: relying on arrival time.

(b) Answer: partition by account or attach sequence/version and reject/reconcile stale event. Trap: random partitioning.

(c) Answer: persist offset with state; replay only idempotent versioned events. Trap: ack before state commit.
