Artificial Intelligence - Most Asked Exam Questions

Paper-led question families, answer requirements, and construction patterns for the four supplied CT421 papers.

How To Use This Document

Primary evidence: the current-format papers are 2022/23, 2023/24, and 2024/25. All three use four 25-mark questions, each split into assessed subparts.

Legacy/backup evidence: 2021/22 has the same four-question structure and is used for recurring variants. Variants absent from the latest three are explicitly marked legacy-only.

Method: 44 current-paper assessed units are mapped below. A question is kept separate whenever its requested construction, example, proof-style explanation, calculation, or mark structure differs.

  1. Work through families in priority order.
  2. Attempt the paper wording before reading the answer checklist.
  3. Use the flashcards for exact definitions, formulas, and constructions.

Current Priorities

Ranked primarily by recurrence in 2022/23-2024/25, then by available marks. A one-paper new-format question remains a current priority when it occupies a full recent subpart.

Priority 1 - Genetic-Algorithm Design for Constrained Allocation

Recurrence and marks: 3/3 current papers, 43 marks. This is the strongest design family.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2022/23 Q2(b), 14Allocate unique projects to students, each ranking ten projects. Apply a GA, including a suitable fitness function, chromosome representation, and operators.Permutation/uniqueness constraint and ranked preferences.
2023/24 Q2(b), 14Place items with weights and values in capacity-limited containers, maximising value. Give fitness, representation, and operators.Multiple-knapsack capacity constraint.
2024/25 Q2(b)(i)-(iv), 15Assign N tasks to M processors with processing-time, memory, and capability constraints. Design a GA: chromosome, fitness, selection, crossover, mutation.Explicit selection requirement and several processor constraints.

Given a constrained allocation problem, design a GA whose encoding, fitness function, selection, crossover, mutation, and repair mechanism are valid for that specific constraint set.

Complete answer must contain: (1) one gene per decision, such as task i → processor j; (2) a fitness formula that rewards the stated objective and penalises every violated constraint; (3) a parent-selection mechanism, normally tournament selection; (4) crossover that combines assignments; (5) mutation that makes a small valid assignment change; and (6) repair or rejection after crossover/mutation where invalid offspring are possible.

Standard construction: for tasks, use chromosome x = (x1, …, xN), where xi ∈ {1, …, M} is the processor assigned to task i. A valid fitness is −makespan − λΣ penalties, where penalties cover memory excess and incompatible assignments. For knapsack, let xi ∈ {0, 1, …, K}, where 0 means unselected and k means knapsack k; maximise Σ vi minus a large penalty for each capacity excess.

Priority 2 - Game Trees, Minimax, and Alpha-Beta Pruning

Recurrence and marks: minimax is in 3/3 current papers, 27 direct marks; alpha-beta is explicit in 2024/25 and recurring in the legacy paper.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2022/23 Q1(a), 10For a suitable game, show minimax choosing moves; explain game-tree construction and value assignment; discuss efficiency and improvement.General construction plus efficiency discussion.
2022/23 Q1(b), 8Augment minimax where all potential game states cannot be enumerated.Depth limit and heuristic / sampling response.
2023/24 Q1(a), 9For misère Nim with piles 4 and 3, draw the game tree and show minimax.Concrete game-tree trace; last move loses.
2024/25 Q1(a)(i), 5For tic-tac-toe, draw the first three levels and evaluate positions with minimax, with X maximising.Depth-limited tree and stated MAX player.
2024/25 Q1(a)(ii), 5Apply alpha-beta to that tree and illustrate a branch that can be pruned.Must show a specific α ≥ β cutoff, not only define pruning.

Complete answer must contain: make legal moves children of a state; label alternating levels MAX and MIN; assign terminal utilities, or a heuristic at a cutoff depth; propagate maxima at MAX nodes and minima at MIN nodes; choose the root child with the final maximum value. For alpha-beta, carry α, MAX's best guaranteed value, and β, MIN's best guaranteed value; prune when α ≥ β because later children cannot change an ancestor's decision.

Standard example: if a MAX ancestor already has α = 6 and a MIN child sees one successor valued 4, then β = 4. Since α ≥ β, do not inspect that MIN node's remaining successors. The MIN node can never return more than 4, so MAX will not choose it over the known 6.

Priority 3 - Game Theory, Cooperation, and Auctions in MAS

Recurrence and marks: 3/3 current papers, 58 marks across Q3. This is the highest-mark current block, with several distinct families.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2022/23 Q3(a)(i)-(iii), 9Compare English and Dutch auctions: protocol, rational bidder strategy, and limitations.All three requested headings are compulsory.
2022/23 Q3(b), 8Suggest an efficient approach for agents to negotiate agreement on attributes in addition to price.Multi-attribute negotiation, not a one-item auction.
2022/23 Q3(c), 8Explain, with examples, dominant strategy and Nash equilibrium.Definitions require examples.
2023/24 Q3(a), 7Explain speech acts in MAS and give negotiation-setting examples.Communication-act examples required.
2023/24 Q3(b), 8Explain, with examples, dominant strategy and Nash equilibrium.Direct repeat of the 2022/23 definition family.
2023/24 Q3(c), 10Explain the Prisoner's Dilemma and mechanisms promoting cooperation in populations playing it.Population mechanisms, not only a payoff matrix.
2024/25 Q3(a), 6Using the Prisoner's Dilemma, explain dominant strategy and Nash equilibrium.Definitions tied to one named game.
2024/25 Q3(b), 9Explain tit-for-tat, why it succeeds, and its performance under noisy moves.Noise/failure mode is a separate requirement.
2024/25 Q3(c)(i)-(iii), 10Compare two auction types by mechanics, strategic behaviour, and efficiency in reaching optimal allocations.Efficiency replaces the older explicit limitations heading.

Game-theory proof pattern: write the two actions and payoffs. An action is dominant for player i if, for every action profile of the other players, it gives at least as high a payoff as every alternative. A profile is a Nash equilibrium if no one player can improve their payoff by changing strategy while all other strategies remain fixed. In the Prisoner's Dilemma, defect is dominant for both; (defect, defect) is Nash, although (cooperate, cooperate) is better jointly.

Auction construction: English: ascending price, bidders remain until price reaches private value; it reveals information but can be slow/collusive. Dutch: descending price, first acceptance wins; a bidder trades a lower price against losing to another bidder; it is fast but strategically uncertain. For multi-attribute negotiation, define offers over price, deadline, quality, and quantity, evaluate with weighted utilities, then seek Pareto-efficient offers where improving one agent necessarily harms another.

Cooperation answer: tit-for-tat cooperates first and then repeats the opponent's previous move. It is nice, retaliatory, and forgiving after renewed cooperation. Noise can turn cooperation into apparent defection, causing a retaliation loop; forgiving or generous variants can break it. Other mechanisms are repeated interaction, reputation, partner selection, and incentives/enforcement.

Priority 4 - Explainability and Black-Box Deep Learning

Recurrence and marks: 3/3 current papers, 25 marks. It is the most stable Question 4 family.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2022/23 Q4(a), 8Explain the importance of explainability and, for an AI paradigm, approaches for systems that generate explanations.Any paradigm can be chosen.
2023/24 Q4(a), 8Explain explainability and, with respect to deep learning, approaches that provide explanations.Specifically deep learning.
2024/25 Q4(c), 9Explain black-box deep learning, why it is problematic in real applications, and two approaches to make it interpretable.Explicit black-box definition, real-world risk, and two methods.

Complete answer must contain: a black-box model's output may be accurate while the internal route to it is difficult to inspect; this harms trust, auditability, debugging, bias detection, and accountability in high-stakes decisions. Then describe two methods, not merely name them: feature attribution/saliency identifies input features or image regions that most affected an output; a local surrogate such as LIME fits an interpretable approximation near one prediction. Other valid choices include counterfactual explanations, attention visualisation, concept activation vectors, or interpretable-by-design models.

Priority 5 - BFS, DFS, and Iterative Deepening

Recurrence and marks: 2/3 current papers, 19 marks. The same comparison headings recur exactly.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2023/24 Q1(b), 9Using an example, distinguish BFS, DFS, and iterative deepening by node-visit order; compare time complexity, space complexity, and completeness.Requires both traversal example and comparison.
2024/25 Q1(b), 10Explain how BFS, DFS, and iterative deepening differ in visit order; compare time, space, and completeness.Same family; one extra mark.

Complete comparison: BFS uses a FIFO queue and visits depth 0, then 1, then 2; it is complete for finite branching, takes O(bd) time and O(bd) space. DFS follows one branch before backtracking; it uses O(bm) space and O(bm) time, but is not complete in an infinite-depth space. Iterative deepening repeats depth-limited DFS at limits 0, 1, 2, …; it is complete for finite branching, has O(bd) time and O(bd) space. State assumptions: b is branching factor, d shallowest-solution depth, and m maximum depth.

Priority 6 - Schema Theorem and Exploration-Exploitation

Recurrence and marks: schema theorem in 2/3 current papers for 22 marks; the 2024/25 exploration-exploitation variation is 10 marks.

Paper unitExact or near-exact wordingMeaningful variation
2022/23 Q2(a)(a)-(c), 11With reference to GAs and the schema theorem, explain effects of mutation, crossover, and selection on a population.Operator effects are individually assessed.
2023/24 Q2(a), 11Explain schema theorem, its key principles, and roles of crossover, mutation, and selection.Requires theorem explanation as well as operators.
2024/25 Q2(a)(i)-(ii), 10Discuss exploration-exploitation with respect to GA operators and hill climbing.Must compare population search with local search.

Complete answer: a schema is a pattern such as 1*0*, with * a wildcard. The schema theorem predicts that short, low-order, above-average schemas receive increasing expected representation. Selection favours schemas appearing in fitter individuals. Crossover combines building blocks but can disrupt a schema when a crossover point falls within its defining length. Mutation introduces diversity and can create new material, but a high mutation rate disrupts existing schemas. Selection/crossover mainly exploit known promising regions; mutation and diverse parent choice explore. Hill climbing mainly exploits by moving to a better neighbour and can get trapped on a local optimum, plateau, or ridge; random restarts or occasional non-improving moves add exploration.

Recurrence and marks: artificial life and neuro-evolution appear in 2/3 current papers; ACO is in the newest 2/3 papers.

2022/23 Q4(c), 8With existing systems, explain main properties of an artificial-life system.System example and properties required.
2023/24 Q4(b), 8With existing systems, explain main properties of an artificial-life system.Direct repeat.
2023/24 Q4(c), 9Explain ACO and show, with a simple example, how it solves TSP.Tour construction, not only definition.

Standard examples: Artificial life: Boids agents obey separation, alignment, and cohesion locally, yet flocking emerges globally without a controller. Neuro-evolution: chromosome encodes all weights w1, …, wn, or a graph of nodes/connections; GA fitness evaluates network performance. It avoids gradients but is expensive because many networks must be evaluated. ACO: ants choose edges using pheromone and heuristic distance; short tours deposit more pheromone; evaporation prevents old paths from dominating. For symbolic vs connectionist AI, compare explicit rules and inspectable reasoning with neural networks' data-driven pattern learning; apply both to the same domain, such as medical diagnosis.
