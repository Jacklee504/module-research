Expected Questions By Learning Outcome

An evidence-based priority page. It combines the official learning outcomes with seven local papers, giving the recurring questions that best represent each outcome.

How "Expected" Is Used Here

Evidence rule: a question family is rated highest when it is both official scope and repeated in the recent papers. The 2024/25 and 2025/26 papers are the primary format evidence: both have seven compulsory questions with marks 14, 15, 15, 14, 14, 14, 14.

Not a guarantee: the topic order, individual graph, and specialist-question choice can rotate. "Expected" means that you should prepare the family as if it can appear, not that it will occupy a stated numbered question.

Assessment context: the official page weights the written assessment at 80% and continuous assessment at 20%, so this page deliberately prioritises exam-return material.

Expected Current-Format Families

These are the most defensible expectations if the seven-question 2024/25-2025/26 format continues. They are deliberately organised by family, not forecast question number.

1. BFS with all shortest paths

Expectation: a 14-mark task with a short algorithm-description part and a worked graph-tracing part.

Be ready to: state input and output; run BFS by levels; record distances; retain every valid predecessor; enumerate all shortest root-to-target paths; draw a spanning tree if asked.

Evidence: every paper; the exact 5 + 9 structure is shared by 2024/25 and 2025/26.

2. Centrality calculation on a supplied graph

Expectation: 14-15 marks, likely mixing degree, closeness, and betweenness.

Be ready to: normalise degree correctly; build a distance row for closeness; list node pairs and shortest paths for betweenness; explain why a numerically more central node makes sense structurally.

Evidence: 2025/26 Q5 is 4 + 5 + 5; 2024/25 and the 2022/23-2023/24 papers cover the same measures in different arrangements.

3. Erdos-Renyi models and a probability or expectation

Expectation: a 14-mark block involving both G_ER(n,m) and G_ER(n,p), then a probability, degree distribution, or expected-size calculation.

Be ready to: distinguish fixed-number-of-edges from independently included edges; justify a probability; use E[|E|] = p * n(n-1)/2; recognise binomial degree probabilities.

Evidence: both latest papers use 4 + 5 + 5 marks; older papers rotate in giant components and coin/die degree questions.

4. Small-world comparison

Expectation: a 14-mark block built from characteristic path length or clustering/transitivity, followed by a Watts-Strogatz comparison.

Be ready to: define the requested statistic; derive its Erdos-Renyi value when asked; explain why Watts-Strogatz preserves comparatively high clustering while shortening paths relative to a circle graph.

Evidence: present in all papers from 2018/19 onward and retained as Q7 in both latest papers.

5. Graph construction or representation

Expectation: one 14-15 mark block from adjacency matrices, graph order/size, degree sequences, bipartite two-colouring, projections, or tree construction.

Be ready to: move between a graph and matrix, reason about connectedness and trees, order an adjacency matrix for a stated structure, and make a projection without losing the relevant node part.

Evidence: 2024/25 Q1-Q2 and 2025/26 Q1-Q2 both heavily use these representations.

6. Pruefer codes or another specialist graph family

Expectation: one 14-15 mark specialist block is plausible, but its exact form is not safe to forecast.

Be ready to: encode/decode labelled trees, infer degrees from a Pruefer code, and construct a labelled isomorphic graph. Keep affiliation networks and Laplacian reconstruction as variants, not predictions.

Evidence: Pruefer is a 15-mark Q3 in 2025/26 and is embedded in a 14-mark tree/Laplacian question in 2024/25; it also appears from 2021/22 onward.

Expected Coverage, Not Forecast Slots

Directed networks: prepare strong/weak connectedness, strongly connected components, and Bow-Tie as a coverage block. They are well-supported historically but have disappeared from the two current-format papers.

Markets and document networks: prepare the lecture material, including any current examples. The official outcomes require them, but their direct paper evidence is concentrated in the older archive.

Format risk: the new seven-question format has only two years of evidence. If it changes, the core families remain much more reliable than the present question order.

Best Revision Sequence

  1. BFS: complete all seven paper variants.
  2. Centrality: degree, closeness, betweenness, then eigenvector as a supporting variant.
  3. Erdos-Renyi: both models, probabilities, expected edges, and degree distributions.
  4. Watts-Strogatz: circle graph, clustering/transitivity, characteristic path length, and comparison language.
  5. Graph foundations: matrices, degree sequences, bipartite projections, and tree constraints.
  6. Pruefer codes and labelled trees.
  7. Directed, market, and document-network material from current teaching.
