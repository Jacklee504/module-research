Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Affiliation/bipartite and Laplacian material is newer local evidence, especially 2024/25; treat it as current coverage rather than a repeat prediction.

(a) Given B, form a one-mode projection and state what an off-diagonal weight counts. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Fix row/column modes; multiply B by its transpose; interpret diagonal separately.

Mark-aware answer: (BBT)ij counts groups shared by people i and j; its diagonal counts each person’s memberships. A projection is weighted unless deliberately thresholded.

Trap: Do not mix B BT with BTB.

(b) Given L=D-A, reconstruct A and verify it is a graph Laplacian. [6 marks]

Question demand: Read Aij=-Lij off diagonal; verify diagonal degree and row sums. Quickest route: Read Aij=-Lij off diagonal; verify diagonal degree and row sums.

Mark-aware answer: Set Aij=-Lij for i!=j and Aii=0. A valid simple-graph Laplacian is symmetric, has nonpositive off-diagonals, diagonal Lii=sum Aij, and every row sum zero.

Trap: Do not copy diagonal entries of L into A.

(c) Explain what diagonal entries of BBT mean. [4 marks]

Question demand: Use the row as a membership indicator. Quickest route: Use the row as a membership indicator.

Mark-aware answer: (BBT)ii is the dot product of person i's membership row with itself, so it counts that person's affiliations. It is not a self-loop unless the projection convention deliberately retains it.

Trap: Remove or report the diagonal before interpreting a simple one-mode graph.
