Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Degree, closeness, betweenness and occasional eigenvector centrality recur in 2017/18-2024/25 papers.

(a) Compare a broker, a locally popular node and a node close to all others. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Map broker to betweenness, local popularity to degree, reachability to closeness; calculate paths before scores.

Mark-aware answer: A broker can have modest degree but high betweenness because many shortest paths use it. High degree only means many direct neighbours. Closeness needs finite/reachable-distance handling in a disconnected graph.

Trap: Do not call all centralities interchangeable importance.

(b) Compute normalised degree centrality of a vertex of degree 3 in an undirected graph with 8 vertices. [6 marks]

Question demand: Use degree divided by the maximum possible degree n-1. Quickest route: Use degree divided by the maximum possible degree n-1.

Mark-aware answer: The maximum possible degree is 7, so normalised degree centrality is 3/7. State whether the paper wants raw degree 3 or the normalised score 3/7.

Trap: Do not divide by the number of edges.

(c) Explain why closeness is awkward on a disconnected graph. [4 marks]

Question demand: Refer to infinite/unreachable distances and name a convention. Quickest route: Refer to infinite/unreachable distances and name a convention.

Mark-aware answer: Ordinary closeness uses the reciprocal of total distance, but unreachable vertices produce infinite distance. One must restrict to a component or use harmonic closeness, which sums reciprocal finite distances.

Trap: Reporting a finite score without a disconnected-graph convention is incomplete.
