CS4423 Topic Guide

Small-World Networks

Explain how shortcuts retain local clustering while shrinking paths.

What the local papers support

Watts-Strogatz, clustering and characteristic path length recur as a local family in the 2017/18-2024/25 archive.

Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction
Start from a ring lattice; rewire each edge with probability beta. Clustering measures neighbour-neighbour links; L is average shortest-path length.At small beta, a few shortcuts sharply lower L while clustering remains high; at high beta clustering falls toward random-graph behaviour.

Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Watts-Strogatz, clustering and characteristic path length recur as a local family in the 2017/18-2024/25 archive.

(a) Explain why an intermediate beta is small world. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Define the lattice baseline, identify the rewiring rule, then state the joint C/L comparison.

Mark-aware answer: The lattice has high C and large L. A small number of long-range edges creates shortcuts, decreasing L while most local triangles remain, so C stays comparatively high. This joint profile is the small-world claim.

Trap: Do not say rewiring increases both clustering and path length.

(b) Define a local clustering coefficient and a characteristic path length. [6 marks]

Question demand: For a vertex count links among its neighbours against possible links; average shortest-path distances for L. Quickest route: For a vertex count links among its neighbours against possible links; average shortest-path distances for L.

Mark-aware answer: For degree k>=2, C_v=number of edges among neighbours divided by C(k,2); define C as an average over vertices using the stated convention. L is the average shortest-path distance over pairs in the connected network (or a stated reachable-pairs convention).

Trap: Do not use the global edge density as local clustering.

(c) Compare a ring lattice, a small-world network and an Erdos-Renyi graph. [4 marks]

Question demand: Give the qualitative C/L profile for each. Quickest route: Give the qualitative C/L profile for each.

Mark-aware answer: A ring lattice has high clustering and long paths. A small-world graph retains relatively high clustering but has short paths through shortcuts. An Erdos-Renyi graph usually has low clustering and short paths at comparable density.

Trap: A shortcut lowers path length; it does not itself prove high clustering.
