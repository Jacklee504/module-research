Prufer Codes and Trees

Encode a labelled tree by repeatedly removing its smallest leaf.

What the local papers support

Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction
A tree on n labels maps to a word of length n-2. Delete the smallest current leaf and record its neighbour. degree(v)=1+occurrences(v).Decode by joining the smallest label absent from the remaining word to its first symbol; finish by joining the final two labels.

Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Prufer encoding/decoding and degree-sequence reasoning recur in the local archive, notably 2017/18-2023/24 specialist tree questions.

(a) Decode the Prufer word (4,4,5,2) on labels 1,...,6 and give degrees. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: At each step choose the smallest label not in the remaining word, add its edge, delete the first symbol.

Mark-aware answer: Edges: 1-4, 3-4, 4-5, 5-2, then 2-6. Frequencies give degrees d(4)=3,d(5)=2,d(2)=2 and all other labels degree 1.

Trap: Do not choose merely any leaf when the convention is the smallest.

(b) Prove the degree-frequency rule for a Prufer code. [6 marks]

Question demand: Explain when a label is deleted and when it is written. Quickest route: Explain when a label is deleted and when it is written.

Mark-aware answer: A vertex v is written once whenever a smaller/current leaf adjacent to v is deleted. It remains until all but one incident edge have been removed, then v is deleted as a leaf without being written. Thus it appears deg(v)-1 times, so deg(v)=1+frequency(v).

Trap: The final two vertices are joined but not written.

(c) How many labelled trees are there on n fixed labels? [4 marks]

Question demand: Use the bijection with words of length n-2. Quickest route: Use the bijection with words of length n-2.

Mark-aware answer: Every labelled tree corresponds bijectively to a Prufer sequence of length n-2 over n labels. There are n choices at each position, hence nn-2 trees.

Trap: Do not count sequences of length n-1.
