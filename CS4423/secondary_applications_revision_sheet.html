Secondary Applications

Maintain coverage of older or intermittent applications without treating them as predictions.

Purpose and relevance

Structural balance, game theory/markets, transport networks and PageRank connect network methods to applications mentioned in the official description. Local evidence makes them older or intermittent: revise after the core eight guide families and weight current teaching heavily.Exam use: Application-to-graph mapping, rule/metric, calculation, update convention and limitation are explicit.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Translate the story into a network model, state the relevant concept, carry out the small calculation/diagram, then explain the result and its assumptions. Begin by naming the application’s status as directed/signed/weighted where relevant.Question interpretation: Identify the named application model, define its nodes and edges, then separate a structural metric from a causal or domain claim.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and formulas

  • Structural balance: signed triangle is balanced when the product of signs is positive.
  • PageRank: r=αPᵀr+(1−α)v; score flows through incoming links with damping.
  • Game/network market: agents choose actions/links under incentives; equilibrium is no unilateral profitable deviation.
  • Transport: weighted/directed edges may represent cost, time or capacity.
  • Rapid recall: Structural balance tests signed triads; assortativity compares endpoint attributes/degrees; community quality compares within-group connection to a baseline; diffusion rules require an explicit update convention.

Step-by-step method

  1. Define nodes, edges, signs/weights and direction.
  2. Select the appropriate application concept.
  3. Show a small diagram, iteration or payoff comparison.
  4. Interpret realistically and state one modelling limitation.
  5. Exam workflow: Translate the narrative into a small labelled graph, calculate the requested local quantity, and finish with one limitation of the model.

Worked starter question and model answer

Question: A signed triangle has +, −, − edges. Is it balanced?

Model answer: The product is (+)(−)(−)=+, so it is balanced: two mutually opposed relationships can be consistent when both parties oppose the same third party.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

In a signed triangle with signs AB=+, BC=-, AC=-, the product is (+)(-)(-)=+, so the triad is balanced: A and B are friends sharing an enemy C. If AC flips to +, the product becomes negative and the triad is unbalanced. The calculation classifies the sign pattern; it does not prove the relationships will evolve toward balance.

Graduated practice

Prompt 1

Why does PageRank use damping? Check: it prevents rank traps and represents random jumps, making a stable distribution possible.

Exam bridge

Model a web, market or transport scenario as a network and justify a measure/algorithm. Check: make representation choices explicit, apply the relevant method, interpret output, and name a limitation rather than overclaiming causality.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: a threshold node adopts when at least half its neighbours have adopted. On path A-B-C-D with A and D initially active, apply synchronous updates. Check B and C each see one of two neighbours active, so both adopt in round 1 and the cascade completes; asynchronous wording could change intermediate states and must be declared.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a signed triangle balanced by visual intuition alone.
  • Confusing PageRank with plain in-degree.
  • Assuming a network model proves real-world causation.
  • Recovery rule: Do not infer causation from a network association or silently choose synchronous updates when the question leaves timing ambiguous.

Final checklist

  • Did I model the application precisely?
  • Did I state assumptions?
  • Did I label it secondary/intermittent?
  • Final 60-second audit: Application-to-graph mapping, rule/metric, calculation, update convention and limitation are explicit.
