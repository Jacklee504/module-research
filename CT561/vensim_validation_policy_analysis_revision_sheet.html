CT561 Topic Guide

Vensim, Validation and Policy Analysis

A simulation result is useful only when its structure, units, behaviour and policy claims have been checked.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions: verification checks equations/units/implementation; validation tests structural and behavioural adequacy for a purpose. Policy analysis varies an intervention and reports sensitivity, not certainties. Question cue: name a concrete test, baseline, scenario range and decision-relevant outcome.

Worked practice 1 — paper-derived validation

Question demand: Validate a staffing model. Plan/choice: separate structure from behaviour. Worked answer: check dimensional consistency/stock conservation and extreme conditions, compare historical trend/known behaviour, then sensitivity-test attrition and adjustment time. Mark-winning points: named tests. Common trap: one fitted plot proves validity.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design policy analysis

Question demand: Recommend workload policy. Plan/choice: baseline plus alternatives over uncertain parameters, report side effects. Worked answer: simulate completion, backlog and fatigue under policy ranges; reject policies that improve short-term throughput but worsen delayed fatigue; disclose uncertainty. Mark-winning points: trade-off. Common trap: best one run is best policy.

Purpose and relevance

Vensim makes a stock-flow specification executable, but the assessment value is in explaining credible model-building, testing and cautious policy comparison.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Separate verification (implemented as intended) from validation (adequate for purpose). Name concrete tests, define a baseline, vary one policy/assumption at a time, compare outcomes and limits.

Key terms and formulas

  • Level: stock; rate: flow; auxiliary: algebraic helper; constant: fixed parameter.
  • Units check: catches dimensionally impossible equations.
  • Extreme-condition test: ask whether zero/very large inputs give sensible behaviour.
  • Behaviour reproduction: compare shape/pattern, not only point fit.
  • Sensitivity analysis: vary uncertain inputs and report whether conclusions change.

Method derivation

  1. State decision purpose, boundary and performance measures.
  2. Build causal then stock-flow structure; set equations, units and initial values.
  3. Verify equations, units and boundary conditions in Vensim.
  4. Validate structure/behaviour against knowledge or data appropriate to the purpose.
  5. Run baseline and policy scenarios; communicate uncertainty and unintended effects.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: A model says that doubling recruitment always doubles productive staff immediately. Give two checks and a repair.

Answer: An extreme-condition/structure check exposes missing training delay; a units check confirms recruitment is staff/time. Add a trainee stock and graduation flow. Then vary training time and recruitment capacity to see whether the policy conclusion remains robust.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

A flow is written as inventory + orders/day. What should you say? Check: it fails dimensional consistency; stocks and rates cannot be added.

Exam bridge

Propose a validation and sensitivity plan for a hospital-capacity policy model. Check: include purpose, tests, baseline metrics, parameter ranges, scenario comparison and what would make you cautious.

High-value traps and corrections

Integrated paper-style practice - validate a policy model [8 marks]

Scenario. A Vensim model predicts that a recruitment subsidy reduces a staffing backlog. The model matches historic backlog data but uses an assumed training delay. Give a validation and policy-analysis sequence.

Question-demand diagnosis: historical fit alone is insufficient; test structure, parameters and behaviour under policy uncertainty.

Fastest route: test units and boundary/structure first, then historical and extreme behaviour, then sensitivity runs.

Worked answer: Check dimensional consistency and whether the hiring, training and attrition feedbacks represent the real process. Compare known historical patterns, including turning points, not only aggregate fit. Challenge the training delay with expert evidence and vary it across plausible values. Run baseline and subsidy policies under the same assumptions, reporting backlog, staffing and cost with uncertainty. If the subsidy helps only under one untested delay, present it as a conditional insight rather than a recommendation.

Topic-specific trap: a calibrated curve can fit history for the wrong causal structure.

  • Claiming a model is “validated” because it runs.
  • Changing several assumptions and attributing the result to one policy.
  • Presenting one fitted graph as proof of future accuracy.

Final answer checklist

  • I distinguish verification and validation.
  • I name testable checks, not just “test it”.
  • I report uncertainty alongside a policy recommendation.
