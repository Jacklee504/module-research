CT561 Topic Guide

Workforce and Stock Management

Goal-seeking policies adjust an accumulated resource toward a target, often with delay.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions/equations: desired staff=students/desired student-staff ratio; discrepancy=desired-actual; adjustment=discrepancy/adjustment time. Stock management combines an anchor (replacement for departures) and adjustment to close gap. Question cue: show actual/desired/discrepancy, all flows and delayed behaviour.

Worked practice 1 — paper-derived staff target

Question demand: 10000 students, ratio 20, staff 500; change ratio to 10. Plan/choice: calculate desired before/after. Worked answer: initially desired=500/equilibrium; new desired=1000, discrepancy=500, so hires include gap/AT plus replacement quits. Mark-winning points: direction and equilibrium. Common trap: ratio numerator/denominator reversed.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Explain overshoot risk after target change. Plan/choice: trace adjustment delay/pipeline. Worked answer: short AT increases corrective hires; recruitment/training delay lets hiring continue after gap shrinks, producing overshoot/oscillation. Test AT scenarios and include attrition. Mark-winning points: delay mechanism. Common trap: faster correction always better.

Purpose and relevance

Inventory, staffing and capacity problems share an anchor-and-adjust structure: compare an actual stock with a desired stock and adjust through a limited flow.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Identify actual stock, target, gap, adjustment time and adjustment flow. Draw the balancing loop, then explain how a delay in hiring, delivery or training changes behaviour.

Key terms and formulas

  • Gap: desired stock − actual stock.
  • Adjustment flow: often gap / adjustment time.
  • Anchor: desired/target stock; adjust: corrective flow.
  • Pipeline: hires in training or orders in transit; it is a stock, not a delay label.
  • Little’s Law: WIP = throughput × cycle time, when averages and a stable system make it applicable.

Method derivation

  1. Set the target from demand, workload or coverage rule.
  2. Calculate the gap between target and actual resource.
  3. Convert the gap into a feasible rate using adjustment time.
  4. Add attrition, completions or delivery delays as separate flows/stocks.
  5. Test a shock: does the loop correct smoothly, oscillate, or overshoot?

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: A team has 40 staff, target 50, and recruitment adjustment time 5 months. Ignore attrition. Find hiring rate.

Answer: Gap = 10 staff. Hiring = 10/5 = 2 staff/month. Workforce is the stock; hiring is its inflow. A training pipeline would delay when hires become productive, so immediate adjustment may be unrealistic.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

Inventory is 70, target 100 and adjustment time 3 weeks. Check: order rate = 10 units/week, before any delivery delay/capacity cap.

Exam bridge

Model an ageing workforce with recruitment, training and retirement; recommend one policy. Check: show pipeline/age stocks where needed, identify delays, then assess a trade-off rather than merely saying “hire more”.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Making a target a material stock without reason.
  • Forgetting attrition or the training pipeline.
  • Using Little’s Law on a clearly non-stationary, non-average snapshot.

Final answer checklist

  • I have actual, desired, gap and adjustment time.
  • Flows have correct units.
  • I mention delay-driven overshoot risk.
