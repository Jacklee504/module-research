CT561 Topic Guide

Supporting Simulation Concepts

Use these concepts to make core modelling answers precise and to handle shorter comparison or interpretation parts.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions: equilibrium has zero net flow; first-order smoothing adjusts output toward input over adjustment time; Little's Law L=lambda W applies stable average flow systems. Boundaries, units and delays determine plausible behaviour. Question cue: state assumptions before selecting a supporting method.

Worked practice 1 — paper-derived smoothing

Question demand: Explain smoothing a noisy demand input. Plan/choice: adjustment stock/flow. Worked answer: Smoothed=INTEG((Input-Smoothed)/AT,initial); larger AT filters more noise but responds later. Mark-winning points: adjustment-time direction. Common trap: smoothing removes delay.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Apply Little's Law to service capacity. Plan/choice: check steady average conditions then calculate. Worked answer: L=lambda W, so with throughput 20/day and mean time 3 days, average WIP=60; do not apply to a transient snapshot without averaging. Mark-winning points: conditions/units. Common trap: use any backlog instantly.

Purpose and relevance

Delays, smoothing, equilibrium, boundaries and uncertainty explain why a model behaves as it does. They also help distinguish CT561’s system-dynamics emphasis from adjacent simulation methods.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Define the term in the model’s own units, connect it to a stock/flow or feedback mechanism, then explain its behavioural consequence. For method comparisons, state scope and uncertainty rather than overclaiming.

Key terms and formulas

  • Equilibrium: inflow = outflow, so a stock is momentarily constant.
  • Delay: a cause affects a result later; often represented by a pipeline stock.
  • Smoothing: a perceived/average signal changes gradually, reducing reaction to noise but adding delay.
  • Model boundary: what is endogenous versus treated as external.
  • Monte Carlo: repeated random sampling to quantify output uncertainty; discrete-event: state changes at events; queueing: arrivals, service and waiting.

Method derivation

  1. State the system purpose and boundary.
  2. Locate accumulations, delays and information used for decisions.
  3. Find equilibrium by setting net flow to zero.
  4. For uncertainty, choose inputs to vary, distributions/justification and output summaries.
  5. Explain whether a discrete-event, Monte Carlo or queueing view is genuinely required by the question.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: A store orders from perceived sales, calculated as a smoothed average of actual sales. Sales jump for one week. Explain the likely effect.

Answer: Perceived sales rises gradually, so ordering responds with delay and is less sensitive to a one-week spike. If the smoothing time is too long, the store may under-order initially; the delay can interact with delivery delay and create oscillation.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

A queue’s arrival rate equals service rate. What can you conclude? Check: the average content may be at equilibrium, but variability/initial queue and assumptions still matter.

Exam bridge

For a call-centre decision, decide whether system dynamics, discrete-event simulation, Monte Carlo or queueing analysis is most suitable. Check: match it to the question (long-term staffing feedback versus individual waits/random arrivals), then state uncertainty and data requirements.

High-value traps and corrections

Integrated paper-style practice - smooth a noisy signal [8 marks]

Scenario. Monthly demand is 100, 140 and 80 units. A manager uses first-order exponential smoothing with smoothing time 2 months and DT=1, starting forecast 100. Calculate two forecast updates and explain the delay trade-off.

Question-demand diagnosis: use the stock-like smoothing equation rather than averaging all observations.

Fastest route: Forecast'=Forecast+(Actual−Forecast)DT/smoothing time.

Worked answer: After demand 140, forecast=100+(40)/2=120. After demand 80, forecast=120+(−40)/2=100. Smoothing removes some noise and avoids reacting fully to a one-month spike, but it lags a genuine step change. Choose the smoothing time against the operational cost of overreaction versus delayed response, then test behaviour on historical and extreme patterns.

Topic-specific trap: a smoothing time of two does not mean “average exactly the last two values”.

  • Assuming equilibrium means the system has no flows.
  • Calling every time lag a delay stock without modelling its mechanism.
  • Using Monte Carlo, discrete-event or queueing terminology as if it were CT561’s central assessed method.

Final answer checklist

  • I state boundary, units and assumptions.
  • I relate delay/smoothing to behaviour.
  • I choose an adjacent method only when its event/randomness question warrants it.

Past-paper note

Equilibrium, delays, smoothing and Little’s Law are useful supporting concepts in CT561 materials. Discrete-event simulation, Monte Carlo and queueing are lower-confidence exam targets: use current teaching material to confirm their depth.
