CT561 Topic Guide

Numerical Integration

Use a flow over a small time step to approximate the next value of a stock.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Equation: Euler: S(t+DT)=S(t)+(inflow-outflow)DT, assuming net flow is constant over the step. Smaller DT usually improves approximation but does not validate structure. Question cue: tabulate time, rates, net flow, DT and next stock with units.

Worked practice 1 — paper-derived Euler

Question demand: S=100, inflow=30/day, outflow=10/day, DT=.5 day. Plan/choice: net flow then multiply DT. Worked answer: net=20/day, change=10, Snext=110. Mark-winning points: rate-times-time units. Common trap: add flow without DT.

Worked practice 2 — paper-derived error analysis

Question demand: Explain when Euler overestimates a stock. Plan/choice: compare left-endpoint rate to changing net-flow curve. Worked answer: if net flow decreases over interval, holding its initial higher value overstates area/change; reduce DT or use higher-order method. Mark-winning points: graphical-integration rationale. Common trap: DT=5 uses five successive DT=1 updates.

Purpose and relevance

Most dynamic models cannot be solved neatly by hand. Euler integration approximates accumulation repeatedly and makes the assumptions visible.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Make a time table. At each row calculate inflow, outflow and net flow from the current stock, then update using the stated DT. State the approximation rule and discuss whether DT is small enough.

Key terms and formulas

  • DT: simulation time step.
  • Net flow: inflow − outflow.
  • Euler: S(t+DT)=S(t)+net flow(t)×DT.
  • Graphical integration: area under a net-flow curve changes the stock.
  • Smaller DT usually reduces discretisation error, but does not repair bad structure or data.

Method derivation

  1. Write the stock and its initial value.
  2. Evaluate rates at the start of the interval.
  3. Multiply net rate by DT and add it to the stock.
  4. Repeat, retaining units and rounded values sensibly.
  5. Check whether a known equilibrium or bound is violated.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: A stock starts at 100. Inflow is 20/day; outflow is 10% of the current stock/day; DT = 1 day. Find two steps.

Answer: At day 0, net = 20 − 10 = 10, so S1 = 110. At day 1, net = 20 − 11 = 9, so S2 = 119. Euler used each interval’s starting stock; it did not continuously update within the day.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

Starting stock 50, constant net flow −3/hour, DT 2 hours. Find the next stock. Check: 44; multiply the rate by two hours.

Exam bridge

Given a changing inflow/outflow graph, sketch the stock and explain where it rises, falls and peaks. Check: use the sign of net flow; a stock peaks where net flow crosses from positive to negative.

Integrated paper-style practice - Euler stock update [8 marks]

Scenario. A reservoir starts at 100 ML. Inflow is 20 ML/day; outflow is 10% of current stock/day; DT=1 day. Calculate two Euler updates and explain one source of approximation error.

Question-demand diagnosis: calculate rates at the start of each interval and retain units.

Fastest route: net at day 0 is 20−10=10, then recompute from the new stock.

Worked answer: S1=100+10(1)=110 ML. At day 1 the outflow is 11 ML/day, so net is 9 and S2=119 ML. Euler holds the start-of-step rate constant across each day. Because outflow rises continuously with stock, a large DT can misestimate the area under the net-flow curve; reduce DT and check convergence, but do not claim that this validates the reservoir model.

Topic-specific trap: never add a rate to a stock without multiplying by DT.

  • Adding a rate directly to a stock without × DT.
  • Using the newly calculated stock to recompute an old interval.
  • Claiming a smaller DT makes a model validated.

Final answer checklist

  • My table shows time, rates, net flow and stock.
  • Units cancel correctly.
  • I have said what Euler approximates.
