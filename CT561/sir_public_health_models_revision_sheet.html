CT561 Topic Guide

SIR and Public-Health Models

SIR is a stock-flow model of movement from susceptibility to infection to removal.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Frequency-dependent SIR notation used on this page: S, I and R are people in susceptible, infectious and removed stocks; N=S+I+R is the fixed closed population. The infection flow is F=βSI/N people/day, where β is the effective transmission rate (/day); recovery is G=γI people/day, where γ is the recovery rate (/day) and 1/γ is mean infectious duration. Hence dS/dt=−F, dI/dt=F−G and dR/dt=G. When S≈N, R₀=β/γ and early infections grow when R₀>1; at any later time Rₑ=R₀S/N. If the alternative notation c,i,d occurs, c is contacts/person/day, i is infection probability/contact, d is mean infectious duration in days: β=ci, γ=1/d, so R₀=β/γ=cid. Question cue: declare this convention, define every parameter with units, then show which mechanism a policy changes.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt)

Question demand: Derive the initial growth threshold. Plan/choice: substitute F and G into dI/dt, then use S≈N and I>0. Worked answer: dI/dt=βSI/N−γI=I(βS/N−γ). Initially S/N≈1, so growth requires β−γ>0, equivalently β/γ>1. Thus R₀=β/γ; in the c,i,d convention it is cid because β=ci and γ=1/d. Mark-winning points: state the closed-population/early-outbreak assumption and distinguish R₀ from the current infected count. Common trap: mixing β with c or γ with d without defining the conversion.

Worked practice 2 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt)

Question demand: Add quarantine and gradual vaccination. Plan/choice: split the infection transition, add a vaccinated stock/flow and preserve conservation. Worked answer: move a stated fraction of infectious people into a quarantine stock with a recovery flow, specify whether its transmission rate is zero or reduced, and add vaccination as a flow from S to protected/removed at a capacity-limited rate. Recalculate N as the sum of every live compartment, report attack rate as cumulative infected/N, and test the policy against a no-policy baseline. Mark-winning points: equations, units and conservation. Common trap: leaving quarantined people infectious without an explicit transmission assumption.

Purpose and relevance

The SIR structure is a compact way to connect feedback, stock-flow thinking and policy analysis in a public-health setting.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Define the population boundary and states, draw the S → I → R flows, give the infection and recovery equations, then say which parameter a proposed policy changes and why.

Key terms and formulas

  • S, I, R: susceptible, infectious and removed/recovered stocks.
  • Frequency-dependent infection: new infections = βSI/N.
  • Recovery: recoveries = γI; infectious duration is approximately 1/γ.
  • Basic reproduction number: R₀ ≈ β/γ in this simple form.
  • Effective reproduction: Rₑ ≈ R₀(S/N); growth requires Rₑ > 1.

Method derivation

  1. List who can enter and leave each state.
  2. Write conservation equations: dS/dt = −infection; dI/dt = infection − recovery; dR/dt = recovery.
  3. Check S+I+R remains N in a closed model.
  4. Map an intervention to β, γ or the available susceptible population.
  5. State assumptions: homogeneous mixing, fixed parameters, no births/deaths unless added.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: N = 1,000, S = 990, I = 10, β = 0.3/day, γ = 0.1/day. Estimate flows now.

Answer: New infections = 0.3×990×10/1000 = 2.97/day. Recoveries = 0.1×10 = 1/day. I initially rises by about 1.97/day. R₀ is about 3, but current growth is explained more directly by Rₑ ≈ 2.97.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

Where would vaccination appear? Check: as a flow from S to protected/removed (or a lower initial S); explain the modelling choice.

Exam bridge

Extend SIR for quarantine or hospital capacity and discuss one benefit and limitation. Check: add explicit stocks/flows, say which feedback changes, and state an evidence/assumption caveat.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Treating R as necessarily “recovered” when it can mean removed.
  • Using βSI without declaring a density-dependent convention.
  • Calling R₀ the number infected at every later time.

Final answer checklist

  • All compartments and flows are labelled.
  • Units for β and γ are consistent.
  • The policy changes a defined mechanism.

Past-paper note

SIR/public-health extensions are a strong repeated topic family in local papers, but the exact disease scenario and requested extension are not predictable.
