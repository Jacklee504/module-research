Current Priority Families

1. Graphical Integration, Euler Method and Error

Frequency: all three current papers, always a complete 25-mark question. This is the most stable paper structure.

  • 2023/24 Q4(a) [12]: constant outflow 50; regular inflow 100 to 0 over five time units. Draw stock-flow including net flow, calculate net flow, and graphically integrate the stock with behaviour modes.
  • 2023/24 Q4(b) [5]: describe Euler and assumptions; for linear net flow, state when it overestimates.
  • 2023/24 Q4(c) [8]: calculate stock using Euler with DT=5.
  • 2024/25 Q4(a) [12]: two constant inflows, 30 and 20 units/minute, against a sawtooth outflow. Draw, calculate net flow, graphical integration and behaviour modes.
  • 2024/25 Q4(b) [5]: state when Euler underestimates and support it over 0-5.
  • 2024/25 Q4(c) [8]: Euler with DT=10.
  • 2025/26 Q1(a) [12]: variable inflow and constant 50 units/day outflow; stock starts at 100 and may go negative.
  • 2025/26 Q1(b) [5]: state when Euler overestimates and support it over 0-2.5 with DT=0.5.
  • 2025/26 Q1(c) [8]: Euler with DT=5.

Given inflow/outflow graph(s), draw the stock-flow model including net flow; calculate net flow; integrate it graphically to plot the stock and explain each behaviour mode. Then describe forward Euler, its assumptions and error direction, and calculate the Euler stock at the stated DT.

Complete answer must contain: Net Flow = Inflow - Outflow; Stock = INTEG(Net Flow, Initial Stock); a stock rectangle, material flow pipes and an auxiliary net-flow variable. For linear sections use trapezoid area ((NF_start + NF_end)/2) x elapsed time. Plot stock at endpoints, zero crossings and slope changes. State: stock rises/falls by the sign of net flow, reaches a turning point where net flow is zero, and changes curvature where net flow changes direction.

Euler method: S(t+DT)=S(t)+NF(t) x DT. It treats net flow as constant over DT, therefore stock change as linear, and needs DT small enough for the approximation. Forward Euler uses the left endpoint: it overestimates a falling linear net flow and underestimates a rising linear net flow. Show the supplied support calculation as a sum of start-of-step rectangles, then compare it with the exact triangular/trapezoidal area.

Do not merge graphical integration and Euler: graphical integration uses the area under the changing net-flow curve; Euler uses rectangles at the stated DT. The paper repeatedly tests both distinctions.

2. Workload Backlog Model, Feedback Polarity and Policy Analysis

Frequency: all three current papers, normally a full question worth 25-26 marks.

  • 2023/24 Q1(a) [8]: infer reinforcing versus balancing one-stock structures from an accelerating-growth graph and a goal-seeking curve.
  • 2023/24 Q1(b,c) [10,7]: order-fulfilment backlog, work pressure, time per task, workweek and fatigue; show polarities and identify the workweek side-effect loop.
  • 2024/25 Q1(a) [5]: define feedback and its two types; explain why feedback structures improve stock-flow models.
  • 2024/25 Q1(b,c) [12,8]: Assignment Backlog with Allocated, Rework and Completed; name three negative policy loops and two positive side-effect loops, tracing each from increasing backlog.
  • 2025/26 Q3(a) [6]: define link polarity, loop polarity and feedback from the environmental-quality example, then discuss generated behaviour.
  • 2025/26 Q3(b,c) [12,7]: Claims Backlog with New Claims, Reworked Claims and Claims Completed; identify three policy loops and three side-effect loops and recommend a response.

Construct a signed stock-flow model of a backlog under work pressure. Identify intended balancing policies and reinforcing side-effects; prove each loop polarity by tracing an increase through the full closed loop; then discuss the best policy response.

Construction: first write the stock equation, for example Backlog = INTEG(New Work + Rework - Completed, Initial Backlog). Use pipes for named inflows/outflows. Add Work Pressure, Time per Task/Claim, Workweek, Temporary Staff, Fatigue and quality/rework only as auxiliaries and signed information links.

Polarity proof: a positive link means variables move in the same direction; a negative link means opposite directions, all else equal. A loop with an odd number of negative links is balancing; an even number is reinforcing. Still write the verbal increase-trace: a rising backlog raises pressure, a policy raises completion, and completion lowers backlog, so the final effect opposes the initial rise.

Policy answer: distinguish immediate balancing capacity responses from delayed reinforcing side-effects. Shorter time per task can raise rework; longer workweek can raise fatigue and reduce completion; untrained temporary staff can reduce productivity. Recommend a policy only after naming its mechanism, delay, intended outcome and quality/capacity trade-off.

3. SIR/Public-Health Calculations, Proofs and Extensions

Frequency: all three current papers. The common core is SIR, but each paper preserves a materially different extension.

  • 2023/24 Q3(a) [10]: explain infection and recovery equations and derive the equilibrium condition c i d = 1, assuming S=N.
  • 2023/24 Q3(b) [15]: build a seven-stock SEI2H2R model with asymptomatic/symptomatic infection, hospital delay, different infectiousness and equations.
  • 2024/25 Q3(a) [5]: calculate force of infection and herd-immunity threshold from N=10,000, 2% infectious, R0=12, D=7.
  • 2024/25 Q3(b) [15]: SIR plus quarantine fraction, control flag, equations, feedbacks and explanation of force of infection.
  • 2024/25 Q3(c) [5]: modify the model for young and elderly cohorts.
  • 2025/26 Q2(b) [12]: SIR countermeasures: 20% of infection flow to quarantine, vaccinated stock, goal-seeking vaccination fraction, attack rate, equations and feedbacks.
  • 2025/26 Q2(c) [5]: bubble plot of final attack rate by quarantine fraction and final vaccination fraction.

Build or extend a compartmental infection model with equations and feedbacks, calculate/derive the requested reproduction or threshold measure, and explain exactly how an intervention changes transmission, recovery or susceptibility.

Core construction: lambda = beta I/N, Infections = lambda S = beta I S/N, Recoveries = I/D; then S=INTEG(-Infections,S0), I=INTEG(Infections-Recoveries,I0), R=INTEG(Recoveries,R0). If paper notation is c, i, d, use beta=c i and R0=c i d. Explain units: lambda is per susceptible per time; infection/recovery flows are people per time.

Required proof pattern: at the infection peak, set dI/dt=0, so beta I S/N=I/D. For I>0, divide by I and use R0=beta D, giving S/N=1/R0. In a wholly susceptible equilibrium, set S=N to obtain c i d=1; an outbreak grows when the corresponding reproduction number exceeds 1.

Extension distinction: 2024/25 moves a stated fraction of Infectious people to quarantine and uses a 0/1 control flag. 2025/26 explicitly splits the infection flow: To Q=QF lambda S, To I=(1-QF) lambda S, and quarantined people do not infect. Do not silently replace one construction with the other. Vaccination is a flow from S to Vaccinated; if its fraction is goal-seeking, give its adjustment equation. For cohorts, replicate S/I/R by group and specify within/between-group infection terms or a contact matrix.

4. Stock Management, Staff Recruitment and Ageing Chains

Frequency: all three current papers, with 17-24 marks of direct stock-management work.

  • 2023/24 Q2(b) [5]: describe anchor and adjustment and identify the general structure used for each.
  • 2023/24 Q2(c) [12]: consulting startup with Rookie and Experienced stocks, 12-month progression delay, attrition and target-driven recruitment.
  • 2024/25 Q2(b) [7]: extend adjustment-to-goal to stock management, describe anchor/adjustment and show anchor delay.
  • 2024/25 Q2(c) [12]: University staff recruitment: Staff starts at 500, Students 10,000, desired ratio falls 20 to 10 at time 10; predict staff and identify settling-speed auxiliary.
  • 2025/26 Q4(a) [5]: add expected University enrolments to a supplied stock-flow model with equations.
  • 2025/26 Q4(b) [5]: compare adjustment-to-goal and stock management, stating their key difference, anchor and adjustment.
  • 2025/26 Q4(c,d) [10,5]: Trainee, Experienced and Senior pharmaceutical workforce, stage attrition/progression, then target drops 300 to 100 and equations/behaviour must be sketched.

Explain stock management as expected replacement plus correction of the desired-actual gap, then construct a workforce model with equations, feedbacks, delays and the response to a changed target.

Core equations: Discrepancy=Desired Stock-Actual Stock; Adjustment=Discrepancy/Adjustment Time; Expected Losses=SMOOTH(Actual Losses, Expectation Adjustment Time); Required Inflow=Expected Losses+Adjustment. The anchor replaces expected normal losses, whereas adjustment closes a current target gap. At equilibrium, adjustment is zero and required inflow equals expected loss.

University construction: Desired Staff=Students/Desired Student-Staff Ratio; Staff=INTEG(Hires-Quits,500); Quits=Quit Fraction x Staff; Hires=Expected Quits+(Desired Staff-Staff)/Staff Adjustment Time. At 10,000 students and ratio 20, desired staff is 500. A ratio of 10 changes target to 1,000, so Staff rises towards 1,000 at a decreasing rate. The staff adjustment time most directly sets response speed.

Ageing-chain construction: add progression flows Trainees/12 and Experienced/36 (or the paper's stated delays), one attrition flow per stage, and Total Staff as the sum. A lower target makes adjustment negative; if no dismissal flow exists, bound hires at zero and explain that attrition/progression determine the reduction speed.

5. Fractional Rates, Adjustment to a Goal and Smoothing

Frequency: directly examined in 2023/24 to 2025/26, but with distinct formulations.

  • 2023/24 Q2(a) [8]: build a stock-flow structure that produces a smoothed version of a random input and explain adjustment time.
  • 2024/25 Q2(a) [6]: describe with examples fractional increase rate, fractional decrease rate and adjustment to a goal.
  • 2025/26 Q2(a) [8]: modify a fractional decrease flow where decrease fraction G is itself an adjustment-to-goal structure approaching maximum G*; comment on a real context.

State the flow formula, units and expected behaviour for fractional growth/decline or adjustment; when a rate itself changes, make that rate a goal-seeking state rather than treating it as an instant constant.

Formulas: Increase=Stock x Fractional Increase Rate, Decrease=Stock x Fractional Decrease Rate, Adjustment=(Goal-Actual)/Adjustment Time. A fraction has units 1/time, so the resulting flow has stock units/time. Constant fractional rates produce exponential behaviour.

Smoothing/variable rate: Smoothed=INTEG((Input-Smoothed)/AT, Initial Smoothed). For the 2025/26 varying decrease fraction, use G=INTEG((G*-G)/AT,G0) and Outflow=S x G. State that larger AT responds more slowly and smooths more strongly; do not call it a simple moving average.
