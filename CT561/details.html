Exam Pattern

Exam Repetition

High. CT561 is predictable at both topic and question-type level. The scenario changes, but the required moves stay very similar: draw a stock-flow model, identify feedback, do simple integration, explain a system-dynamics structure, or extend an SIR/public-health model.

  • Most reviewed papers ask 3 questions out of 4.
  • All questions are equally weighted.
  • 2024/25 and 2025/26 keep the same broad structure.
  • The paper consistently rewards knowing reusable model patterns rather than memorising one-off facts.
  • Past papers are useful because the starred lecturer has stayed stable across the whole reviewed archive.
Repetitive Topics To Drill
  • Stock-and-flow modelling is the core repeat area: stocks, inflows, outflows, auxiliaries, equations and translating text scenarios into models.
  • Feedback loops recur heavily: link polarity, loop polarity, balancing/reinforcing loops, workload pressure, correction loops and side effects.
  • Graphical/Euler integration is high-value: net-flow calculation, stock graphs, DT choice, integration error and why smaller DT improves approximation.
  • SIR/public-health modelling is one of the safest targets: infection/recovery flows, force of infection, R0, herd immunity and extensions such as vaccination or quarantine.
  • Stock management and workforce models recur: anchor/adjustment structures, recruitment, workforce pipelines, aging chains, target stock and adjustment time.
  • Supporting marks often come from Vensim terminology, validation, policy sensitivity, equilibrium, Little's Law, delays and smoothing.

  • Formulate, test and analyse simulation models and policy sensitivity.
  • Build, test and evaluate models using Vensim.

Exam / CA Strategy
  • This is 70% exam and 30% CA.
  • The CA is useful but not enough to make this a CA-first module.
  • The exam is predictable enough that structured practice should pay off.
  • Highest-value preparation areas:
  • Stock vs flow definitions and stock equations.
  • Drawing stock and flow models from text.
  • Link polarity, loop polarity, feedback loops, positive vs negative loops.
  • Named workload loops and side-effect loops.
  • Net flow calculation and graphical integration from inflow/outflow charts.
  • Euler integration, DT choice, integration assumptions, overestimate/underestimate.
  • SIR model equations, R0, force of infection, herd immunity threshold, outbreak threshold.
  • SIR extensions: quarantine, vaccination, cohorts, hospitalisation, attack rate.
  • Stock management structure: anchor, adjustment, adjustment time, target stock, hire/recruitment rate.
  • Vensim terminology and simple model validation/sensitivity thinking.
  • Because this is now Semester 1, do not treat it as a later-year revision module. CA and exam preparation will likely arrive earlier than some historical Semester 2 context suggests.
Short Learning Material

Use Degreed/Percipio-style catalogues for short tutorials, guided projects, or video chapters. Prefer material under 3 hours per topic.

  • Systems thinking fundamentals: feedback loops, stocks, flows, delays and causal-loop diagrams.
  • System dynamics modelling: stock-flow diagrams, simulation steps and interpreting model behaviour.
  • Discrete-event simulation basics: events, queues, resources, arrivals and service times.
  • Monte Carlo simulation: random variables, sampling, confidence intervals and sensitivity analysis.
  • Queuing theory intro: M/M/1 intuition, utilisation, waiting time and bottlenecks.
  • Simulation verification/validation: calibration, assumptions, experimentation and reporting results.
