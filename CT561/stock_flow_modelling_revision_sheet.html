CT561 Topic Guide

Stock-and-Flow Modelling

Turn a verbal system into levels, rates, information links and equations before trying to simulate it.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Definitions/equation: stock(t+DT)=stock(t)+(inflows-outflows)DT; stocks have units, flows stock/time and auxiliaries convey information. Question cue: translate nouns to stocks, verbs/rates to flows, specify equations/units and initial conditions.

Worked practice 1 — paper-derived translation

Question demand: Model customers gained/lost. Plan/choice: one stock with fractional flows. Worked answer: Customers=INTEG(gained-lost,C0); gained=.10 Customers/time, lost=.03 Customers/time, so net=.07 Customers/time. Mark-winning points: units and initial value. Common trap: call sales/week a stock.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Build a resource-limited vaccination flow. Plan/choice: stocks Unvaccinated/Vaccinated; flow=min(people needing vaccine, vaccine supply, medic capacity). Worked answer: capacity=medics*productivity; VaccinesDispensed=min(Unvaccinated, Vaccines, capacity), update both stocks and state time units. Mark-winning points: physical constraint. Common trap: flow exceeds any resource.

Purpose and relevance

System dynamics represents how quantities accumulate and change through time. In CT561, a good stock-flow diagram is both an explanation and a model specification.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Underline quantities that exist at a time (stocks) and quantities measured per time (flows). Draw stocks first, add valves and clouds, then auxiliaries/information arrows. State units and write the stock equation.

Key terms and formulas

  • Stock/level: accumulated state, e.g. people in a queue [people].
  • Flow/rate: change per time, e.g. arrivals [people/day].
  • Auxiliary: calculated quantity; cloud: model boundary.
  • Conservation: Stock(t + DT) = Stock(t) + (inflow − outflow) × DT.
  • Each flow must have stock/time units; information arrows affect a rate but do not move material.

Method derivation

  1. Choose the system boundary and time unit.
  2. List accumulations; make each one a rectangle.
  3. For each physical transfer, draw an inflow/outflow and name the rate.
  4. Add drivers and equations; check every equation’s units.
  5. Write initial values and explain expected behaviour.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: A clinic starts with 80 waiting patients. Arrivals are 12/day and treatment is 10/day. Model one day.

Answer: One stock, Waiting patients, has an arrival inflow and treatment outflow. Its net flow is 2 patients/day, so after one day it is 80 + (12 − 10) × 1 = 82 patients. The treatment rate may depend on staff capacity, but capacity is an auxiliary unless it accumulates.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

A reservoir has 500 ML, rainfall adds 40 ML/day and demand removes 55 ML/day. Give the equation and value after two days. Check: 470 ML; distinguish the level from both daily rates.

Exam bridge

Draw and justify a stock-flow model for a software project’s work backlog, including rework. Check: include backlog, work completion, work discovery/rework, units, at least one information link, and assumptions.

High-value traps and corrections

Integrated paper-style practice - translate a verbal system [8 marks]

Scenario. A library has 500 available books. Borrowing is 40/day and returns equal 8% of books on loan per day. Specify stocks, flows and one feedback connection, then explain the first stock update if 100 books are on loan.

Question-demand diagnosis: distinguish accumulations from rates and make the conservation relation visible.

Fastest route: use Available and On-loan as stocks; Borrowing and Return as opposing flows.

Worked answer: Books on loan is a stock with inflow Borrowing and outflow Return; Available books is the complementary stock. Return=8 books/day initially. If borrowing remains 40/day, On-loan rises by 32 books in one day, while Available falls by 32. The availability stock can constrain borrowing, forming a balancing feedback: fewer available books limit new loans. Draw an information arrow for that dependence, not a material flow of “availability”.

Topic-specific trap: a rate such as borrowing/day is not itself a stock.

  • Drawing a causal arrow as though it transfers people or material.
  • Calling “sales per week” a stock.
  • Omitting initial conditions or mixing days and weeks.

Final answer checklist

  • Every stock has units and an initial value.
  • Every flow has stock/time units.
  • My stock equation matches the arrows.
