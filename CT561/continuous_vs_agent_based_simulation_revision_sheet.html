CT561 Topic Guide

Continuous versus Agent-Based Simulation

Choose the representation that preserves the decisions and interactions the question needs.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Compare: continuous stock-flow models aggregate levels/rates and feedback; agent-based models represent heterogeneous agents, rules, networks and emergent outcomes. Both simplify. Question cue: select representation from the decision, interactions/heterogeneity, data and validation burden.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style comparison

Question demand: Model city infection with identical mixing versus household contacts. Plan/choice: match assumptions to method. Worked answer: aggregate SIR suits homogeneous population/policy trends; ABM represents household/contact heterogeneity and local interventions but needs microdata and repeated stochastic runs. Mark-winning points: assumption and limitation. Common trap: people means ABM.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Defend a hybrid policy study. Plan/choice: use stock-flow for system capacity and ABM for targeted behaviour where necessary. Worked answer: calibrate aggregate flows, expose ABM rules/network assumptions, compare behaviours/sensitivity and report uncertainty; choose complexity only if decision changes. Mark-winning points: validation plan. Common trap: detail removes assumptions.

Purpose and relevance

CT561’s system-dynamics core treats aggregate stocks as continuously changing. Agent-based simulation represents individual entities and local rules; each reveals different mechanisms.

What a question really asks — answer plan

Describe the unit of representation, time/change mechanism, heterogeneity and interactions. Then choose a method from the decision question—not because one is more detailed—and state a limitation.

Key terms and contrasts

  • Continuous/system dynamics: aggregate stocks and rates, differential/Euler updates, feedback-focused.
  • Agent-based model (ABM): individual agents with state, behaviour, network/space and interaction rules.
  • Emergence: population pattern arising from local interactions.
  • Heterogeneity: agents may differ in attributes or behaviour.
  • Aggregation: summarising individuals can hide distributions and contact structure.

Method derivation

  1. Write the decision and outcome of interest.
  2. Ask whether individual identity, contact network, location or rules matter.
  3. If aggregate feedback dominates, specify stocks/rates; if local interactions dominate, specify agents/rules.
  4. Choose time step/event logic and data needed to calibrate it.
  5. Validate at the relevant scale and report what the abstraction omits.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Problem: Compare modelling flu transmission in a whole city with modelling vaccination uptake in friendship groups.

Answer: An aggregate SIR model can explain city-level infection/recovery feedback efficiently when mixing is a reasonable approximation. An ABM is better for uptake through friendship ties because individual beliefs and network clusters matter. A hybrid may use an ABM for contacts and aggregate capacity stocks.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

Which method better answers “how does total warehouse inventory respond to a supplier delay?” Check: usually continuous stock-flow, unless individual order priorities are central.

Exam bridge

Compare continuous and agent-based simulation for evacuation planning, with one requirement and limitation of each. Check: discuss entities, interaction/space, data and validation; do not say ABM is automatically more accurate.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Equating continuous with “no time step”.
  • Calling any model with people an ABM.
  • Claiming fine-grained agents remove assumptions.

Final answer checklist

  • I name the modelled entity and scale.
  • I link the choice to the decision question.
  • I give a credible limitation/data need.
