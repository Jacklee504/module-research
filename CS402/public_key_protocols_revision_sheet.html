Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Diffie-Hellman and ElGamal appear in the 2017/18-2024/25 archive, most often as a Q3/Q4 numerical protocol part.

(a) In Z*29 with g=2, Alice chooses x=5 and Bob y=4. Find public values and shared key. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Compute A=25, B=24 mod 29, then B5 (or A4) by squaring.

Mark-aware answer: A=3 and B=16. The shared key is 165=25 mod 29, equal to 34. Alice and Bob publish A,B, not x,y.

Trap: Do not transmit the shared key or treat g as secret.

(b) Describe ElGamal key generation, encryption and decryption in a cyclic group. [6 marks]

Question demand: Name g,x,h=gx; use fresh r; show the inverse cancellation. Quickest route: Name g,x,h=gx; use fresh r; show the inverse cancellation.

Mark-aware answer: Publish (G,g,h) with h=gx. Encrypt m using fresh r as (c1,c2)=(gr,m hr). Decrypt c2(c1x)-1=m because c1x=grx=hr.

Trap: Reusing r can leak relations between messages.

(c) Explain the man-in-the-middle weakness of unauthenticated Diffie-Hellman. [4 marks]

Question demand: Identify the substituted public values and the two different keys. Quickest route: Identify the substituted public values and the two different keys.

Mark-aware answer: An attacker can replace Alice's A with EA to Bob and Bob's B with EB to Alice. The attacker then shares one key with Alice and another with Bob, relaying/decrypting/re-encrypting traffic. Authentication binds public values to identities.

Trap: DH alone establishes a key, not peer identity.
