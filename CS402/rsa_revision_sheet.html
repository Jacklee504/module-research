Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction

Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: RSA is Q3 in every 2018/19-2024/25 paper: key generation, encryption/decryption, invalid-key checks and a security claim.

(a) For p=11, q=17, e=7, construct the key and encrypt m=8. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Compute n and phi; use extended Euclid for d; square-and-multiply 87 mod 187.

Mark-aware answer: n=187, phi=160 and 7-1=23 mod 160 because 7(23)-160=1. Public key (187,7), private d=23. 82=64, 84=169, so 87=169*64*8=134 mod 187.

Trap: Do not invert e modulo n, and do not say a primality test would factor n.

(b) Show why decrypting me with d returns m for every residue modulo pq. [6 marks]

Question demand: Use ed=1+k(p-1)(q-1); prove congruence separately mod p and mod q, then apply CRT. Quickest route: Use ed=1+k(p-1)(q-1); prove congruence separately mod p and mod q, then apply CRT.

Mark-aware answer: Modulo p, if p divides m both powers are 0; otherwise Fermat gives mp-1=1 and med=m. The same argument holds mod q. Since the two residues agree mod p and q, CRT gives med=m mod pq.

Trap: Euler's theorem alone only covers gcd(m,n)=1; include the divisible case.

(c) Explain why a fast primality test would not by itself break RSA. [4 marks]

Question demand: Separate deciding primality from factoring a composite modulus. Quickest route: Separate deciding primality from factoring a composite modulus.

Mark-aware answer: RSA uses public n=pq already known to be composite. A primality algorithm does not reveal p and q, whereas factoring n would yield phi(n) and hence d. Therefore the claim confuses primality testing with factorisation.

Trap: Do not say RSA depends on primality being hard to decide.
