(a) List the points of y2=x3+2x2+2x over F5 and identify an order-2 point. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Tabulate squares y2 in F5, evaluate the right side for x=0,...,4, add O, then use y=0 for order 2.

Mark-aware answer: Right sides are 0,0,1,3,1. Hence points are (0,0),(1,0),(2,+/-1),(4,+/-1),O. (0,0) and (1,0) have order 2 because each equals its own inverse.

Trap: Do not forget O or assume every x gives two points.

(b) State the exceptional cases in P+Q on an elliptic curve. [6 marks]

Question demand: Separate O, inverse points, distinct x values, and doubling. Quickest route: Separate O, inverse points, distinct x values, and doubling.

Mark-aware answer: O is the identity. If Q=-P, then P+Q=O. For P!=Q with xP!=xQ, use slope (yQ-yP)/(xQ-xP); for P=Q with yP!=0 use (3xP2+a)/(2yP). If yP=0, then 2P=O.

Trap: Division means multiplication by a nonzero inverse in the field.

(c) Why is the discriminant/non-singularity condition needed? [4 marks]

Question demand: State what failure creates and its effect on the group law. Quickest route: State what failure creates and its effect on the group law.

Mark-aware answer: If the cubic has a repeated root, the curve is singular (a cusp or node). The usual chord-and-tangent formulas no longer give the intended elliptic-curve group. Non-singularity ensures a well-defined abelian group with O.

Trap: Do not treat every cubic equation as an elliptic curve.
