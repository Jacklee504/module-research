1. Classical Ciphers And Key Spaces

FrontBackEvidence
How do you decode a 27-character shift cipher from one known mapping?Use A=0, …, Z=25, _=26. With c≡p+k (mod 27), find k≡c−p (mod 27) from the mapping, then decrypt every symbol by p≡c−k (mod 27). Show k before decoding.Q1(a), 2023/24–2025/26
What is the method for 2 × 2 Hill-cipher decryption over Zn?For K=[[a,b],[c,d]], compute det(K)=ad−bc. If gcd(det(K),n)=1, calculate K−1=det(K)−1[[d,−b],[−c,a]] modulo n. Multiply each ciphertext column pair by K−1 and convert the residues back.Q1(b), recurring
When is a Hill matrix a valid key?Exactly when det(K) is a unit modulo n; equivalently, gcd(det(K),n)=1. Otherwise K has no inverse modulo n and cannot decrypt uniquely.2025/26 Q1(c)
State Kerckhoff's principle.A cryptosystem should remain secure even if everything about the system is public except the secret key. Security must not depend on keeping the algorithm secret.2018/19, 2024/25
How many keys does a 1-dimensional affine cipher over Zn have?Encryption is x↦ax+b modulo n. The multiplier a must be a unit, giving φ(n) choices; b is arbitrary, giving n choices. Therefore the key space has φ(n)n elements.Q1(d), 2018/19–2025/26
How does Vigenère work over the CS402 27-character alphabet?Repeat the key to the message length, represent both strings in Z27, and encrypt positionwise by ci≡pi+ki (mod 27). Decrypt by subtraction.2023/24 Q1(c)

2. Symmetric Cryptosystems, Perfect Security, And Shannon

FrontBackEvidence
Formally define a symmetric cryptosystem.A symmetric cryptosystem consists of non-empty finite sets P of plaintexts, C of ciphertexts, and K of keys, with maps ek:P→C and dk:C→P for every k∈K, such that dk(ek(p))=p for every p∈P and k∈K.Q2 definition
Define perfect security for a symmetric cryptosystem.For every p∈P and every c∈C with Pr(C=c)>0, observing c does not change the probability of p: Pr(P=p ∣ C=c)=Pr(P=p). Equivalently, the ciphertext gives no information about the plaintext.Q2 perfect security
Do perfectly secure cryptosystems exist? Give the standard example.Yes. The one-time pad is perfectly secure when the key is chosen uniformly at random, is as long as the message, is used once, and encryption combines plaintext and key in the same finite alphabet or group.2022/23-2025/26
State Shannon's theorem on perfect security.For a finite perfectly secure symmetric cryptosystem in which every plaintext and ciphertext has positive probability, |K|≥|C|≥|P|.Q2 Shannon
Prove |K|≥|C|≥|P| for perfect security.For each k, ek:P→C is injective, so |C|≥|P|. Fix a positive-probability p. For every positive-probability c, perfect security gives Pr(P=p∣C=c)=Pr(P=p)>0, so some key sends p to c. One key sends fixed p to one ciphertext, so |K|≥|C|.2017/18, 2024/25
Compare block ciphers and stream ciphers.A block cipher encrypts fixed-size blocks using a key, often with a mode of operation; examples include AES or DES. A stream cipher generates a keystream and combines it symbol-by-symbol with plaintext, usually by XOR over binary alphabets; examples include LFSR-based stream ciphers or A5/1.2018/19, 2024/25

3. Linear Feedback Shift Registers

FrontBackEvidence
Define an LFSR of length L.An LFSR over a field, usually Z2, has an L-symbol state and generates a sequence from (s0,…,sL−1) by a linear recurrence. In the CS402 convention, sn+L=c0sn+…+cL−1sn+L−1 over Z2.2024/25–2025/26 Q2
What is an LFSR connection polynomial?It encodes the recurrence: C(X)=c0+c1X+…+cLXL, normally with c0=cL=1. A non-zero lower coefficient identifies a previous sequence term used in the feedback. For example, 1+X2+X3 gives sn+3=sn+sn+2 over Z2.2025/26 Q2(b)(ii)
How do you recover an LFSR keystream from known plaintext and ciphertext in a binary stream cipher?Use si=xi+yi mod 2, because encryption and decryption use XOR/addition in Z2. Then fit the length-L recurrence to the recovered keystream bits to identify the connection polynomial. State the recurrence convention being used.2022/23, 2023/24
How do you find an LFSR's least period?Generate full L-bit states until the initial state repeats; the number of steps is the least period, not the first repeated output bit. For 2025/26, 101→010→100→001→011→111→110→101, so the period is 7.Q2(c)/Q4(b), 2021/22–2025/26

4. RSA And Public-Key Cryptography

FrontBackEvidence
Compare symmetric and public-key cryptosystems.Symmetric cryptography uses one shared secret, e.g. AES: it is fast but needs secure key distribution. Public-key cryptography uses a public/private pair, e.g. RSA or ElGamal: it simplifies distribution but is slower and relies on factoring or discrete logarithms. Diffie–Hellman provides key exchange, not encryption.2022/23–2023/24 Q3(a)
Describe the complete RSA lifecycle.Choose distinct primes p,q; set n=pq and φ(n)=(p−1)(q−1). Choose e with 1<e<φ(n) and gcd(e,φ(n))=1; find d with ed ≡ 1 mod φ(n). Publish (n,e); keep d secret. Encrypt m∈Zn as c=me mod n, and decrypt as m=cd mod n.Q3(a), 2023/24–2025/26
How do you test an RSA public key (n,e)?Factor n. Standard RSA requires n=pq for distinct primes. Compute φ(n)=(p−1)(q−1) and check gcd(e,φ(n))=1.Q3(b), 2024/25–2025/26
How do you calculate an RSA ciphertext?For public key (n,e) and message m∈Zn, calculate c≡me (mod n) by repeated squaring. Show the modular-power working as well as the final c.Q3(c), 2024/25–2025/26
Why does factorisation break RSA but primality testing alone not?Factoring public n=pq reveals p,q, hence φ(n) and d≡e−1 (mod φ(n)), enabling decryption. Primality testing only decides whether a candidate is prime; it does not find the factors of n.Q3(d), 2023/24–2025/26

5. Number Theory, Primality, And Cyclic Groups

FrontBackEvidence
Describe fast exponentiation for ae mod n.Write e in binary or repeatedly square. Maintain a running product modulo n; square the base each step and multiply it into the product when the current binary digit of e is 1. Reduce modulo n after every multiplication.Fast exponentiation
Describe Fermat's primality test.For input n>1, choose 1≤a≤n−1. If gcd(a,n)>1, declare n composite. Otherwise calculate an−1 mod n: if it is not 1, declare composite; if it is 1, state only that n passes this base and is probably prime.2018/19, 2024/25, 2025/26
Define pseudoprime in the Fermat-test context.A composite n is a pseudoprime to base a if gcd(a,n)=1 and an−1≡1 mod n, so it passes Fermat’s test for that base despite being composite.2024/25 Q3(d)
Define a Carmichael number.A composite n is a Carmichael number if an−1≡1 mod n for every a coprime to n. Equivalently, it fools Fermat’s test for all coprime bases.2018/19, 2024/25
Why can Fermat's test still detect a Carmichael number as composite sometimes?If the chosen base a is not coprime to Carmichael number n, then gcd(a,n)>1 reveals a non-trivial factor and the test can declare composite. Carmichael numbers fool Fermat only for coprime bases.2021/22, 2023/24
Define a cyclic group.A group G is cyclic if there exists g∈G such that every element of G is a power of g; then g is a generator and G=⟨g⟩.Q4 cyclic groups
State Gauss's characterisation for Zn* being cyclic.Zn* is cyclic exactly when n is 1, 2, 4, pk, or 2pk, where p is an odd prime and k≥1.Q4 cyclic groups
Apply Gauss's characterisation to the recurring examples.Zn* is cyclic for n=121=112 and n=169=132 by the pk case, and for n=242=2·112 by the 2pk case. It is not cyclic for n=315=32·5·7, which is none of Gauss’s forms.2021/22–2025/26

6. Diffie-Hellman And ElGamal

FrontBackEvidence
Describe Diffie–Hellman key exchange.Publicly choose cyclic G=⟨g⟩. Alice chooses secret a and sends ga; Bob chooses secret b and sends gb. Each computes the same key gab.Q4(b/d), 2023/24–2025/26
Describe the complete ElGamal construction.In cyclic G=⟨g⟩, Bob chooses private b and publishes (G,g,h=gb). To encrypt m∈G, Alice chooses fresh random r and sends (c1,c2)=(gr,mhr). Bob computes c1b=hr and recovers m=c2(c1b)−1 in G.Q4(c/d), 2022/23–2025/26

7. Elliptic Curves

FrontBackEvidence
What points are on an elliptic curve over a finite field in these papers?For a curve y2=f(x) over Zp, test every x∈Zp, calculate f(x) mod p, and include each y whose square equals that value modulo p, plus the point at infinity O.2022/23, 2024/25
What is the point at infinity O?O is the identity element of the elliptic-curve group: P+O=P for every point P. The inverse of P=(x,y) over a field is −P=(x,−y).Elliptic curves
How do you identify non-identity order-2 points on y2=f(x)?A non-identity point P has order 2 iff P+P=O, equivalently P=−P. Since −(x,y)=(x,−y), this requires y=0. Solve f(x)=0 and list (x,0). O+O=O too, but O has order 1, not 2.2022/23, 2024/25
For the real curves in the latest papers, find all points satisfying P+P=O, and give a point with non-zero double.O+O=O, but O has order 1; a non-identity point has order 2 precisely when y=0. For y2=x3+3x, the full answer is O,(0,0); Q=(1,2) lies on the curve and has Q+Q≠O. For y2=x3−x, the full answer is O,(-1,0),(0,0),(1,0).2023/24 Q4(e), 2025/26 Q4(d)
Describe elliptic-curve addition, including exceptional cases.For P,Q with Q≠−P, take the third curve intersection of their line and reflect it in the x-axis. For P=Q, use the tangent. Also, O is the identity, −(x,y)=(x,−y), and P+(−P)=O; a vertical tangent gives 2P=O.2018/19, 2025/26 Q4(d)(i)

FrontBackEvidence
How do you solve an affine cipher key from two known plaintext/ciphertext pairs?Write c1=ap1+b mod n and c2=ap2+b mod n. Subtract to obtain c1−c2=a(p1−p2) mod n; if the difference is invertible, solve for a, substitute for b, then check gcd(a,n)=1.2018/19 Q1(b)
State Euler's theorem.If gcd(a,n)=1, then aφ(n)≡1 mod n. It supports RSA correctness and modular-exponent reduction.2017/18, 2018/19
What is a brute-force attack, and when is it feasible?It tries every key until meaningful plaintext is found. It is feasible only when the key space is small, keys are quick to test, and correct plaintext can be recognised.2021/22, 2022/23
Give a singular LFSR of length 3.Over Z2, sn+3=sn+2 is singular: it does not depend on sn, so distinct states can have the same next state and the transition is not invertible.2021/22 Q2(c)
What are the points on y2=x3+2x2+2x over Z5?E(Z5)={O,(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,1),(3,4),(4,2),(4,3)}. The non-identity order-2 points are (0,0),(1,0),(2,0).2022/23 Q4(e)
What Enigma property made it stronger than Vigenere against ciphertext-only attacks?Enigma used a changing polyalphabetic substitution controlled by rotors, so the substitution changed after each character and produced a much longer and more complex period than a simple repeated Vigenere key.2017/18 Q3(c)
What is the key-space size for a d-dimensional affine cipher over an alphabet of p letters, with p prime?A key has the form v↦Av+B over Fpd, where A is invertible and B arbitrary. Thus there are |GLd(Fp)|pd=(pd−1)(pd−p)…(pd−pd−1)pd keys.2017/18 Q4
Define two-factor authentication.Authentication requiring two different categories of evidence, such as something the user knows, something the user has, or something the user is.2017/18 Q6(a)
Define forward security.Compromise of a long-term key should not reveal past session keys or past encrypted communications.2017/18 Q6(a)
What is A5/1?A5/1 is a GSM stream cipher based on three LFSRs with irregular majority-clocked stepping, historically used for over-the-air mobile phone traffic encryption.2017/18 Q7(b)
Prove the RSA correctness congruence from 2017/18 Q8(a).Let N=pq, gcd(a,N)=1, and d≡e−1 (mod (p−1)(q−1)). Then ed=1+tφ(N), so (ae)d=aed=a(aφ(N))t≡a (mod N) by Euler's theorem.2017/18 Q8(a)
Why does a non-Carmichael composite pass k independent Fermat tests with probability at most (1/2)k?For non-Carmichael composite m, bases falsely satisfying am−1≡1 (mod m) form a proper subgroup of Zm*, so there are at most half as many. Non-coprime bases reveal a factor. Thus k independent passes have probability at most (1/2)k.2017/18 Q8(b)
What is Pollard's rho method for factorisation trying to find?It iterates a polynomial modulo n to produce a pseudo-random sequence and compares pairs of iterates; a non-trivial gcd(|x_i-x_j|, n) gives a factor of n.2017/18 Q10(a)
