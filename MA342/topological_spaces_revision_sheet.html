MA342 Topic Guide

Topological Spaces

Make topology axioms and standard examples automatic.

Purpose

Recognise or construct a topology, especially on a finite set, and use its open sets precisely.Exam use: Underlying set, all three axioms, any counterexample family and complement-based closed sets are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Write the proposed family τ and the underlying set X.
  2. Check ∅, X, arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
  3. For a named topology, state which sets are open rather than relying on a picture.
  4. Question interpretation: For verify a topology, check empty/full, arbitrary unions and finite intersections; for refute, one explicit failed axiom is enough.

Definitions

A topology τ on X contains ∅ and X, is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections. Its members are open; complements of open sets are closed. The discrete topology is P(X); the cofinite topology has ∅ and sets with finite complement.Rapid recall: A topology tau on X contains empty and X, is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections; open/closed are topology-relative, and complements turn union statements into intersection statements.

Proof method

For finite candidate families, list the nontrivial unions and intersections. To disprove a topology, one failed axiom is enough.Exam workflow: List candidate sets systematically for finite X, use the strongest closure test first, and name the exact family whose union/intersection fails.

Worked simple example

On X={a,b}, τ={∅,{a},X} is a topology: it contains ∅ and X; every union is in τ; the only nontrivial intersection is {a}∩X={a}.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

On X={a,b,c}, let tau={empty,X,{a},{a,b},{a,c}}. It is not a topology because {a,b} intersect {a,c}={a}, which is present, but {a,b} union {a,c}=X is present; checking all pairs and unions shows it actually is a topology. Its closed sets are X,empty,{b,c},{c},{b}. The example illustrates why a guessed counterexample must be calculated rather than asserted.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Is {∅,{a},{b},X} a topology on {a,b}? Check: it is discrete.

2. Exam style: On a finite X with at least two points, show the cofinite topology is discrete. Check: each singleton has finite complement, so is open.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: find the topology generated by basis B={{1},{2,3}} on X={1,2,3}. Check arbitrary unions give {empty,{1},{2,3},X}; basis elements cover X and their intersections satisfy the basis condition.

Practice question - verify topology axioms [8 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2024/25 Q1(a-b). Task. On X={1,2,3}, decide whether tau={∅,X,{1},{2,3}} is a topology and list its closed sets. [8 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: check arbitrary unions and finite intersections; here tau is generated by two complementary basic opens.

Worked answer: ∅ and X belong to tau. Any union of members is again one of ∅,{1},{2,3},X. Finite intersections are also among those four sets: notably {1}∩{2,3}=∅. Thus tau is a topology. Taking complements in X gives closed sets X,∅,{2,3},{1}, respectively, so the closed-family is the same four sets.

Topic-specific trap: finite intersections are required, but arbitrary intersections need not be open.

Mistakes

  • Checking only unions of two sets and forgetting the arbitrary-union axiom.
  • Calling every subset open in the cofinite topology on an infinite set.
  • Confusing a basis with the full topology.
  • Recovery rule: A family can pass several sample unions and still fail an arbitrary-union axiom; on finite families, enumerate all distinct unions.

Checklist

  • I can state all topology axioms.
  • I can distinguish discrete, indiscrete and cofinite examples.
  • I can give one decisive counterexample to an axiom.
  • Final 60-second audit: Underlying set, all three axioms, any counterexample family and complement-based closed sets are explicit.
