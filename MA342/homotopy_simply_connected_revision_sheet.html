MA342 Topic Guide

Homotopy and Simply Connected Spaces

Make deformation arguments explicit through a continuous parameter.

Purpose

State a homotopy precisely, construct elementary contractions, and distinguish simple connectedness from mere connectedness.Exam use: Formula, continuity, image containment, endpoint maps and base/path conditions appear.

Question plan

  1. Identify the two maps f,g:X→Y.
  2. Give H:X×[0,1]→Y with H(x,0)=f(x), H(x,1)=g(x).
  3. Check H remains in Y and is continuous.
  4. Question interpretation: For homotopic, supply a continuous H(x,t) with both endpoint maps; for simply connected, include path-connectedness and contract every based loop.

Definitions

f and g are homotopic if such a continuous H exists. X is simply connected if it is path connected and every loop is homotopic to a constant loop. A star-shaped subset of Rⁿ is contractible, hence simply connected.Rapid recall: A homotopy H:Xx[0,1]->Y satisfies H(x,0)=f(x), H(x,1)=g(x). A contraction is a homotopy from id_X to a constant; contractible spaces are simply connected when nonempty.

Proof method

For a star-shaped set with centre x₀, use H(x,t)=(1−t)x+tx₀. The line segment condition keeps H in X; polynomial operations give continuity.Exam workflow: Write the candidate formula, verify it stays inside the target, prove continuity, and check both t=0 and t=1 exactly.

Worked simple example

R² is contractible: H(x,t)=(1−t)x deforms the identity to the constant map at 0.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Show the unit disk D^2 is contractible. Define H(x,t)=(1-t)x. Since ||H(x,t)||=(1-t)||x||<=1, the image remains in D^2; scalar multiplication is continuous; H(x,0)=x and H(x,1)=0. Therefore id_D is homotopic to the constant map, so every loop contracts and D^2 is simply connected.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Show any two constant maps X→R are homotopic. Check: linearly interpolate their values.

2. Exam style: Prove a star-shaped U⊆Rⁿ is simply connected. Check: contract U to its star centre, then contract loops.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: explain why S^1 is not contractible using the supplied theorem that a retraction D^2->S^1 cannot exist. Check if S^1 contracted, the standard extension argument would construct such a retraction; clearly state dependence on the no-retraction theorem rather than treating a picture as proof.

Practice question - explicit contraction [8 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2024/25 Q4(e). Task. Show the unit disk D2 is contractible, then state the simply-connected conclusion. [8 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: provide a continuous homotopy that stays in D2, with both endpoint conditions.

Worked answer: Define H:D2×[0,1]→D2 by H(x,t)=(1-t)x. It is continuous. If ||x||≤1 then ||H(x,t)||=(1-t)||x||≤1, so it has the stated codomain. H(x,0)=x and H(x,1)=0, the constant map at the origin. Thus idD2 is homotopic to a constant map, so D2 is contractible. Every loop contracts by composing its homotopy with this contraction, so D2 is simply connected.

Topic-specific trap: contracting in the surrounding plane is insufficient unless the entire homotopy remains inside the space.

Mistakes

  • Giving a family H without endpoint checks.
  • Failing to prove H takes values in the target space.
  • Calling a connected space simply connected without addressing loops.
  • Recovery rule: A formula that leaves the space is not a homotopy in that space; verify the codomain condition for every t.

Checklist

  • I can write the domain and endpoint equations of H.
  • I can contract a star-shaped set.
  • I know S¹ is connected but not simply connected.
  • Final 60-second audit: Formula, continuity, image containment, endpoint maps and base/path conditions appear.
