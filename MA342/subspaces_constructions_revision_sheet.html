Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Subspace/product/quotient constructions are a 2023/24-2024/25 Q1/Q4 core, including S1 as a quotient of [0,1].

(a) Show S1 is a quotient of [0,1]. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Use q(t)=exp(2 pi i t); it is a continuous surjection and identifies exactly 0 and 1, so the quotient map induces a bijection from [0,1]/{0~1} to S1.

Mark-aware answer: The induced bijection is a homeomorphism by the quotient topology (or compact-to-Hausdorff argument). Thus S1 is the quotient identifying endpoints.

Trap: Do not call an arbitrary continuous surjection a quotient without the topology condition.

(b) Show the subspace topology on Z inherited from R is discrete. [6 marks]

Question demand: For each integer n, exhibit an open R-set whose intersection with Z is {n}. Quickest route: For each integer n, exhibit an open R-set whose intersection with Z is {n}.

Mark-aware answer: For n in Z, (n-1/2,n+1/2) is open in R and its intersection with Z is {n}. Hence every singleton is open in the subspace; arbitrary unions of singletons show every subset of Z is open.

Trap: The singletons are not open in R itself; specify the subspace.

(c) Write a basis for the product topology on X x Y. [4 marks]

Question demand: Use products of opens, not arbitrary subsets of the Cartesian product. Quickest route: Use products of opens, not arbitrary subsets of the Cartesian product.

Mark-aware answer: {U x V : U open in X, V open in Y} is a basis. It covers XxY, and a point in two basis products lies in (U1 intersect U2)x(V1 intersect V2), another basis set inside the intersection.

Trap: A x B is not automatically basic unless A,B are open.
