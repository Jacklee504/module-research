Priority 1 - Core Topology, Subspaces, Bases, Closure, And Interior

Named Topologies

FrontBackEvidence
Define a topology on X.A collection τ of subsets of X such that ∅ and X belong to τ, arbitrary unions of members of τ belong to τ, and finite intersections of members of τ belong to τ.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
What is the standard topology on R?The topology whose open sets are arbitrary unions of ordinary open intervals (a,b).2025/26
What is the cofinite topology on X?The topology whose open sets are ∅ together with all subsets U⊆X for which X\U is finite.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
What is the discrete topology on X?The topology P(X), in which every subset of X is open.2024/25, 2025/26
How can you show the standard and cofinite topologies on R are different?The interval (0,1) is open in the standard topology. Its complement R\(0,1) is infinite, so (0,1) is not open in the cofinite topology. Therefore the two topologies differ.2025/26

Subspace Topology And Discreteness

FrontBackEvidence
What is the subspace topology on A⊆X?The open sets of A are precisely the sets A∩U, where U is open in X. Thus V⊆A is open in A if and only if V=A∩U for some open U⊆X.2023/24, 2025/26
When is a subspace discrete?A subspace A is discrete if and only if every singleton {a}, with a∈A, is open in the subspace topology. Equivalently, every subset of A is open.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
Why is Q not discrete in its usual subspace topology?Every ordinary open interval containing q∈Q contains another rational. Therefore no interval intersects Q in {q}, so {q} is not open in Q.2023/24
Why is Z discrete in its usual subspace topology?For every n∈Z, (n−1/2,n+1/2)∩Z={n}. Thus every singleton of Z is open, so Z is discrete.2024/25
Why is [0,1] not discrete in its usual subspace topology?Every ordinary open interval containing 1/2 meets [0,1] in points other than 1/2. Thus {1/2} is not open in [0,1], so [0,1] is not discrete.2025/26

Bases And The Lower-Limit Topology

FrontBackEvidence
What is a basis, and what is the lower-limit basis on R?A basis B is a collection of open sets such that every open set is a union of members of B. For the lower-limit topology on R, B={[a,b): a,b∈R and a<b}.2023/24, 2024/25
What does clopen mean, and why is [0,1) clopen in the lower-limit topology?Clopen means both open and closed. [0,1) is a basic open set. Its complement is (−∞,0)∪[1,∞)=⋃n≥1[−n,0)∪⋃n≥1[1,n+1), so it is open; hence [0,1) is closed as well.2024/25

Neighbourhoods, Interior, Closure, And Accumulation

FrontBackEvidence
What is a neighbourhood, and how does it characterise interior?N⊆X is a neighbourhood of x if some open U satisfies x∈U⊆N. The interior A° is the largest open subset of A; equivalently, x∈A° if and only if A is a neighbourhood of x.2024/25, 2025/26
What is the closure of A?The closure cl(A) is the intersection of all closed subsets of X containing A. Equivalently, x∈cl(A) if and only if every neighbourhood of x meets A.2023/24, 2025/26
What is an accumulation point of A?A point x such that every neighbourhood of x contains a point of A other than x; equivalently, every neighbourhood of x meets A\{x}. Unlike a closure point, x itself cannot be the only point of A met by the neighbourhood.2023/24, 2025/26
Why does having no accumulation points imply that A is closed?Let x∈X\A. Since x is not an accumulation point of A, some neighbourhood N of x satisfies N∩(A\{x})=∅. As x∉A, N∩A=∅. Choose an open U with x∈U⊆N; then U⊆X\A. Thus X\A is open, so A is closed.2025/26

Priority 2 - Compactness, Hausdorffness, Cofinite, And Lower-Limit Examples

Compactness And Non-Compact Examples

FrontBackEvidence
What does compact mean?A space X is compact if every open cover of X has a finite subcover: whenever X=⋃i∈IUi with every Ui open, there are finitely many i1,…,in with X=Ui₁∪…∪Uiₙ.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
Is standard R compact?No. The open sets (−n,n), n∈N, cover R. Any finite subfamily has a largest index N and is contained in (−N,N), so it misses points such as N+1.2023/24
How can you disprove compactness of the lower-limit line?The lower-limit open cover {[n,n+1):n∈Z} covers R. A finite subfamily covers only a bounded subset of R, so it cannot cover all of R; therefore the lower-limit line is not compact.2024/25, 2025/26

Cofinite Spaces

FrontBackEvidence
What does dense mean, and which sets are dense in a cofinite space?A⊆X is dense if cl(A)=X, equivalently if every nonempty open set meets A. In an infinite cofinite space, every infinite subset is dense: a nonempty open set has finite complement and therefore cannot be disjoint from an infinite A.2024/25, 2025/26
Is an infinite cofinite space compact?Yes. Given an open cover, choose a nonempty member U. Its complement X\U is finite; choose one cover member for each point of X\U. Together with U, these finitely many sets form a finite subcover.2023/24
Why is an infinite cofinite space not Hausdorff?Any two nonempty open sets have finite complements. Their intersection has complement contained in the union of two finite sets, so it is nonempty when X is infinite. Thus distinct points cannot have disjoint open neighbourhoods.2023/24, 2024/25

Hausdorff Separation

FrontBackEvidence
What does Hausdorff mean?A space X is Hausdorff if, for every distinct x,y∈X, there are disjoint open sets U,V with x∈U and y∈V.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
Why is the lower-limit topology on R Hausdorff?For x<y, [x,y) and [y,y+1) are disjoint basic open neighbourhoods of x and y. Therefore any two distinct points can be separated by disjoint open sets.2025/26

Compactness Theorems

FrontBackEvidence
Why is every closed subset of a compact space compact?If A is closed in compact X and {Ui} covers A, then {Ui}∪{X\A} is an open cover of X. A finite subcover exists; removing X\A leaves a finite subcover of A.2024/25
Why is a compact subspace of a Hausdorff space closed?Let A be compact in Hausdorff X and x∉A. For each a∈A choose disjoint open sets Ua∋a and Va∋x. Finitely many Ua₁,…,Uaₙ cover A. Then V=Va₁∩…∩Vaₙ is an open neighbourhood of x disjoint from A. Thus X\A is open and A is closed.2025/26
Why is a continuous bijection from compact X to Hausdorff Y a homeomorphism?For every closed F⊆X, F is compact, so f(F) is compact and hence closed in Hausdorff Y. Thus f is a closed bijection, which makes f−1 continuous. Since f is already continuous, it is a homeomorphism.2023/24

Priority 3 - Connectedness, Separations, Continuous Images, And IVT

Separations And Connectedness

FrontBackEvidence
What is a separation of X?A pair U,V of disjoint, nonempty, open subsets of X whose union is X: X=U∪V.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
What does connected mean?A space is connected if it has no separation; equivalently, its only clopen subsets are ∅ and X.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26

Connectedness Examples On Q

FrontBackEvidence
Why is discrete Q disconnected?{0} and Q\{0} are disjoint, nonempty, open sets whose union is Q.2025/26
Why is indiscrete Q connected?The only open sets are ∅ and Q, so there is no nonempty proper open set that could form a separation.2025/26
Why is Q with the usual subspace topology disconnected?Let U=Q∩(−∞,√2) and V=Q∩(√2,∞). They are nonempty, disjoint, relatively open in Q, and U∪V=Q because √2∉Q. Hence they form a separation.2025/26
Why is cofinite Q connected?Every two nonempty cofinite open subsets of the infinite set Q intersect. A separation would require two disjoint nonempty open sets, so no separation exists.2025/26

Continuous Images And The Intermediate Value Theorem

FrontBackEvidence
Why is a continuous surjective image of a connected space connected?Let f:X→Y be continuous and surjective, with X connected. If Y=U∪V were a separation, then f−1(U) and f−1(V) would be disjoint, nonempty, open sets covering X—a contradiction. Hence Y is connected.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
How does connectedness give the Intermediate Value Theorem?For continuous h:[a,b]→R, the interval [a,b] is connected, so h([a,b]) is connected and therefore an interval. Every value t between h(a) and h(b) lies in h([a,b]), so h(c)=t for some c∈[a,b].2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26

Priority 4 - Homeomorphisms And Invariants

Definition And Invariants

FrontBackEvidence
What is a homeomorphism?A bijection f:X→Y such that both f and f−1:Y→X are continuous. It is an isomorphism of topological spaces.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
What properties are used as homeomorphism invariants in these papers?Compactness and connectedness, including connectedness after deleting corresponding points.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26

Non-Homeomorphism Proofs

FrontBackEvidence
Why are R and [0,1] not homeomorphic?[0,1] is compact and R is not, and compactness is preserved by homeomorphisms.2024/25, 2025/26
Why are R2 and R not homeomorphic?If h:R→R2 were a homeomorphism, then h would restrict to a homeomorphism R\{0}→R2\{h(0)}. But R\{0} is disconnected, whereas R2\{h(0)} is path-connected. This contradiction proves they are not homeomorphic.2024/25
Why are S1 and [0,1] not homeomorphic, and when is point removal useful?If h:[0,1]→S1 were a homeomorphism, remove an interior point 1/2 and its image h(1/2). The space [0,1]\{1/2} is disconnected, whereas S1\{h(1/2)} is connected. This contradiction shows they are not homeomorphic. Point removal is useful when simpler invariants agree but punctured spaces have different connectedness.2023/24, 2025/26

Give a homeomorphism R→(0,∞) and its inverse.f(x)=ex is a continuous bijection from R to (0,∞), and f−1(y)=ln(y) is continuous. Therefore f is a homeomorphism.2024/25, 2025/26

Priority 5 - Product, Initial, Final, And Quotient Topologies

Product, Initial, And Final Topologies

FrontBackEvidence
What is a basis for the product topology on X×Y?The sets U×V, where U is open in X and V is open in Y, form a basis. The product-open sets are arbitrary unions of these rectangles.2023/24, 2024/25
Define the initial and final topologies.For maps fi:X→Yi, the initial topology on X is the coarsest topology making every fi continuous; it is generated by fi−1(U) for U open in Yi. For maps gi:Xi→Y, the final topology on Y is the finest topology making every gi continuous; W⊆Y is open exactly when every gi−1(W) is open in Xi.2024/25, 2025/26

Quotient Topology And The Circle

FrontBackEvidence
What is the quotient topology on X/~?Let q:X→X/~ send x to its equivalence class [x]. The quotient topology is the final topology for q: a set W⊆X/~ is open if and only if q−1(W) is open in X.2023/24, 2024/25
Why is S1 a quotient of [0,1], and how is the identification written?The map q(t)=(cos(2πt),sin(2πt)) is a continuous surjection [0,1]→S1. It identifies exactly 0 and 1, so it induces a continuous bijection [0,1]/(0~1)→S1. The quotient is compact and S1 is Hausdorff, so this bijection is a homeomorphism. Thus S1≅[0,1]/(0~1).2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26

Priority 6 - Cantor Set: Definition, Compactness, Images, And Quotients

Construction And Ternary Description

FrontBackEvidence
How is the Cantor set C constructed?Start with C0=[0,1]. At each stage remove the open middle third of every closed interval remaining from the previous stage. The Cantor set is C=⋂n≥0Cn.2024/25, 2025/26
How can the Cantor set be described using ternary expansions?C is the set of points in [0,1] having a ternary expansion that uses only the digits 0 and 2. Equivalently, its points are ∑n=1∞2an/3n, where each an is 0 or 1.2024/25, 2025/26

Topological Properties

FrontBackEvidence
Why is the Cantor set compact Hausdorff?Each Cn is closed in [0,1], so C=⋂Cn is closed in compact [0,1]; hence C is compact. As a subspace of the Hausdorff space R, C is Hausdorff.2025/26

Continuous Images And Quotient Obstructions

FrontBackEvidence
Which continuous-image directions hold between C and [0,1]?[0,1] is a continuous image of C: the restriction of the Cantor function sends ∑n=1∞2an/3n to ∑n=1∞an/2n and is surjective. Conversely, C cannot be a continuous image of [0,1], because a continuous image of connected [0,1] is connected, while C is disconnected.2023/24
What property rules out a quotient map from C onto standard R?A quotient map is a continuous surjection. Since C is compact, its continuous image would be compact; standard R is not compact. Therefore R cannot be a quotient of C.2025/26

Priority 7 - Homotopy And Star-Shaped/Convex Spaces

Homotopy Definitions

FrontBackEvidence
When are f,g:X→Y homotopic?They are homotopic if there is a continuous H:X×[0,1]→Y such that H(x,0)=f(x) and H(x,1)=g(x) for every x∈X. H is a homotopy from f to g.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
When are X and Y homotopy equivalent?When there are continuous maps f:X→Y and g:Y→X such that g∘f is homotopic to idX and f∘g is homotopic to idY.2023/24
What does simply connected mean?A space X is simply connected if it is path-connected and every loop in X is homotopic, while fixing its endpoints, to a constant loop. Equivalently, its fundamental group is trivial.2023/24

Constructing Homotopies

FrontBackEvidence
What straight-line homotopy works for maps into Rn, and why?For continuous f,g:X→Rn, set H(x,t)=(1−t)f(x)+tg(x). This is continuous, has H(x,0)=f(x) and H(x,1)=g(x), and stays in Rn; hence it is a homotopy.2024/25, 2025/26
For what more general targets does straight-line interpolation work?It works for a convex subset Y⊆Rn: whenever y0,y1∈Y and t∈[0,1], (1−t)y0+ty1 lies in Y. Thus the same formula gives a homotopy between maps into Y.2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26
How do you contract a star-shaped space with centre x0?S is star-shaped with centre x0 if, for every x∈S, the segment {(1−t)x+tx0:t∈[0,1]} lies in S. Then H(x,t)=(1−t)x+tx0 contracts S to x0; hence S is contractible and therefore simply connected.2023/24
