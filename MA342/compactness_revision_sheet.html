Compactness

Convert arbitrary open covers into finite arguments.

What the local papers support

Compactness is Q2 in both 2023/24 and 2024/25, with closed subsets of compact spaces and lower-limit/cofinite tests.

Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction
Compact means every open cover has a finite subcover. A closed subset F of compact X is compact. Continuous images of compact spaces are compact.To disprove compactness exhibit one cover with no finite subcover.

Medium/hard adapted practice

Paper family: Compactness is Q2 in both 2023/24 and 2024/25, with closed subsets of compact spaces and lower-limit/cofinite tests.

(a) Prove a closed F subset X is compact when X is compact. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Adjoin X\F to an arbitrary open cover of F, take a finite subcover of X, then discard X\F.

Mark-aware answer: If {U_i} covers F, {U_i} union {X\F} covers X. Compactness gives finitely many U_i plus perhaps X\F covering X; the finitely many U_i cover F. Hence F is compact.

Trap: Do not start from a particular cover when proving compactness; it must be arbitrary.

(b) Give an explicit open cover showing R is not compact in its usual topology. [6 marks]

Question demand: Use Un=(-n,n) and show any finite choice is bounded. Quickest route: Use Un=(-n,n) and show any finite choice is bounded.

Mark-aware answer: {(-n,n):n in N} covers R. A finite subfamily has largest index N, so its union is (-N,N), which misses N+1. Hence no finite subcover exists and R is not compact.

Trap: An unbounded set can be compact in another topology; name the usual topology.

(c) State the finite-subcover proof of compactness of [0,1] in R. [4 marks]

Question demand: Invoke Heine-Borel with its hypotheses. Quickest route: Invoke Heine-Borel with its hypotheses.

Mark-aware answer: [0,1] is closed and bounded in Euclidean R; by Heine-Borel it is compact. Equivalently, every open cover admits a finite subcover by the theorem.

Trap: Heine-Borel is a theorem about Euclidean spaces, not arbitrary topological spaces.
