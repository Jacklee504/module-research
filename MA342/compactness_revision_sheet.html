MA342 Topic Guide

Compactness

Convert arbitrary open covers into finite arguments.

Purpose

State compactness exactly and deploy the standard closed-subset and continuous-image proof patterns.Exam use: The cover is arbitrary when proving compactness, explicit when disproving it, and every theorem hypothesis is named.

Question plan

  1. Begin with an arbitrary open cover.
  2. Identify the finite subcover supplied by a known compact set.
  3. For a closed subset, add the open complement and remove it after selecting finitely many sets.
  4. Question interpretation: For prove compact, begin with an arbitrary open cover; for disprove, exhibit one explicit cover with no finite subcover; for preserve, name the continuous-image theorem and verify its hypotheses.

Definitions

X is compact iff every open cover of X has a finite subcover. In a compact space, every closed subset is compact. In R, closed bounded intervals are compact (Heine–Borel).Rapid recall: Compact means every open cover has a finite subcover. Closed subsets of compact spaces and continuous images of compact spaces are compact; Heine-Borel is restricted to Euclidean space.

Proof method

Given a cover of closed F⊆X, add X\F; compactness gives a finite subcover, and discard X\F to cover F.Exam workflow: Keep the quantifiers visible: choose an arbitrary cover, modify or pull it back, invoke compactness once, then translate the finite subcover back to the target.

Worked simple example

{0}∪{1/n:n≥1} is compact in R because it is closed and bounded; equivalently, use Heine–Borel.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Prove (0,1) is not compact without citing Heine-Borel. Let U_n=(1/n,1) for n>=2. These sets cover (0,1): for x>0 choose n>1/x. Any finite subfamily has a largest index N and its union is U_N=(1/N,1), which misses 1/(N+1). Hence this particular open cover has no finite subcover.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Is (0,1) compact in R? Check: no; use the cover (1/n,1).

2. Exam style: Prove a closed F⊆X is compact when X is compact. Check: add X\F to an arbitrary cover of F.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: if X is compact, Y Hausdorff and f:X->Y continuous and bijective, prove f is a homeomorphism. Check a closed F subset X is compact, f(F) is compact, hence closed in Hausdorff Y; thus f is closed and f^{-1} is continuous.

Practice question - use an open cover [8 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2024/25 Q2(b-d). Task. Prove that (0,1) is not compact using an explicit open cover, not Heine-Borel. [8 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: exhibit a cover, prove coverage, then defeat every finite subfamily. Take Un=(1/n,1), n≥2.

Worked answer: For x∈(0,1), choose n>1/x; then 1/n<x<1, so x∈Un. Hence {Un} covers (0,1). A finite subfamily has a largest index N, and since the Un are nested, its union is UN=(1/N,1), which misses 1/(N+1). Thus this open cover has no finite subcover and (0,1) is not compact.

Topic-specific trap: a cover by sets open in (0,1) need not be open in R; state the ambient space you use.

Mistakes

  • Choosing a finite cover instead of proving a finite subcover of an arbitrary cover.
  • Using Heine–Borel outside Euclidean space.
  • Forgetting closedness in the closed-subset theorem.
  • Recovery rule: A sequence argument is not automatically available in an arbitrary topological space; use open covers unless sequential compactness has been established.

Checklist

  • I can give a noncompact open-cover counterexample.
