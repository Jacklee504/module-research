Hausdorff Spaces

Separate points and use compact-to-Hausdorff rigidity.

Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction
Hausdorff: distinct x,y have disjoint open neighbourhoods. Metric spaces are Hausdorff. Infinite cofinite spaces are not Hausdorff.A continuous bijection compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism because it maps closed subsets to compact, hence closed, subsets.

Medium/hard adapted practice

(a) Is Z with cofinite topology Hausdorff? Justify. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Choose distinct m,n. Any nonempty open neighbourhoods are cofinite; their intersection is cofinite and nonempty since Z is infinite.

Mark-aware answer: No. Two nonempty cofinite sets have finite-complement union as complement of their intersection, so their intersection cannot be empty in infinite Z. Thus m,n cannot be separated.

Trap: Do not infer Hausdorffness just because singleton sets are named.

(b) Prove every metric space is Hausdorff. [6 marks]

Question demand: For x!=y choose disjoint balls with radius less than half their distance. Quickest route: For x!=y choose disjoint balls with radius less than half their distance.

Mark-aware answer: Let r=d(x,y)/3>0. B(x,r) and B(y,r) are open. If z belonged to both, triangle inequality gives d(x,y)<=d(x,z)+d(z,y)<2r=2d(x,y)/3, impossible. Thus they separate x,y.

Trap: Using radius d(x,y) can leave the balls overlapping.

(c) Use compact-Hausdorff rigidity to justify a proposed inverse is continuous. [4 marks]

Question demand: Check compact domain, Hausdorff codomain, continuous bijection. Quickest route: Check compact domain, Hausdorff codomain, continuous bijection.

Mark-aware answer: If f:X->Y is continuous and bijective, X compact and Y Hausdorff, then f is a homeomorphism. Therefore f-1 is continuous without an extra preimage calculation.

Trap: The theorem needs both compactness and Hausdorffness.
