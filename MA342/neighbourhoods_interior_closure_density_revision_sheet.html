MA342 Topic Guide

Neighbourhoods, Interior, Closure and Density

Use open sets to classify boundary behaviour.

Purpose

Define and compute interior and closure, use accumulation points correctly, and prove density via neighbourhood intersections.Exam use: In 2025/26 these ideas occur in three different parts: definitions in Q1(c), a 5-mark closedness proof in Q1(e), and a 6-mark cofinite-density example in Q2(b).

Question plan

  1. Specify the ambient topology.
  2. For interior, seek the largest open subset contained in A.
  3. For closure/density, test every open neighbourhood of a point.
  4. For an accumulation-point argument, remember to remove the point itself from A.
  5. Question interpretation: “Define” needs a topology-valid criterion; “show closed” is usually fastest by proving the complement open; “give an example with justification” needs both a proper subset and a density proof.

Definitions

N is a neighbourhood of x if it contains an open U with x∈U⊆N. The interior int(A) is the largest open subset of A. The closure cl(A) is the smallest closed subset containing A; equivalently, x∈cl(A) iff every open neighbourhood of x meets A. A point x is an accumulation point of A iff every neighbourhood of x meets A\{x}. A is dense iff cl(A)=X.Rapid recall: Closure tests N∩A; accumulation tests N∩(A\{x}); density means every nonempty open set meets A.

Proof method

Prove two inclusions for a computed closure. To show density, take an arbitrary nonempty open set and find a point of A in it. To prove A closed from the absence of accumulation points, take x∉A, choose a neighbourhood missing A\{x}, observe that it misses A because x∉A, and conclude X\A is open.Exam workflow: Name the ambient topology, write the exact neighbourhood intersection being tested, and state the final open-complement or closure conclusion.

Worked simple example

In R, int([0,1])=(0,1) and cl((0,1))=[0,1]: any interval around 0 or 1 meets (0,1).

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Let A be a subset of a topological space X with no accumulation point in X. For x∈X\A, x is not an accumulation point, so some open neighbourhood U of x satisfies U∩(A\{x})=∅. Since x∉A, this simplifies to U∩A=∅. Thus every point of X\A has an open neighbourhood contained in X\A, so X\A is open and A is closed.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Find cl({1/n:n≥1}) in R. Check: include 0.

2. Exam style [6 marks]: In the cofinite topology on Z, give a proper dense subset. Check: take 2Z; it is proper and infinite, and every nonempty cofinite-open set meets every infinite subset.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: in the subspace X=[0,1], find interior and closure of A=[0,1/2). Check int_X A=[0,1/2), since [0,epsilon)=X intersect (-1,epsilon) is open in X; cl_X A=[0,1/2]. This differs from ambient interior in R.

Practice question - no accumulation points [5 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2025/26 Q1(e). Task. Let A be a subset of a topological space X. Show that if A has no accumulation point in X, then A is closed in X. [5 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: prove X\A open point-by-point; restricting to x∉A turns a neighbourhood avoiding A\{x} into one avoiding all of A.

Worked answer: Let x∈X\A. Because A has no accumulation points, x is not an accumulation point of A. Hence an open neighbourhood U of x exists with U∩(A\{x})=∅. Since x∉A, A\{x}=A, so U∩A=∅ and U⊆X\A. Every x∈X\A therefore has an open neighbourhood contained in X\A. Hence X\A is open, and A is closed.

Topic-specific trap: the definition removes {x}; explicitly use x∉A before replacing A\{x} by A.

Mistakes

  • Treating closure as A plus only “visible” endpoints.
  • Using metric-ball intuition without checking the topology.
  • For density, checking only one neighbourhood.
  • Recovery rule: Endpoints can be interior points in a subspace; always name the ambient space before computing.

Checklist

  • I can state the neighbourhood test for closure.
  • I can distinguish closure points from accumulation points.
  • I can compute examples in R and cofinite spaces.
  • I can turn density into an open-set intersection proof.
  • Final 60-second audit: Ambient topology, neighbourhood criterion, both inclusions and endpoint behaviour are explicit.
