MA342 Topic Guide

Neighbourhoods, Interior, Closure and Density

Use open sets to classify boundary behaviour.

Purpose

Compute interior and closure, and prove density via neighbourhood intersections.Exam use: Ambient topology, neighbourhood criterion, both inclusions and endpoint behaviour are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Specify the ambient topology.
  2. For interior, seek the largest open subset contained in A.
  3. For closure/density, test every open neighbourhood of a point.
  4. Question interpretation: For interior or closure, test arbitrary neighbourhoods; for dense, prove every nonempty open set meets the subset; for boundary, calculate closure minus interior.

Definitions

N is a neighbourhood of x if it contains an open U with x∈U⊆N. int(A) is the largest open subset of A. x∈cl(A) iff every open neighbourhood of x meets A. A is dense iff cl(A)=X.Rapid recall: int A is the largest open subset of A; cl A consists of points whose every neighbourhood meets A; bd A=cl A minus int A; A is dense iff cl A=X.

Proof method

Prove two inclusions for a computed closure. To show density, take an arbitrary nonempty open set and find a point of A in it.Exam workflow: Prove both inclusions, state whether openness is ambient or subspace, and use a carefully chosen neighbourhood to exclude a point from a closure.

Worked simple example

In R, int([0,1])=(0,1) and cl((0,1))=[0,1]: any interval around 0 or 1 meets (0,1).

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

In R with the usual topology, for A=Q intersect (0,1), int A is empty because every interval contains irrationals. Its closure is [0,1]: every neighbourhood of a point in [0,1] meets Q intersect (0,1), while a point outside [0,1] has a small interval disjoint from A. Hence bd A=[0,1].

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Find cl({1/n:n≥1}) in R. Check: include 0.

2. Exam style: Prove an infinite subset A of a cofinite space X is dense. Check: every nonempty open set has finite complement and therefore meets A.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: in the subspace X=[0,1], find interior and closure of A=[0,1/2). Check int_X A=[0,1/2), since [0,epsilon)=X intersect (-1,epsilon) is open in X; cl_X A=[0,1/2]. This differs from ambient interior in R.

Mistakes

  • Treating closure as A plus only “visible” endpoints.
  • Using metric-ball intuition without checking the topology.
  • For density, checking only one neighbourhood.
  • Recovery rule: Endpoints can be interior points in a subspace; always name the ambient space before computing.
