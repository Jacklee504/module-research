Rapid refresher

Core ruleExam distinction
A separation is two nonempty disjoint open-in-X sets whose union is X. Continuous images of connected spaces are connected. Intervals in R are connected.A homeomorphism preserves connectedness; use a puncture/component invariant to disprove a proposed homeomorphism.

Medium/hard adapted practice

(a) Show a continuous surjection f:X to Y sends connected X to connected Y. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Assume Y=U union V is a separation; pull back U,V and contradict connectedness of X.

Mark-aware answer: f-1(U),f-1(V) are disjoint nonempty open sets in X whose union is X, because f is continuous and surjective. This is a separation, contradiction. So Y is connected.

Trap: Surjectivity is needed to make both preimages nonempty.

(b) Show that R minus {0} is disconnected. [6 marks]

Question demand: Give a separation in the subspace topology. Quickest route: Give a separation in the subspace topology.

Mark-aware answer: R\{0}=(-infinity,0) union (0,infinity). Both sets are nonempty, disjoint and open in the subspace (indeed open in R), and their union is all of R\{0}. Hence it is disconnected.

Trap: The sets must be open in the space being separated.

(c) Use connectedness to show R and R2 are not homeomorphic. [4 marks]

Question demand: Compare what happens after deleting a point. Quickest route: Compare what happens after deleting a point.

Mark-aware answer: R minus a point has two connected components, whereas R2 minus a point is path connected. A homeomorphism would restrict to a homeomorphism of punctured spaces and preserve connectedness, contradiction.

Trap: Do not rely solely on dimension as an unstated theorem.
