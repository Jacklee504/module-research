MA342 Topic Guide

Connectedness

Use separations and continuous images to transfer interval intuition.

Purpose

Classify spaces by constructing or ruling out separations, then transfer connectedness through continuous surjections.Exam use: The 2025/26 paper devotes 13 marks in Q3(b-c) to the definition and four topologies on Q, then 6 marks in Q4(b) to continuous images and the Intermediate Value Theorem.

Question plan

  1. Write a proposed separation as two disjoint nonempty open sets covering X.
  2. For a positive result, rule out every possible separation or use a known connected source and a continuous surjection.
  3. When several topologies occur on the same set, restart the openness test for each topology.
  4. Question interpretation: “For disconnected spaces, exhibit a separation” requires the two sets and all four checks; “brief justification” for connected cases still needs the decisive topology fact.

Definitions

X is connected if it has no separation X=U∪V with U,V disjoint nonempty open subsets. A continuous image of a connected space is connected. Intervals in R are connected.Rapid recall: A separation is X=U union V with U,V disjoint, nonempty and open in X. Continuous images of connected spaces are connected; connected subsets of R are intervals.

Proof method

If f:X→Y is continuous and onto and Y=U∪V were a separation, then f-1(U) and f-1(V) would be disjoint nonempty open sets covering X, contradicting connectedness of X. Surjectivity supplies nonemptiness and coverage.Exam workflow: For each candidate separation check open, disjoint, nonempty and covering; for a preservation theorem, use preimages and name where surjectivity is used.

Worked simple example

[0,1] is connected. Hence f([0,1]) is connected for any continuous f; for f(t)=e²πit this gives connectedness of S¹.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

On Q, connectedness depends on the topology. The discrete topology is disconnected via {0} and Q\{0}. The indiscrete topology is connected because it has no nonempty proper open set. The usual subspace topology is disconnected via Q∩(-∞,√2) and Q∩(√2,∞). The cofinite topology is connected because Q is infinite and any two nonempty cofinite-open sets intersect.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation [3 marks]: Define connectedness using separations. Check: include open, disjoint, nonempty and covering.

2. Exam style [6 marks]: If f:X→Y is continuous and onto and X connected, prove Y connected, then explain the IVT consequence. Check: pull back a separation, then use that a connected subset of R is an interval.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Let h:[a,b]→R be continuous and suppose h(a)≤y≤h(b). The interval [a,b] is connected, so h([a,b]) is connected by the continuous-image theorem. Connected subsets of R are intervals. Since h([a,b]) contains h(a) and h(b), it contains every value between them, in particular y. Thus h(c)=y for some c∈[a,b], which is the Intermediate Value Theorem.

Practice question - four topologies on Q [13 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2025/26 Q3(b-c). Task. (a) Define connectedness. [3 marks] (b) Decide whether Q is connected with each of the discrete, indiscrete, usual subspace and cofinite topologies. Exhibit a separation for every disconnected case and briefly justify every connected case. [10 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: use a singleton for the discrete case, the absence of proper opens for the indiscrete case, an irrational cut for the usual subspace case, and unavoidable intersections for the infinite cofinite case.

Worked answer: A space is connected if it admits no separation into two disjoint nonempty open sets. With the discrete topology, Q is disconnected: {0} and Q\{0} form a separation. With the indiscrete topology, Q is connected because the only open sets are ∅ and Q, so no separation exists. With the topology inherited from standard R, put U=Q∩(-∞,√2) and V=Q∩(√2,∞). These sets are relatively open, nonempty, disjoint and cover Q because √2∉Q, so they form a separation. With the cofinite topology, Q is connected: if U and V were disjoint nonempty open sets, then Q=(Q\U)∪(Q\V) would be finite, contradicting that Q is infinite.

Topic-specific trap: the rational cut must occur at an irrational number; cutting at a rational leaves that point uncovered unless the sets are changed.

Mistakes

  • Forgetting the sets in a separation must be nonempty.
  • Using images rather than preimages in the theorem proof.
  • Assuming every connected space is path connected.
  • Recovery rule: The separating sets need only be open in the subspace under discussion, not necessarily open in the ambient space.

Checklist

  • I can state a separation exactly.
  • I can use the continuous-image proof.
  • I can build a simple disconnected counterexample.
  • I can classify the discrete, indiscrete, usual and cofinite topologies on Q.
  • I can derive IVT from connected continuous images.
  • Final 60-second audit: Nonempty, disjoint, open-in-space and union conditions are all checked, or the preservation theorem's hypotheses are explicit.
