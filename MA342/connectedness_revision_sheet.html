MA342 Topic Guide

Connectedness

Use separations and continuous images to transfer interval intuition.

Purpose

Prove a space is connected, find a separation when it is not, and apply the continuous-image theorem.Exam use: Nonempty, disjoint, open-in-space and union conditions are all checked, or the preservation theorem's hypotheses are explicit.

Question plan

  1. Write a proposed separation as two disjoint nonempty open sets covering X.
  2. For positive results, use a known connected source and a continuous surjection.
  3. For homeomorphism questions, compare what point removal does to connectedness.
  4. Question interpretation: For prove connected, rule out a separation or use a continuous image; for disprove, give two nonempty disjoint open-in-the-space sets whose union is the space.

Definitions

X is connected if it has no separation X=U∪V with U,V disjoint nonempty open subsets. A continuous image of a connected space is connected. Intervals in R are connected.Rapid recall: A separation is X=U union V with U,V disjoint, nonempty and open in X. Continuous images of connected spaces are connected; connected subsets of R are intervals.

Proof method

Assume f(X)=U∪V is a separation. Then f⁻¹(U),f⁻¹(V) are a separation of X, contradicting connectedness.Exam workflow: Assume a separation, push it through a map or exploit an intermediate-value argument, and explicitly obtain the contradiction to nonemptiness/disjointness.

Worked simple example

[0,1] is connected. Hence f([0,1]) is connected for any continuous f; for f(t)=e²πit this gives connectedness of S¹.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Show X={(x,y) in R^2: xy=1} is disconnected. Let U=X intersect {x>0} and V=X intersect {x<0}. They are open in the subspace X, disjoint, nonempty ((1,1) in U and (-1,-1) in V), and U union V=X because xy=1 forbids x=0. Thus U,V form a separation.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Show {0,1} with the discrete topology is disconnected. Check: {0} and {1} separate it.

2. Exam style: If f:X→Y is continuous and onto and X connected, prove Y connected. Check: pull a separation of Y back under f.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: prove the closure of a connected set A is connected. Check if cl(A)=U union V were a separation and A met both, intersections would separate A; so A lies in one, say U. Since U is also closed in cl(A), cl(A) subset U, contradicting nonempty V.

Practice question - prove a space disconnected [8 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2024/25 Q3(b) and Q4(a). Task. Show X={(x,y)∈R2:xy=1} is disconnected. [8 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: construct a separation by the sign of x, with subspace-open sets.

Worked answer: Put U=X∩{x>0} and V=X∩{x<0}. They are open in X because the two half-planes are open in R2. They are disjoint and nonempty: (1,1)∈U and (-1,-1)∈V. Every point of X has x≠0 because xy=1, hence U∪V=X. Thus U,V are a separation and X is disconnected.

Topic-specific trap: saying 'two branches in a picture' is not a proof; establish relative openness and coverage.

Mistakes

  • Forgetting the sets in a separation must be nonempty.
  • Using images rather than preimages in the theorem proof.
  • Assuming every connected space is path connected.
  • Recovery rule: The separating sets need only be open in the subspace under discussion, not necessarily open in the ambient space.

Checklist
