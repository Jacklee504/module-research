(a) Show R and (0,infinity) are homeomorphic. [8 marks]

Question demand: apply the definition/calculation with working. Quickest route: Give f(x)=ex and f-1(y)=ln y; cite ordinary continuity.

Mark-aware answer: exp is a bijection R to (0,infinity), continuous in the subspace topology, and ln is continuous on (0,infinity). Therefore exp is a homeomorphism.

Trap: A bijective continuous map is not automatically a homeomorphism.

(b) Prove f:X->Y is continuous using the open-set definition. [6 marks]

Question demand: Take arbitrary open U in Y and prove f-1(U) open in X. Quickest route: Take arbitrary open U in Y and prove f-1(U) open in X.

Mark-aware answer: Write 'let U be open in Y'. Calculate f-1(U) and express it as an open set or union/basis element in X. Since this works for every U, f is continuous.

Trap: Checking only one convenient open set is insufficient.

(c) Explain why R and [0,1] are not homeomorphic. [4 marks]

Question demand: Use compactness as a homeomorphism invariant. Quickest route: Use compactness as a homeomorphism invariant.

Mark-aware answer: [0,1] is compact in R by Heine-Borel, while R is not compact via the cover (-n,n). A homeomorphism preserves compactness, so no homeomorphism exists.

Trap: A bijection between the sets does not preserve topology.
