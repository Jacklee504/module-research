MA342 Topic Guide

Continuity and Homeomorphism

Prove structure is preserved by maps rather than visual resemblance.

Purpose

Test continuity through preimages and rule in or out homeomorphisms using the cheapest valid invariant.Exam use: The 2025/26 Q3(d) groups three homeomorphism decisions into 9 marks: one point-removal argument, one compactness obstruction and one explicit positive construction.

Question plan

  1. For continuity, start with an arbitrary open set in the codomain.
  2. For a homeomorphism, prove bijective, continuous, and continuous inverse—or invoke a justified theorem.
  3. For each negative comparison, select one preserved property that distinguishes the pair and state both sides of the contrast.
  4. Question interpretation: “Show not homeomorphic” needs a contradiction from a topological invariant; “show homeomorphic” needs an actual map with bijectivity and continuity in both directions.

Definitions

f:X→Y is continuous iff f⁻¹(V) is open in X for every open V⊆Y. A homeomorphism is a bijective continuous map with continuous inverse; homeomorphic spaces have the same topological properties.Rapid recall: f is continuous iff f^{-1}(U) is open for every open U. A homeomorphism is a bijective continuous map with continuous inverse and preserves compactness, connectedness and separation properties.

Proof method

Calculate preimages, not images. For non-homeomorphism, assume h exists and transfer compactness, connectedness or a point-removal property to obtain a contradiction. A homeomorphism restricts to a homeomorphism after deleting corresponding points.Exam workflow: Name the invariant before using it, show the spaces differ on it, and for a positive map verify bijection, continuity and inverse continuity separately.

Worked simple example

The map f:R→R, f(x)=2x, is a homeomorphism: it is bijective and f⁻¹((a,b))=(a/2,b/2); its inverse y↦y/2 is continuous by the same argument.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

The three 2025/26 comparisons use different fastest routes. S1 is not homeomorphic to [0,1]: if h existed, delete h-1(1/2); the punctured circle is connected but [0,1]\{1/2} is disconnected. Standard R is not homeomorphic to [0,1] because the interval is compact and R is not. Finally, x↦ex is a homeomorphism R→(0,∞), with continuous inverse ln.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Why does compactness rule out a homeomorphism R→[0,1]? Check: compactness is preserved, [0,1] is compact and R is not.

2. Exam style: Show [0,1] is not homeomorphic to S¹. Check: remove an interior point of the interval and the corresponding point of the circle, then compare connectedness.

Hard paper-style synthesis

To use point removal rigorously, suppose h:S1→[0,1] is a homeomorphism and let p=h-1(1/2). The restriction h|S¹\{p} is a homeomorphism onto [0,1]\{1/2}. But S1\{p} is homeomorphic to an open interval and is connected, whereas [0,1]\{1/2} is separated by [0,1/2) and (1/2,1]. Contradiction.

Practice question - three homeomorphism decisions [9 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2025/26 Q3(d). Task. (a) Show S1 and [0,1] are not homeomorphic. (b) Show R and [0,1] are not homeomorphic. (c) Show R and (0,∞) are homeomorphic. [9 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: use point-removal connectedness for (a), compactness for (b), and the exponential/logarithm pair for (c).

Worked answer: For (a), suppose h:S1→[0,1] were a homeomorphism and put p=h-1(1/2). Then h restricts to a homeomorphism S1\{p}→[0,1]\{1/2}, impossible because the first space is connected and the second is disconnected. For (b), [0,1] is compact while R is not, and homeomorphisms preserve compactness. For (c), f:R→(0,∞), f(x)=ex, is continuous and bijective; its inverse ln:(0,∞)→R is continuous. Therefore f is a homeomorphism.

Topic-specific trap: connectedness alone does not distinguish S1 from [0,1]; the distinguishing invariant is connectedness after deleting corresponding points.

Mistakes

  • Testing images of opens instead of preimages.
  • Calling a continuous bijection a homeomorphism without an inverse argument.
  • Using an invariant that is not actually topological.
  • Recovery rule: A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism; verify inverse continuity or cite a theorem that supplies it.

Checklist

  • I can state the preimage definition exactly.
  • I can prove a simple affine homeomorphism.
  • I can use connectedness to separate non-homeomorphic spaces.
  • I can choose between compactness and point-removal arguments efficiently.
  • Final 60-second audit: Domain/codomain topologies, arbitrary-open-set preimage, bijection and inverse-continuity evidence are separated.
