MA342 Topic Guide

Continuity and Homeomorphism

Prove structure is preserved by maps rather than visual resemblance.

Purpose

Test continuity through preimages and rule in or out homeomorphisms using invariants.Exam use: Domain/codomain topologies, arbitrary-open-set preimage, bijection and inverse-continuity evidence are separated.

Question plan

  1. For continuity, start with an arbitrary open set in the codomain.
  2. For a homeomorphism, prove bijective, continuous, and continuous inverse—or invoke a justified theorem.
  3. Use compactness, connectedness or point-removal properties to disprove a proposed homeomorphism.
  4. Question interpretation: For continuity use preimages of arbitrary open sets; for homeomorphism prove bijection plus continuity in both directions; for non-homeomorphism choose a preserved invariant.

Definitions

f:X→Y is continuous iff f⁻¹(V) is open in X for every open V⊆Y. A homeomorphism is a bijective continuous map with continuous inverse; homeomorphic spaces have the same topological properties.Rapid recall: f is continuous iff f^{-1}(U) is open for every open U. A homeomorphism is a bijective continuous map with continuous inverse and preserves compactness, connectedness and separation properties.

Proof method

Calculate preimages, not images. For non-homeomorphism, assume one exists and transfer a preserved property to obtain a contradiction.Exam workflow: Name the topology on domain and codomain, compute a general preimage, and never infer inverse continuity from bijectivity alone unless a compact-to-Hausdorff theorem applies.

Worked simple example

The map f:R→R, f(x)=2x, is a homeomorphism: it is bijective and f⁻¹((a,b))=(a/2,b/2); its inverse y↦y/2 is continuous by the same argument.

Medium/hard paper-style synthesis

Let f:R->(0,infinity), f(x)=e^x. It is bijective with inverse ln. For an open V in (0,infinity), f^{-1}(V)=ln(V), open because ln is continuous; similarly the inverse is continuous. Hence f is a homeomorphism. In contrast R is not homeomorphic to S^1: removing one point disconnects R into two rays, while S^1 minus any point remains connected.

Graduated exam bridges

1. Foundation: Prove a constant map X→Y is continuous. Check: each preimage is X or ∅.

2. Exam style: Show [0,1] is not homeomorphic to S¹. Check: removing an interior point disconnects [0,1], whereas removing one point from S¹ leaves a connected space.

Hard paper-style synthesis

Paper-style synthesis: prove the identity id:(R,discrete)->(R,usual) is continuous but its inverse is not. Check preimages of usual-open sets are discrete-open; the singleton {0} is open in the discrete codomain but not in the usual domain for the reverse map.

Practice question - test a proposed homeomorphism [8 marks]

Inspired by: MA342 2024/25 Q3(c). Task. Prove f:R→(0,∞), f(x)=ex, is a homeomorphism. [8 marks]

Demand diagnosis and fastest route: establish bijection and continuity of both f and its inverse; the codomain has the subspace topology.

Worked answer: The exponential is continuous and maps R into (0,∞). It is strictly increasing, so injective; for y>0, x=ln y gives ex=y, so it is onto. Its inverse is ln:(0,∞)→R, and ln is continuous on its domain. Therefore f is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse, hence a homeomorphism.

Topic-specific trap: a continuous bijection alone is not generally a homeomorphism; inverse continuity must be justified.

Mistakes

  • Testing images of opens instead of preimages.
  • Calling a continuous bijection a homeomorphism without an inverse argument.
  • Using an invariant that is not actually topological.
  • Recovery rule: A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism; verify inverse continuity or cite a theorem that supplies it.
