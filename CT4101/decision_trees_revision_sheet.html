CT4101 Topic Guide

Decision Trees: Entropy and Information Gain

Turn uncertainty calculations into a clear split choice.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Equation: H(S)=-sum p(c)log2p(c); IG(S,A)=H(S)-sum(|Sv|/|S|)H(Sv). A split reduces label uncertainty. Question cue: calculate parent entropy, weighted child entropy, then select the largest gain and interpret.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) gain

Question demand: Compare two candidate splits. Plan/choice: retain branch-size weights. Worked answer: compute each child’s class probabilities/entropy, weight by its proportion, subtract from parent; choose higher IG. Mark-winning points: all substitutions and conclusion. Common trap: unweighted child average.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Explain why a many-valued attribute can look attractive and propose control. Plan/choice: relate gain to fragmentation, then validation/pruning. Worked answer: near-singleton branches can make training entropy low but generalise poorly; limit depth/min samples, use gain ratio where appropriate and tune by cross-validation. Mark-winning points: generalisation link. Common trap: lowest gain is best.

Purpose and relevance

A decision tree asks a sequence of feature questions to reach a class or number. Its readable structure makes it useful for explaining predictions; split calculations are a recurring exam skill.

What a question really asks — answer plan

“Choose the root” asks you to calculate parent uncertainty, calculate each candidate’s weighted child uncertainty, subtract, then select the largest gain. Show every probability and weight.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and formulas

  • Entropy H(S)=−Σ pᵢ log₂pᵢ; a pure set has H=0.
  • Weighted entropy after split A: Σ (|Sᵥ|/|S|)H(Sᵥ).
  • Information gain IG(S,A)=H(S)−weighted entropy.
  • Higher gain means the split reduces class uncertainty more.

Step-by-step method

  1. Count class totals at the parent; turn counts into p values.
  2. Compute parent entropy.
  3. For every branch of a candidate feature, compute branch entropy.
  4. Weight each branch by its share of parent examples.
  5. Subtract and choose greatest gain; repeat recursively if asked.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question: A node has 4 yes and 0 no examples. Find entropy.

Model answer: p(yes)=1 and p(no)=0. H=−1·log₂1−0·log₂0=0 (the zero term contributes 0). The node is pure, so make it a yes leaf rather than split it.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

A node has 2 yes and 2 no. Find entropy. Check: −.5log₂.5−.5log₂.5=1 bit, the maximum for two classes.

Exam bridge

Given two candidate attributes, calculate information gain and choose the root. Check: include parent entropy, each branch entropy, branch weights, both gains, and a final “choose ___ because it has larger IG” statement.

High-value traps and corrections

  • Forgetting branch-size weights.
  • Choosing lowest information gain.
  • Rounding early enough to reverse a close comparison.

Final answer checklist

  • Are probabilities correct?
  • Did I weight child entropies?
  • Did I explain the selected split?
