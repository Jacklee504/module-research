CT4101 Topic Guide

kNN and k-means

Two similar names, two different jobs: predict from labelled neighbours or form unlabelled groups.

Exam-use protocol

Before writing, identify the command word: calculate/trace requires an ordered derivation; explain requires a definition, mechanism and assumption; compare/design requires matched criteria, a justified choice and a limitation. The topic-specific recall and worked routes below are the material to adapt—not text to copy blindly.

Part-by-part recall: definitions, assumptions and mechanics

Compare: kNN is supervised prediction from labelled neighbours; k-means is unsupervised centroid clustering. Both depend on scaled distances; kNN chooses k by validation, k-means alternates assignment and mean update. Question cue: state task, metric, k selection and limitation.

Worked practice 1 — paper-style synthesis (recurring-topic pattern; not a quoted paper prompt) kNN

Question demand: Choose k for regression. Plan/choice: use CV MAE/RMSE across candidates, optionally inverse-distance weights. Worked answer: select the k with best validation error; weighting reduces influence of distant neighbours but can increase variance. Mark-winning points: validation separation. Common trap: mean label for classification.

Worked practice 2 — synthesis / design

Question demand: Cluster customers with k-means. Plan/choice: scale features, assess k/seeds and interpret. Worked answer: initialise centroids, assign nearest, recompute means to convergence; use elbow/silhouette and business profiles, repeat seeds. Mark-winning points: unsupervised/no labels. Common trap: no centroid update.

Purpose and relevance

kNN is a lazy supervised method: nearby labelled examples vote (or average). k-means is unsupervised: it repeatedly assigns points to centroids and moves centroids to the means of their assigned points.

What a question really asks — answer plan

For an algorithm question, distinguish the task first. Then give the numbered loop, calculate distances/updates carefully, and finish with one limitation such as scaling or local optima.

Prerequisites, vocabulary and formulas

  • Euclidean distance: d(x,z) = √Σ(xᵢ − zᵢ)².
  • kNN: k is neighbour count; classify by majority vote, regress by mean.
  • k-means centroid: coordinate-wise mean of a cluster.
  • Within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS): Σ‖x − centroid‖²; elbow plots it against k.

Step-by-step method

  1. Scale inputs if their units differ.
  2. kNN: calculate all query distances, select k smallest, then vote/average.
  3. k-means: choose k initial centroids, assign every point to nearest, recompute means, repeat until assignments stop changing.
  4. State that different initial centroids can give different local solutions.

Worked practice 3 — foundational calculation

Question: For query 3, labelled points are 1:A, 2:A, 6:B. Use kNN with k=2 on this line.

Model answer: Distances from 3 are 2, 1, 3. The two closest labels are A and A, so predict A. This is classification because labels are classes.

Question interpretation and independent practice

Prompt 1

Points (1,1) and (3,3) are in one k-means cluster. Find its centroid. Check: ((1+3)/2,(1+3)/2)=(2,2).

Exam bridge

Compare kNN and k-means: task, labels, training work, prediction/assignment work, k, and scaling. Check: kNN needs labels and defers work until query; k-means has no labels and iterates during fitting. In both, k and distances matter, so scale when units differ.

Integrated syllabus-based practice - kNN versus k-means [8 marks]

Scenario. Labelled points near (0,0) are red and near (10,10) blue. An unlabelled point x=(2,1) has three nearest labelled neighbours, red, red, blue. Explain its kNN label, then explain why k-means cannot directly supply that label.

Question-demand diagnosis: separate supervised prediction from unsupervised grouping.

Fastest route: vote among the k labelled neighbours, then contrast labels with centroids.

Worked answer: With k=3 and equal weights, x is red by majority vote; a distance-weighted variant reinforces nearby red evidence. k-means could place x in a cluster whose centroid is near the red region, but it has no class labels and cluster identifiers are arbitrary. Use scaled features and choose k by validation for kNN; for k-means, inspect cluster quality and interpret clusters after fitting.

Topic-specific trap: k in kNN is neighbours, while k in k-means is clusters.

  • Calling k-means classification because it produces groups.
  • Using the mean label for kNN classification.
  • Forgetting to recompute centroids after assignment.

Final answer checklist

  • Have I said supervised vs unsupervised?
  • Are distances and ties handled explicitly?
  • Did I mention scaling and a limitation?
